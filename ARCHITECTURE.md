# Architecture

Kiko runs on the Mac as the **hub**: it hears Claude Code sessions finish (through their hooks), decides what to say, listens for your words and sends them on. Every other app (the iOS companion first) is a **thin client**: one more mic, speaker and screen for the hub. The rules (routing, the reply window, "voice can", permissions) live only on the hub; a client does screens, audio and what only it can do (lock-screen push-to-talk, a Live Activity).

The entry points stay in `src/` on purpose: `log.ts` recognises the app by `src/app.ts`, and `start.ts` restarts it by that path.

`src/app.ts` keeps only what the window, its menu and the settings screen ask of the app (`onAction`), starting up (`main`), shutting down and the wiring; the rest is in `src/app/`. Its parts import each other freely (one module once, they share its state: what changes is the one `state` object in `state.ts`, since a module can't assign to another's `let`). Nothing they reach may start with a top-level await: Bun's bundle can't take one inside an import cycle, so `main()` loads the window's glue and the TUI (Ink, and Yoga under it) itself; `install-live.test.ts` builds the executable and catches it.

## Three rules
1. **Mac code never imports the iPhone app's code** (nor any other app's). The iPhone app (SwiftUI, XcodeGen, a thin client) lives in its own private repo, with a synced copy of `protocol/`.
2. **The phone server lives in `src/remote/`**, and goes through `src/core/` like the Mac's own push-to-talk.
3. **Apps talk only through `protocol/`**: one schema, a version on every message, and example messages that every side's tests check.

## Platform adapters

`src/platform/types.ts` has one small interface per Mac capability, each the shape of what the Mac code already does: **mic**, on-device **speech** to text, the built-in **voice** (`say`), the **speaker**, the push-to-talk **hotkey**, the **volume** duck, the **network** (the addresses a phone can reach, and Bonjour), and where the **keys** are kept (the Keychain). Terminals and the coding agent aren't platform pieces: they have adapters of their own (below). `src/platform/index.ts` picks the platform once, at startup; `src/core/` and the entry points use `platform` from there and never import `src/platform/macos/`. The harness swaps the Mac modules, Claude Code's hooks socket, the terminals' AppleScript and tmux for stubs (`tests/harness/loader.mjs`; its tmux is "not installed" unless a case says otherwise).

A Linux port would add `src/platform/linux/` with each of those pieces (the comment at the top of `types.ts` lists what each replaces) and a case in `index.ts`; nothing in `src/core/` changes. The keys have a piece of their own, outside `Platform` (below, "The keys").

## Agent and terminal adapters

Core talks to the coding agents and to the places sessions run only through two interfaces, each picked once in its `index.ts`, the way the platform is (core is the app's logic, so it imports the agent registry, `agents/index.ts`, at run time; and the settings screen's key choices come from `terminals/tmux/keys.ts`, plain data, rather than through `terminals/index.ts`, which would load every terminal adapter):

- **The agent** (`src/agents/types.ts`, `agent` from `src/agents/index.ts`): `list()` the running sessions you started (never headless runs or ones another Claude started: `claude-code/sessions.ts` `hiddenWhy`; each with its process's `pid` where known: tmux finds its pane by it; one continued in a background process keeps its terminal session's); `onHeld` gets each finished turn (with what it said) or permission prompt, held for you as a `Held`: `answer(reply | null)` a finished turn, `allow(always)` / `deny(why)` a prompt, `answerQuestions(answers)` a prompt that is the agent asking you `questions` (Claude Code's AskUserQuestion: the hook allows it with the tool's own input plus `answers`), `release()` either, `onClosed` when it goes away by itself, `watchTyped` for words typed into it by hand meanwhile, `saidThisTurn` for your words that reached it during the turn (sent while it was busy too), which its summary answers first; `onSessionsChanged` when the agent says one started or ended; `onSessionEnded` at once with the ID of one that ended (a Close waiting for it goes on); `onPromptSubmitted` when it says a session took a prompt in (the phone's words have started); `lastAsked` / `lastSaid` / `names` / `watchHistory` from its own record, and `historyFile`, the file `watchHistory` follows (for the Kiko window's viewer, a process of its own); `knownFolders` it has run sessions in (with the names given there), from its history; `folderTrusted`, whether it trusts a folder (Claude Code: `trust.ts` reads only `projects[<folder>].hasTrustDialogAccepted` from its `~/.claude.json`, in-process, never writes it, never logs anything else from it; as Claude Code decides it, the folder counts as trusted when it or any folder above it is, up to its git repo's root (to `/` outside one), or a worktree's main checkout is; `KIKO_CLAUDE_CONFIG` points the tests at a fixture); and what to type to manage one (`interruptKey`, `renameCommand`, `exitCommand`, and `startCommand({ prompt, name })`, the command line a new session starts with, nothing typed in), with `heldReplies`, how many replies through a hold it takes in a row. **Claude Code** (`src/agents/claude-code/`) is the only one: its global hooks over the socket (`hooks.ts`, run by `hooks/claude-code-hook.mjs`: Stop and PermissionRequest are held; SessionStart and SessionEnd are optional, told without waiting, and call `onSessionsChanged` once for several together (SessionEnd also `onSessionEnded` at once), with the look every 4 seconds kept for sessions they missed; SessionStart and Stop carry `pid`, the hook's parent (`process.ppid`: Claude Code, or a shell it ran the hook in), logged with `session_started` and `hook_stop` so a strange session ID can be traced to its process; UserPromptSubmit is optional too, told without waiting and printing nothing, and calls `onPromptSubmitted` with the prompt; `settings.ts` has the entries for `~/.claude/settings.json`), `claude agents` and the pid records (`sessions.ts`: a status in the agent's words (`statusOf`: `blocked` when a dialog waits in its window, a permission prompt say, shown as red "needs you"; `waiting` when a background job stopped on a question of its own, `claude agents`' state `blocked` with nothing open, shown as yellow "waits for you", like a reply window; `sessionStatus` in `core/ui-store.ts` maps them for the list, the bottom line and the phone; busy or idle is whether it's in a turn, `turns.ts`, since `claude agents` says busy for as long as anything of the session runs, a leftover background shell too, which once kept a job "working" for hours at its prompt: its hooks first (UserPromptSubmit opens a turn, PermissionRequest and PermissionDenied happen in one, Stop ends it whatever still runs, SessionStart has none yet, a held Stop answered with a reply carries it on); `claude agents` only for a session no hook has spoken for this run (Kiko restarted, one found late), and even then a busy one whose transcript ended its last turn is idle; and the transcript's tail (`transcripts.ts` `transcriptTurn`: a stat each listing, the last 256 KB read only when it changed) for what the hooks don't say: a turn a background task's notification starts after the Stop (no UserPromptSubmit comes), and one interrupted with Esc (no Stop comes), as does `claude agents` saying idle 15 seconds after the turn opened; a turn opening or ending gets the sessions listed again at once. What still runs in the background is apart, `AgentSession.background` (`background.ts`: `{ count, quiet?, quietSince? }`): only a background job says, in `~/.claude/jobs/<id>/state.json` (read, never written: `inFlight.tasks`, and `fan`, each task with its `startedAt`, and `doneAt` once an agent is done); a task is quiet once its output (`/tmp/claude-<uid>/<project>/<session>/tasks/<id>.output`, a subagent's a link to its transcript) hasn't been written for 10 minutes, mtimes only, never a process's command line; a terminal session has none (nothing on disk says), unless its turns moved into a job (`continued`: the job's). `core/ui-store.ts` `backgroundNote` words it for the list ("idle · 2 bg", "2 in background (quiet 8h)" in the classic look), the router's session lines and the phone's `background`; `KIKO_CLAUDE_JOBS` and `KIKO_CLAUDE_TASKS` point the tests at fixtures); a running terminal session the listing leaves out is added from its record; and a terminal session whose turns Claude Code moved into a background process (← on an empty prompt opens its agents view and parks the session there) is listed once: a background job is X's when a live terminal session X in its folder has `parkedJobId` set to the job's `id` (first: a job parked before X's first turn starts fresh, with no `--resume`; when Claude Code clears `parkedJobId` the link goes), else when its command line (`ps`, once per process) has `--fork-session --resume <…/X.jsonl>`, picked by X's `parkedJobId` when several claim it. Listed once: the process's ID, status and transcript, since its hooks come from it, with X's names, kind and window, X's ID kept in `aliases` and X's own row hidden; core looks sessions up by either ID with `findSession`, and the timeline by `idsOf`), its transcripts (`transcripts.ts`, `typed-in.ts`, and `core/history.ts` for the folded conversation), and the folders in `~/.claude/projects` read back to paths (`projects.ts`: from a `cwd` a transcript recorded, else by walking the disk, since the folder names can't be decoded on their own). Where a new session opens is `core/projects.ts`: those folders, the running sessions' and the projects root's, matched to the name said (exactly, else closely), else a fresh scratch folder. The floor's `Waiter` is the part of a `Held` it needs.
- **The terminals** (`src/terminals/types.ts`, `terminals` from `src/terminals/index.ts`): each adapter `find`s the sessions it can reach as they're listed, and `type`s into them; a terminal also takes keys (`key`, which can refuse with why, as `type` can) and opens windows (`open`, which tells its handle as soon as it has one: tmux's pane), and says what's in front in one it opened (`program`: the command still, the shell again, or gone); `ready()` says whether it can be used now. `listSessions()` (core) gives each session the `place` of the first adapter that reaches it: `{ via, id, keys, pid? }`. New sessions open in the first adapter that opens windows and is ready (`openers()`), the next only if it couldn't start anything. The adapters, in the order they're asked:
  - **tmux** (`tmux/`, first on every computer): Kiko's own server (`tmux -L kiko`, its config `tmux/tmux.conf` given with `-f`; the user's `~/.tmux.conf` and their own servers are never touched). `find`: `list-panes -a` and `ps`, a session matched to the pane whose first process is its process (the agent's `pid`) or an ancestor of it, never by title (`mapping: "process"`), and never one of the Kiko window's own panes (`@kiko-role`). `type` and `key` check it all again first (pane there with the same first process, the session's process in it, on its tty, in the foreground and not stopped; a pane in copy mode is brought out of it). A pane titled as Claude Code's agents list (`#{pane_title}`, read against `terminals/views.ts`, as Ghostty reads its window's title: a session parked with ← keeps its process in the pane) is `mapping: "agents"`, never typed into. The words go into a buffer of their own over stdin (`load-buffer -`: never on a command line, where `ps` shows them), and tmux checks and pastes in one step (`if-shell -F` on the pane's first process and the program in front, `pane_current_command`, both as checked: a voice-opened pane never in the window runs `claude; exec $SHELL`, so its first process stays the same after Claude Code quits; its title not an agent's list of sessions (`m/r` on `pane_title`); and for several lines that it still asks for bracketed paste; then `paste-buffer`, `-p` for several lines): a buffer still there after means nothing went in (Claude Code quit meanwhile, a shell in front). Enter on its own after 150ms, checked again; anything off is refused with why, said out loud (`core/reach.ts` `didntType`). A tmux command that hasn't finished after 5 seconds (a hung server) is killed and refused as "tmux didn't answer" (`tmuxRunner`; not a popup, which tmux answers only when it closes), so your words are never left waiting unsaid. `open`: a new tmux session named for the folder, the command in the user's login shell (left in it after; a shell that doesn't read quotes as sh does, fish say, gets it through `/bin/zsh`), shown in the Kiko window when it's there (below), else in a new Ghostty window running `tmux attach` (`tmux/show.ts`; Terminal.app without Ghostty). tmux not installed: it finds and opens nothing, and the others do as before. **tmux off this run** (below): it runs no tmux at all, opens nothing (`ready()` false, so Ghostty opens new sessions), refuses `type` and `key`, and `find` only marks the sessions running in Kiko's tmux as `mapping: "off"`, found by `ps` alone: their process descends from the server, whose pid comes from its socket (`serverPids`: `lsof -t` on `$TMUX_TMPDIR/tmux-<uid>/kiko`, resolved, kept until the socket is another file). `findPlaces` lets "off" stand as it does "ambiguous" (a Ghostty window showing that tmux would type through it), and `cantReach` says so ("pixie is in Kiko's tmux, and tmux is off this time…"). `bin/kiko` (`src/cli.ts`) is how the user starts a session there by hand: `kiko claude [args…]`. `screen` and `press` are for a pane it opened whose program hasn't started a session yet (Claude Code's trust question, below): `capture-pane -p` of that pane, and one key sent only while its program (not the shell) is in front, tmux checking the pane's first process and program as it sends. Not yet: reading a session's screen (the live session view), typing into the user's own tmux, Linux (later work).
  - **Rex** (`rex/`, after Agent messages and before Ghostty; 2026-11-05): Rex (superlogical.com/rex) through its CLI, `rex`, always `--autostart=false`, without REX_SESSION/REX_BLOCK in its environment, and only while a session may be in Rex (`rexNeeded`: its hook said `rex`, or its terminal isn't known, while Rex's server socket is there: a stat, no process), so someone who doesn't use Rex never has `rex` started. Every look and send is one `rex do` (`rex/rex.ts`: a Lua script of ours, its arguments over stdin, never on a command line), on the local server's terminal blocks only (its `host_id`). `find`: one script lists every block with its `process` (front pid, executable name, folder, the pids under it: never argv0 or anything of a command line) and title (~20 ms for 7 blocks); a session is matched by its process, never by title: the block its hook said (REX_BLOCK, `hooks/lib.mjs` `terminalEnv`, kept per session in `agents/claude-code/terminals.ts` as `AgentSession.pane`, so `Findable.pane`) while its process is in it, else the one block whose front process or one under it is the session's (`mapping: "process"`; two sessions in one block, or its process in two: `ambiguous`); a session whose process isn't known (Codex) by its program in front and folder, as tmux's `byProgram`. A block titled as an agents list: `agents`. `type` and `key` look at the block again first (the session's process in front, not one it started, not a shell; title), and the send itself goes in only if Rex finds the same process in front (and, for a bracketed paste, still asking for one) in the same script. One line is typed (`write`); several lines, or one for a session that wants it (Codex) while its program asks, are Rex's paste event, which brackets them; without bracketed paste, line breaks become spaces. Enter on its own after 150ms, checked again. Keys are Rex's key events (Escape, Enter, Tab, Shift+Tab, arrows). `open`: a new tab (`rex run`) in the session Kiko was started from (its REX_SESSION), else the first; in the login shell (`-l -i -c "<line>; exec $SHELL -l"`), its block the handle; `program`, `screen` (the `format` method's last screen rows), `press` and `placeIn` on it as tmux's on a pane. Not done: an event stream (`rex events`) instead of a listing per find, and showing a new session in Rex's app when Kiko wasn't started from one of its sessions (a client action, behind Rex's Remote Control setting).
  - **iTerm2 and Terminal.app** (`iterm/`, `terminal-app/`, both on `tty/`; after Rex and before Ghostty; 2026-11-05): over AppleScript run by the helper app, each script given its arguments as argv. Both apps say each session's (tab's) `tty`, and a session's process says its own (`ps -o pid=,tty=`: never its arguments), so a session is matched tty to tty (`mapping: "process"`); two sessions on one tty: `ambiguous`. A session whose process isn't known (Codex) by its program in front on a tab's tty (`ps -t`: the foreground group's leader, by executable name) and that process's folder (`lsof -a -d cwd`), as tmux's `byProgram`: the tab it was opened in, else the only one in its folder (`only`); no folder, no match. iTerm's session name titled as an agents list: `agents` (Terminal says no titles). Listed only while a session may be in that app (`ttyNeeded`: its hook said `iterm`/`terminal`, or its terminal isn't known) and the app runs (`pgrep -xq iTerm2`/`Terminal`, every 5 s at most); every script but a new window's also checks `application … is running` before it tells the app anything. Before every word and key, the session's process is looked at again (`ps`: running, on that tty, in front there, not stopped); the send's own script finds the session again by its id and tty (Terminal: its tab by tty) and writes only then. Nothing is selected, activated or brought forward except by `open` (a new window: `cd <folder> && <command>` typed into the default shell). iTerm: `write text … newline false` (bytes as typed, to that session only), one line as is, several (or Codex's one) as one bracketed paste (ESC[200~…ESC[201~, lines split by Return), Enter (`\r`) on its own after 150 ms, checked again; its send script refuses a session whose name contains "claude agents" (`views.ts` `VIEW_PHRASES`); keys are their bytes (Escape is one ESC, arrows CSI A–D, Shift+Tab CSI Z); `screen` is the session's `contents`; `press`, `placeIn`, `program` on a window it opened. Terminal: `do script <text> in <tab>`, which always adds its own Return: the words go in as one bracketed paste every time, so that Return is Enter (sends them); no keys (no way to send one without a Return after it; System Events would need focus and Accessibility), so Interrupt and Rename aren't offered for a Terminal session (`core/can.ts`), unless its agent does them (Codex); `screen` is the tab's `contents`. iTerm and Terminal open new sessions only after every other adapter (`openers`: Ghostty stays the default). iTerm's Broadcast Input (when you turn it on for a window) would send what's written to every session in it: Kiko can't see that setting.
  - **Ghostty** (`ghostty/`: matched by folder and title over AppleScript; Mac-only), the fallback for sessions started in it, outside Kiko's tmux, and where new sessions open if tmux isn't installed. Its AppleScript gives a terminal only its id, title and working directory (no tty or pid). A window on Claude Code's agents list (titled "claude agents": a session parked with ←; the titles in `terminals/views.ts`, which tmux reads too) is never typed into, since its prompt starts a new session: `mapping: "agents"`, said as such (`core/reach.ts`). `typeInto` lists the sessions once more, fresh, before giving up on an `ambiguous` or `agents` one (`type_relisted`).
  - **T3 Code** (`t3code/`, asked first: only T3's own threads, by their agent, so no other adapter guesses a window in the same folder for one): `type` sends `orchestration.dispatchCommand` `message.dispatch` through the T3 agent's connection; no keys, no windows.
  - **Agent messages** (`agent/`, after tmux and before Ghostty): a session whose agent takes your words itself (`Agent.message`, for one whose `Agent.can` says `reply`: OpenCode's, through its service) wherever it runs, as a new message, its place `{ via: "agent", id: "<agent>:<session>", keys: false }`. One in Kiko's tmux is tmux's (typed into its pane, shown in the window); nothing is guessed by title in Ghostty for one that can take a message. No keys: Interrupt and Rename go through the agent too (`core/can.ts byAgent`).

  **No adapter for the Claude desktop app.** Its only way in was its UI: the words on the clipboard, Cmd+V and Return. Kiko never touches the clipboard, so that's gone (2026-10-29). A third-person question about a named session as the first sentence ("Is Harbor still deploying?", "What's the status for MintBox?", "What did MintBox say the issue was?", "Has Atlas finished?"), or any question naming one with the app's name up front ("Kiko, does MintBox know about the cert?"; not "can"/"could", which ask for work), skips the classifier, which sent them to the session, and goes to the router with those sessions in `<asks_you_about>` and their rule (added to the instructions only then). It answers with `say` from the list, or says it doesn't know and ends "Want me to ask Harbor?" (`asksWhere` keeps the offer, so a yes sends your words there). A `pass_on` or `ask_session` it makes anyway becomes `offer_session` with "Want me to ask Harbor?" (guard `asked_about`). Questions said to the session ("Bobby, are you …?") were taken up front, before this, as were replies to the one waiting.

**Never silent.** Your words always end somewhere you hear about: the router returns the calls it dropped (a handle not in `<sessions>`, `Dropped`, with the handle it was given); `decide()` says what it couldn't find ("I don't see a session called dozer.", `unknown_session`), or puts the call back for a session still starting named by its folder or name; and after `decide()`, words with no action, nothing said and no question asked are logged as `nothing_done`, said ("I didn't do anything with that: …") and fail the phone's talk (`decideAndAct`). Every send decided already ended sent, held, or said (`notSent`). Words a terminal couldn't take are said every time (`type_failed` and `send_failed` log the line, `said`); for an `ambiguous` or `agents` session the line ends "Say try again to send it.", and "try again" within 2 minutes sends the same words there (`decide.ts` step `retry`). An announcement waits for the app's lines ahead of it to be heard (`AudioQueue.clearAhead`), so such a line isn't run into, and only its own sound makes it heard (`floor.playing(own)`).

**The router's time limit** (`core/hedge.ts`, `ROUTE_TIMING` in `core/router.ts`). The router's request is between your words and where they go. Not answered in 4s, a second request starts beside it; the first answer is taken and the other cut off, so the decision is made once; each has 8s, and the SDK's own retries are off for it. A fast failure worth retrying starts the second at once. When neither answers, `route()` throws and decide.ts falls back (below; a new press still cancels it: null, "you pressed again"). Every Haiku client comes from `core/anthropic.ts`, which notes the SDK's own retries per response for the logs (`requestInfo`). The numbers come from measured router latency (2026-10-30).

**When the router can't answer** (2026-11-12; `core/kept.ts`, `core/online.ts`, `core/fallback.ts`, `decide.ts` `routeFailed` and `keptFor`, `deliver.ts` `retryKept`). The Mac's internet dropped for two minutes and two messages for Rowan were lost with "Something went wrong". Now a router with no answer (timeouts, a network error, no key) keeps the words (`KeptWords`, in memory, shown on Kiko's page) and says so once: "I'm offline. I'll send that when I'm back." (or "I couldn't work out where that goes. Trying again."). No session is guessed (the user, 2026-11-12: a guess will often be wrong); only the router or a name sends them. They're routed again by themselves, oldest first and each once (taken off the list before it's decided), through the normal `decide()`: after 2s, 5s, 10s, 20s, then every 30s, and at once when the network is back (`online.onBack`); a send that goes says "Sent to Rowan." and isn't held. One routed more than 2 minutes after it was said is asked about first ("Still send "Nice, this one reads much better…" to Rowan?", a `Question` with `kept`: a yes sends it). New words that go to a session while some wait, with the router answering again, send the waiting ones first (`flushKept`; a late one is then held, not asked about). After 8 tries or 3 minutes the retries stop: "I couldn't send that. Say send it to try again."; then, until 10 minutes after they were said, "send it" routes them again, and "send that to Rowan" or the name alone ("Rowan.") sends them there at once, offline or not; a bare "send it" or name while a session waits for your reply is its reply. Words that were the app's (a question about a session, the dictionary, a correction, a short answer to its question) are only said not done. `online` takes the network as down on a network error (the relay's "getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND", a model fetch's ENOTFOUND/ENETUNREACH…), a model timeout while the relay is down, or the router timing out twice in a row, for 90s after the latest sign unless something gets through; while down, the classifier, the clean-up, the trim and the router aren't asked (`DecideDeps.offline`) and a retry due counts as a try without waiting. Back online with words the retries gave up on: said once ("I'm back online. Your last message is still waiting: …"). Any other failure of `deliver()` (talk.ts `act`) keeps the words too, without retries. The phone's talk fails with the same line. Logged: `route_fallback`, `words_kept`, `kept_retry`, `kept_held`, `kept_gave_up`, `kept_taken`, `kept_expired`, `online_back`.

## The safety gate (2026-11-02)

Turned on, Kiko asks before anything that sends, deletes, pays or posts, whatever the session's own permission mode or allow rules: a session in bypass permissions, one with "always allow git push", a Codex session with a loose sandbox. Until then Kiko only saw the prompts an agent chose to show. **Off by default** (since 2026-11-02, the day it was built: off keeps what was there before, and you can turn it on for more control): Settings → Permissions turns it on.

- **Off, the hooks pass first thing** (`hooks/lib.mjs` `guardSaved`): before loading the rules or reaching the app, a gate event reads its flavour's settings file (`KIKO_SETTINGS`, else `~/.config/<flavour>/settings.json`, as `core/settings.ts` `SETTINGS_FILE`) and exits 0 with nothing printed unless the gate is on there (`guardOn`: `guard` saved true; or, not saved, `guardOff` non-empty: kinds turned off by hand while it was on by default, so that gate stays on). No file: off. A file that isn't JSON (caught mid-write): on, and the app, whose `config.guard` is the same `guardOn`, decides. The read is about 15 µs, and skipping the rules' import saves about 1.5 ms, so the off path costs the hook's start and nothing more (about 24 ms, the same as with no app running). The setting isn't sent to the hooks or kept anywhere else: Settings saves `guard` (true or false, always written once chosen) and the next tool call reads it.

- **The rules** (`hooks/guard.mjs`, with the parser in `hooks/guard/parse.mjs` and the rules in `hooks/guard/rules-*.mjs`: `remote` (git, gh, HTTP clients, mail, scp), `local` (files, disks, databases), `deploy` (hosting and cloud CLIs, registries), `scripts` (risky names, inline code), `mcp`; no imports but `node:fs`/`node:path` and each other, run inside the hooks, kept to a few files since each costs the hook's start about 0.15 ms; `hooks/guard.d.mts` types it for src): `guardCheck(tool, input, cwd)` → `{ hits, unclear }` or null. Shell tools (Claude Code's `Bash` and `Monitor`, Codex's `Bash`, a string or an argv) are parsed (`parseShell`: `&& || ; | &` and newlines, quotes as single words, `$(…)`, backticks, `<(…)` and subshells as commands of their own, redirections and their files dropped, comments skipped, heredoc text kept on its command and run only when fed to a shell, `if/while/do` words skipped), then `unwrap` takes off what only runs what follows (`VAR=x`, `sudo`, `env`, `command`, `nohup`, `time`, `timeout`, `xargs`, `npx`/`pnpm dlx`/`pnpm exec`/`bunx`/`uvx`/`uv run`, `op run --`, …), and `bash -c`, `eval` and `ssh host cmd` are followed. A rule per CLI (`RULES`): git (push, `--force`, `--delete`/`:ref`, `branch -D`, `clean -f`, `send-email`; `push --dry-run` passes), gh (by its first two words; `gh api` by method and fields, GraphQL mutations), rm -r (not for build output or caches inside the project, `safeToRemove`, nor the temp folders), find `-delete`, curl/wget/HTTPie (POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE or data to a host that isn't loopback; payment hosts are pays), wrangler, vercel, netlify, fly, firebase, railway, terraform/pulumi/cdk/serverless/helm/kubectl, aws/gcloud/az/doctl, docker push and volume deletes, every package registry's publish, Stripe's CLI writes, SQL drops and unqualified `DELETE FROM` in psql/mysql/sqlite and friends (heredocs included), mail tools, osascript sends, scp/rsync to another host. **Unclear** (`hint`: the kind their words suggest): scripts, package scripts and make/just/task targets named with a risky word (`./deploy.sh`, `pnpm run release`), an unknown CLI whose first words are a risky verb, inline code that posts or charges (`python -c "requests.post(…)"`), AWS writes. Everything else is nothing: reads, edits, builds, tests, and ordinary commands whose arguments only mention those words (`echo "git push"`, `grep deploy`). **Not blinded by syntax** (2026-11-19's security review): `$'…'` (its `\'` inside) and `$"…"` are read as quotes, zsh's `=(…)` as a command; what the parser can't read for sure (`rough`: a `(` inside a word, as zsh's glob qualifiers that run code, `*(e:'cmd':)`; a quote, bracket or backtick never closed) is unclear, never nothing. Unclear too: a command named by an expansion (`$g push`, `git $(echo push)`), xargs feeding git, gh, a shell or a CLI that reaches another machine, `git -c alias.*=…`, inline code that shells out (`os.system`, `child_process`, perl's `system`; its strings are read as commands, so `perl -e 'system("git push")'` is a hit), `curl -K`. An MCP tool that runs a shell command (`…__run_shell {cmd}`) gets that command's verdict too. It's a backstop, best effort, not the main control. **MCP tools by name** (`checkMcp`: `mcp__<server>__<tool>`, split into words): a read verb first is nothing; pay/buy/refund/charge or a domain registration is pays; delete/remove/destroy deletes; send/email/reply/notify, or a message or comment being posted, sends; deploy/publish/release/merge/push/DNS records/a pull request created posts; any other write verb (create, update, execute, …) is unclear, except on local servers (filesystem, memory, playwright, Claude Docs, …). Tool descriptions don't reach a PreToolUse hook, so they aren't used. About 10 µs a command.
- **Claude Code** (`hooks/claude-code-hook.mjs`, PreToolUse, registered with matcher `Bash|Monitor|mcp__.*` and timeout 15: reads and edits never start it; `agents/claude-code/settings.ts` `HOOK_MATCHERS`; the start's upgrade adds it to a connected file). No socket of its own flavour's: exit 0 at once (before reading the event). The gate off in Settings: exit 0 (above). Not flagged: exit 0 without reaching the app. Flagged: the event with the rules' findings goes to the app (`converse` in `hooks/lib.mjs`), which answers one line, `{"guard":{"ask":true,"reason":…}}` or `{"guard":{"ask":false}}`; an ask is printed as `permissionDecision: "ask"` with the reason ("Kiko asks first: this posts (push to GitHub)."). A connection that closes without a verdict (an app from before the gate) passes; one taken but not answered within 6 s (`KIKO_GUARD_WAIT_MS`) asks all the same. The gate's own code failing (its rules throw, or don't load) asks too ("Kiko asks first: its safety check failed on this call.", the error on stderr), never a silent pass; Codex's hook, which can't ask, denies instead. Claude Code then asks, in every mode and past any allow rule (checked on 2.1.316, see below), and its prompt comes back as an ordinary PermissionRequest: a card, by voice and on the phone. Headless runs (`sdk-*` entrypoints) are left alone, as for every event; a session another Claude started (`hiddenSession`) passes (`guard_pass`, nobody here could answer it).
  - **Checked against the real Claude Code** (2.1.316, 2026-11-02, a throwaway session in a temp folder, its user settings left out, `--settings` with a PreToolUse hook answering "ask" and a PermissionRequest hook that logged and denied): under `--dangerously-skip-permissions` the PermissionRequest hook ran with `permission_mode: "bypassPermissions"` and its deny was honoured ("Denied by PermissionRequest hook"); with an allow rule (`--allowedTools "Bash(echo *)"`, default mode) the same; with the PermissionRequest hook allowing, the command ran. Not `deny`: that would make Kiko's own flow the only way through and lose Claude Code's own prompt when Kiko lets go.
- **Codex** (`hooks/codex-hook.mjs`, its PreToolUse entry widened to `request_user_input|Bash|mcp__.*`, timeout 300). T3 Code's Codex threads stay T3's (`otherApp`).
- **OpenCode, T3 Code**: not gated (SUPPORT.md, "Asks before it sends, deletes, pays or posts").
- **The verdict** (`core/guard.ts` `decideGuard`, the app's part in `app/guard.ts` `guardFor`, set as `agent.onGuard`; an adapter's default is the rules alone): off in Settings, or every kind found turned off: pass. - **Timing** (`tests/unit/agents/guard-hook.test.ts`, this Mac): the hook's start is the cost: about 23 ms with no app, 24 ms for `git push` with the gate off (the default), 25 ms for an ordinary command with it on and the app running (the rules about 10 µs), 25 ms for a flagged one including the round trip; reads, edits and searches never start it. ## Read-only calls allowed without asking (2026-11-12)

A permission prompt whose call only reads is allowed by Kiko at once, this once, with nothing said, no card and no phone question. Why: one question about a release brought six spoken prompts in a minute (acceptEdits), every one a read that Claude Code asked for only because it was chained, used `$(…)`, left the project folder or ran curl. **On by default**; Settings → Permissions → "Allow read-only commands" (`autoAllowReads`, next to the safety gate) turns it off.

- **Where:** the app, first thing in `app/announce.ts` `onRequest` (before Permissions by voice: a read isn't asked at all), not the hook: one place for both agents, the setting in hand, hidden sessions and quick checks keep their own handling, and the round trip is the local socket the prompt reaches anyway (no model is called). `core/auto-allow.ts` `readOnlyAllowed` decides which prompts qualify: only Claude Code's and Codex's (`Held.agent`, set by `HookHeld` and `CodexHeld`; OpenCode's and T3 Code's prompts and Kiko's own cards ask as before), never one the safety gate asked (`guardOf`), a quick check's, Claude's questions, or a tool other than Bash, Read, Grep, Glob. Allowed with `allow(false)`: never `updatedPermissions`, nothing written to the agent's settings.
- **The rules** (`hooks/guard/auto-allow.mjs`, in code; since 2026-11-19 they **fail closed**: a security review found the old parser could be fooled into allowing anything, with `$'…'`, zsh's `=(…)` and glob qualifiers, `sed -e'w …'`, file names a glob turns into flags, and git run on a repo's planted config). Only what is surely a read is allowed; anything unusual asks as an ordinary prompt.
  - **Its own lexer** (`lex`), not the gate's parser: plain words, `'…'` and `"…"` (no `$`, backtick or `\`-escape in them), and `;` `&&` `||` `|` and newlines between commands. Anything either shell (bash or zsh, the user's) gives a meaning to asks: every `$` (`$'…'`, `$"…"`, `$(…)`, `$VAR`, arithmetic), backticks, `(` `)` and process substitution, every redirection but one (below: so `>/dev/null`, `&>`, `1>`, `2>file`, `2>>`, `2>&1 >file` ask), heredocs, `[` `]` globs and braces, `#`, `!`, `^`, a backslash outside double quotes (so backslash-newline too), a `~` that isn't a path's start (`HEAD~3` is fine), a lone `&`, zsh's `=cmd`, `'a''b'` (zsh's RC_QUOTES reads it differently), unclosed quotes, control characters, non-ASCII outside quotes.

And `*` and `?` as ls, cat, head and wc's files only (`GLOB_READERS`: in none of them, BSD's or GNU's, does a flag write or run anything; not file, whose -C writes, nor rg/grep/find/git, where a file named `--pre=sh` runs code), only in a glob's last part, no `**`, no quotes in it (`globProblem`): Kiko matches it itself against the folder's names (hidden ones, any case, and any `?` as any run of characters: more than either shell picks), and every match must be a path allowed on its own (no link, no secret) whose name doesn't start with `-`; one starting with `.` that could pick `.` or `..` asks (bash 3.2's `.?` and `.*` do). A glob anywhere else (a flag, a flag's value, another command's word) asks.

## The keys
Kiko's own calls use four API keys (`src/core/keys.ts` KEYS): `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`, `CARTESIA_API_KEY`. They're kept in the macOS Keychain; the environment and `.env` still work, for development.

- **Precedence:** the environment, then `.env`, then the Keychain. `config.ts` notes which keys the environment had, loads `.env` (which never overrides), then `fillKeys` reads only the missing ones from the Keychain. The keys Kiko finds itself (the Keychain's, `.env`'s, one entered in setup or settings) are held in memory (`src/core/key-values.ts` `heldKeys`), never in `process.env`; every call site reads its key with `keyOf` (or `config.*Key`), yours from the environment first. So no program Kiko starts inherits one: before 2026-11-02 they were put in `process.env`, and the app `kiko setup` opened got setup's Keychain keys in its environment (its `keys` log said "environment"), as did each helper, Pocket and settings screen the app ran. A key your own shell exports stays in `process.env` (yours). The app's launch also takes out any held key (`cli.ts` appLaunch, `withoutHeldKeys`). `config.keys.sources` says where each came from (`environment`, `.env`, `Keychain`), for settings → Keys and the start log (`keys`: names and sources, never a key). Keys in `process.env` reach no child they shouldn't, as `.env`'s never did: tmux gets `cleanEnv()`, quick tasks strip them (`quick-task.ts` STRIP).
- **The store** (`src/platform/types.ts` KeyStore) is a piece of its own, picked by `src/platform/keys.ts` rather than `index.ts`: `config.ts` reads it while it loads, and `index.ts`'s pieces import `config`. `KIKO_KEYCHAIN=off` turns it off; a path uses that keychain file instead of the login keychain (tests only). Under the test runner (`NODE_TEST_CONTEXT`) it's off unless a file is given, and `tests/setup.ts` and the harness set it off: no test touches the login keychain.
- **The helper** (`native/macos/keychain.swift`, run by `src/platform/macos/keychain.ts`): generic-password items, service `sh.kiko.keys`, account the key's name. `get <names…>` (a JSON object on stdout), `set <name>` (the key on stdin), `delete <name>`, `list` (names only: reading attributes needs no access, so it never prompts). A key only goes in on stdin and out on stdout to the app's pipe: never in argv or a child's environment, never on stderr, never logged. `--keychain <file>` (searched with `kSecMatchSearchList`, added to with `kSecUseKeychain`) is how the test of the real helper uses a throwaway keychain from `security create-keychain` (`tests/unit/platform/macos/keychain.test.ts`).
- **Entering one** (settings → Keys, `kiko keys set`): checked with its provider's cheapest call first (`checkKey`: OpenRouter `GET /api/v1/key`, Anthropic `GET /v1/models?limit=1`, Deepgram `GET /v1/projects`, Cartesia `GET /voices?limit=1`; 8 s), then saved. Turned down (401, or 403 but for Deepgram, whose narrow-scope keys can't list projects): not saved, with the provider's reason (the key taken out of it if it's repeated back). Offline, slow or failing: asked whether to save it anyway; in the settings screen the key is held in memory only, until you answer or settings close. The settings screen's typing shows dots, and hands the key to the app in its own action (`apiKey`), never through `set` (which logs its value). A key saved that no environment or `.env` key shadows is used at once where it can be (OpenRouter's is read per call; the classifier comes on); one an engine started with (Anthropic for Haiku, Deepgram, Cartesia) restarts the app when settings close.
- **Which keys are asked for** (`keysShown`): OpenRouter first, Anthropic always (optional), Deepgram and Cartesia only with that engine chosen or a key for it already set. The voice engine, speech recognition and model options mark the ones whose key is missing (`settings-options.ts` `withKeys`, `needsKey`); picking one opens the key entry in place (`ui/settings.ts`), and the `apiKey` action carries the option (`then`), set by `app.ts` (`onAction`) only once `KeySettings.enter` says the key was saved.
- **What runs without a key** (`keys.ts` `pickEngines`, `config.ts` `enginesFor`): no key is required to start but a model key. A choice whose key is missing gets a stand-in, said once at start and logged (`key_fallback`): Cartesia → Pocket (the macOS voice when Pocket's venv, `~/.local/share/kiko-dev/pocket-venv` for a checkout, isn't there), Deepgram → Apple, Haiku on Anthropic → Haiku through OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_HAIKU`), an OpenRouter model → Haiku on Anthropic. With nothing chosen, the paid engine whose key is there, else the one-key setup (Pocket, Apple, Haiku through OpenRouter), with nothing said. Neither model key: the app stops (`no_model_key`, `NO_MODEL_KEY`), after `config.ts` loads, so `kiko setup` and `kiko keys` still run. Apple speech that can't start is said (`stt_connect`) instead of stopping the app.

**Access control, and its trade-off.** An item the helper adds trusts the helper alone: on the file-based login keychain its access list names the creating program by its code signature's designated requirement. Another program asking for the key gets macOS's "wants to use your confidential information" prompt instead of the key. The catch is what "the same program" means after a rebuild:
- **Signed with your developer identity** (what `native.ts` does when `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` finds a Developer ID Application or Apple Development identity; `KIKO_SIGN_IDENTITY` overrides, `-` for ad hoc): the requirement is the identifier `sh.kiko.keychain` plus your team's certificate, so a rebuilt helper (a new `keychain.swift`) still matches and nothing is asked. Signing may itself ask once to use the identity's private key (Always Allow).
- **Ad hoc** (no identity on this Mac, or signing refused): the requirement is the binary's own hash, so each rebuild is a new program to the Keychain, and the first read after it asks once (Always Allow adds the new one). Rebuilds happen only when `keychain.swift` changes. Each checkout builds its own helper (`native/macos/.build/`), so a worktree's helper is another program too.
- In a checkout, of the bare helpers only the keychain helper is re-signed (the others are only run directly when the helper app can't be: below, "The helper app"). The helper app is signed the same way, with your identity, so its permissions survive a rebuild. A release signs every one (below).
- Not used: the data-protection keychain (`kSecUseDataProtectionKeychain`), which binds items to an access group instead of a signature but needs an entitlement and a provisioning profile, so a signed app bundle.
- **A release** (since 2026-11-01, RELEASING.md "Signing and notarization") signs the helper with the company's Developer ID identity as `sh.kiko.keychain` (`src/core/name.ts` `IDENTIFIER`, the one place the `sh.kiko` prefix is kept), so every later release is the same program to the Keychain and nobody is asked after an update. Keys saved by an ad-hoc-signed helper (an early build) trust that binary alone: the first Developer ID one to read them gets the prompt once (Always Allow).
- A prompt waits for you: the start read (`config.ts`, synchronous) waits up to 60 s, then starts without the Keychain's keys and says why (`keys` log line with `error`; an engine whose key is then missing gets its stand-in, and no model key at all stops the start, saying `kiko keys set openrouter`).

## The helper app (2026-11-02)
macOS asks for a permission on behalf of the "responsible process": the app the asking process runs in. Before this, the helpers were `kiko`'s children, so every permission was the terminal's ("Ghostty would like to access the microphone"), every program in that terminal got it too, and another terminal had to be granted it all again. Now the helpers that need one run in an app of Kiko's own, so macOS's prompts and System Settings say its name and the grants stay whichever terminal `kiko` runs in (a prototype proved it first, since deleted).
- **What it is:** `<NAME>.app` for the release, `<NAME> Dev.app` for a checkout (`name.ts` `flavourNames`: `helperApp`, `helperAppId` = `sh.kiko.helper` / `sh.kiko.dev.helper`), so the two never share a grant; a background-only app (`LSUIElement`: no Dock icon, no menu bar, no window). Its executable (`native/macos/app/host.swift`) is named after the app, since System Settings' Input Monitoring row shows the executable's name (the prototype's said "kiko-proto"). In `Contents/Helpers/`: `mic`, `hotkey`, `speech`, `permissions`, unchanged (`helper-app.ts` `BROKERED`). The rest (`player`, `opus`, `presence`, `keychain`) stay `kiko`'s children: they need no permission, and the Keychain trusts the keychain helper by its own signing identifier, so it must not move. `Info.plist` has the microphone and speech recognition reasons macOS shows (Input Monitoring has no reason key: setup says why first), `BrokerBuild` (what `kiko` compares, below) and `BrokerCommand` (the command it tells you to run when it's double-clicked: it works from the terminal). Icon: the site's "Kiko." mark, the name's first letter (pale) and the periwinkle square dot on Kiko's dark ground, on macOS's icon plate, the same for both flavours (`native/macos/app/icon.swift`; the iPhone app's and the site's icons match).
- **Built by** `scripts/build-helper-app.sh` (names from `name.ts`, never written in it): every Swift file compiled at once (~3 s), the icon drawn, then signed inside out with the hardened runtime: each helper as `<bundle id>.<helper>` (`mic` and `permissions` with `com.apple.security.device.audio-input`), then the bundle (audio-input and apple-events: `scripts/signing/helper-app.entitlements`). A checkout builds it into `native/macos/.build/<NAME> Dev.app` on first use and again only when its sources change (`helper-app.ts` `buildApp`: a hash of the Swift files, the script, the entitlements, its names and the identity, kept as `BrokerBuild`), signed with your developer identity when there is one (`native.ts` `signer`, as the keychain helper): macOS keeps a grant by the app's designated requirement, which is then its identifier and team, the same after every rebuild. Ad hoc (no identity), the requirement is the binary's hash, and every rebuild is asked again.
- **A spawn broker:** `kiko` starts it with `open -g -j <bundle>` (Launch Services, in the background, hidden: started that way it's its own responsible process, and so is each child it starts; run directly by a shell it would be the terminal's), and talks to it on a Unix socket, `~/Library/Caches/<bundle id>/broker.sock` (its folder 0700, the socket 0600; the home from the user database on both sides, not `$HOME`). One connection per client is its control channel, one JSON object per line: `hello` (its pid, bundle path, build, version, whether it's its own responsible process, how many clients), `spawn` (a token, the helper, its arguments, which of stdin/stdout/stderr it gets), `kill`, `quit`, `relaunch`; it answers `spawned` (the pid) or `error`, and `exit` (code or signal) when a child ends. Each stdio stream is a connection of its own whose first line names its token and fd (read a byte at a time, so nothing after it is taken); the app `posix_spawn`s the helper with those sockets as its fds (`POSIX_SPAWN_CLOEXEC_DEFAULT`, signals as default) and closes its own copies, so the helper reads and writes `kiko`'s sockets directly, as it did its pipes: nothing is copied in between, and the helpers didn't change. A client gone (quit, crashed): its children are stopped (SIGTERM, SIGKILL after 2 s). No client and no child for 30 s (enough for `kiko` restarting, or starting its helpers again after macOS's "Quit & Reopen", to find it; a quit `kiko` leaves nothing behind for long), or its socket no longer its own (nobody can reach it): it quits. **One instance** serves a socket: the one holding `<socket>.lock` (`flock`, let go as it quits or is killed); another started meanwhile (macOS's Reopen beside `kiko`'s own restart of it, an `open -n` racing) waits up to 3 s for the lock, then quits quietly, before it could come to the front. **Brought to the front** (double-clicked, or macOS's "Reopen" after its "Quit & Reopen": both with no arguments; `open -g -j` never brings it): an alert says it works from the terminal and which command runs it, but only when no `kiko` of its flavour is about: no client connected, no helper of its running, nothing answering on `kiko`'s hooks socket (`Info.plist` `BrokerAppSocket`, `/tmp/<slug>.sock`) and the last client's pid (`client.pid` beside the socket, written by `kiko` as it connects) gone, after up to 3 s for one to connect. Otherwise it stays quiet (counted as `quiet` in its hello). These decisions are pure functions between `decide:begin` and `decide:end` in `host.swift`, compiled on their own by `tests/unit/platform/macos/helper-app-host.test.ts`.
- **`kiko`'s side** (`src/platform/macos/helper-app.ts`): `spawnHelper(name, args, stdio)` returns a `HelperProcess` at once (streams, `exit`/`close` with code and signal, `kill`, `pid`, `via`), as `spawn` did; `runHelper` collects one run's output (`permissions status`, `mic --list`). `mic.ts`, `hotkey.ts`, `apple-stt.ts` and `permissions.ts` use them. Behind it a `Broker` decides once per process (again after the app goes away): the bundle built or there, then its socket asked `hello` with the bundle's `BrokerBuild`. The same build at the same path: used. Another build (an update, a rebuild) with no other client: told to quit, and this one started; with another `kiko` using it: left alone, and this process's helpers run directly. Nothing answering: `open -g -j`, then the socket waited for (15 s). ## Updates
An installed copy updates itself the way Claude Code does (`src/core/updates/`: `feed.ts`, `layout.ts`, `verify.ts`, `updater.ts`). A development checkout never does: git pull.
- **The install** (the layout the install script writes): `~/.local/share/kiko/versions/<version>/` each version as its tarball unpacked it, `current` and `previous` links to two of them, `~/.local/bin/kiko` → `current/kiko`, and, when `~/.local/bin` wasn't on the PATH, one marked line in the shell's startup file (`uninstall.ts` `PATH_MARK`: `kiko uninstall` takes out only it). A copy is installed when its folder (`src/core/app-root.ts` `APP_ROOT`; KIKO_APP_ROOT stands in for it in tests) is one of `versions/`; its version is that folder's name.
- **The look:** `${KIKO_RELEASES_URL:-https://kiko.sh/releases}/latest.json` (`{ version, date, notes, assets: { "darwin-arm64": { url, sha256 } } }`), at most once a day: on start and every 10 minutes the app's `Updater` asks whether it's due (`due`: a day since the last good look; an hour after one that failed, quietly). Never waited for; offline says nothing (`update_check_failed` in the log). What it saw is kept in `~/.local/state/kiko/update.json` (`checkedAt`, `latest`, `skip`, `announced`, `failed`).
- **The install of a new one** (`install`, under a lock folder so the app and `kiko update` never both do it): only ever forward (a version lower than or the same as the one running or `current` is refused, `DowngradeError`; the updater logs a lower one as `update_downgrade_refused` and says nothing; `--rollback` is the only way back); downloaded (fetch, or a file:// read) with its sha256 checked as it comes (a mismatch: `ChecksumError`, nothing kept), unpacked with `tar` into a folder beside the versions, **checked** (below), and only then its one `kiko-<version>/` renamed into place and the links switched: each made beside the old one and renamed over it, so a start finds one version or the other. The one before becomes `previous`; older ones are removed, never the one running.
- **When the network goes** (`feed.ts` `download`, `readUrl`; 2026-11-16, after a VPN switched on mid-download froze `kiko update` at 88% and then printed Bun's raw ECONNRESET): an attempt that gets no bytes for 20 s is given up on (a socket left half open by a network change never ends by itself), and a download cut off (a reset, a timeout, a broken pipe, a fetch that failed, the stall, a body shorter than its Content-Length, a 5xx, 408 or 429) is tried again up to 3 more times, after 2, 5 and 10 s, all within the overall 10 minutes. When the first answer said `Accept-Ranges: bytes`, it goes on from where it stopped (`Range: bytes=<done>-`, `If-Range` with a strong ETag): a 206 whose `Content-Range` starts there is appended to the file and to the same running sha256; a 200 to the Range, or a 206 that starts elsewhere, is fetched again from the start with a fresh hash. **kiko.sh doesn't take ranges** (the site's Worker answers `/releases/*` from R2 whole, without `Accept-Ranges`), so there it's always from the start. Not tried again: a `ChecksumError`, a refusal for the invite, any other 4xx, a full disk. The feed is tried again twice (after 1 and 3 s, 15 s each). What's said in the end is plain (`NetworkError`): "The download was cut off: the network changed or dropped. Nothing was installed; run kiko update to try again.", or for the feed "kiko.sh couldn't be reached: …"; `kiko update` prints it, the updater keeps it as `failed` (Settings → Updates says it). The error itself and the tries go to the log only: `update_failed` / `update_check_failed` with `why` and `tries` (`kiko update` loads the log only for these). Headers are never logged (they carry the invite's token), and a URL's query is cut out of `why`.
- **What makes it ours** (`verifyRelease`, 2026-11-01): the sha256 comes from the same server as the tarball, so it only catches a broken download. No key of our own: Apple's code signature. Every executable that ships (`kiko`, each helper in `native/`, `bin/tmux`: `signedFiles`, the list `release.sh` signs; and the helper app, `signedBundles`, with `--deep`, as `sh.kiko.helper`) must pass `/usr/bin/codesign --verify --strict -R=<requirement>`, the requirement (`requirement`) `anchor apple generic and certificate 1[field.1.2.840.113635.100.6.2.6] exists and certificate leaf[field.1.2.840.113635.100.6.1.13] exists and certificate leaf[subject.OU] = "YU89X2P36Z" and identifier "sh.kiko.<file>"` (a Developer ID Application certificate of `name.ts` `TEAM`, the file's `IDENTIFIER`-based identifier; file names that aren't plain are refused before they reach it). Scripts can't carry a signature, so the executable vouches for them: `release.sh` builds it last, with the SHA-256 of every other file in its folder baked in (`--define __KIKO_RELEASE__`, `src/core/release-files.ts`), and the new version's `kiko _verify-files` (run once its own signature has passed, with an empty environment) checks its folder holds exactly those files, unchanged, no links, and prints the version it was built as, which must be the feed's (an old build of ours relabelled as newer is a downgrade). A failure is a `SignatureError`: the unpacked folder is deleted (also one unpacked before), nothing is switched, `update_rejected` is logged with why, `update.json` keeps `refused` (that version and file, not fetched again) and the header says "Update 0.1.1 refused: not signed by Kiko's developer"; Settings → Updates has why, and `kiko update` says it.
- **Test and ad hoc builds** (`trustOf`): what an installed copy accepts is baked in when it's built, never read at run time. A release takes only `TEAM`'s Developer ID; one built with `KIKO_SIGN_IDENTITY=-` (the tests', a build to try on this Mac, never uploaded) has `adHoc: true` baked in and takes ad hoc updates too (identifiers, files and version still checked). No setting, environment variable or feed field can loosen a release. A checkout is as strict, but honours `KIKO_UPDATE_SIGNATURES=adhoc|off` (its own source, which whoever runs it can change anyway; the unit tests' fake installs).
- **What you see:** the header (and the window's line, between what it's set to and the keys) says "Update 0.1.1 ready · restart to use it" in cyan, and one info line with the release notes the first time (never said aloud). Settings → Updates: the version, Restart now / Install it now / Look again, and Automatic updates (`autoUpdate`, on by default; off, only "Update 0.1.1 available · kiko update").
- **Restart now** waits like a settings restart (nothing left to read out, nobody talking), then exits with 77; `start.ts` runs the command that starts it (`app-root.ts` `programArgv("start")`) from the new version's folder (`relaunchCommand`), in the same pane, so the window and every session stay. Quitting and running `kiko` does the same.
- **`kiko update`** (`src/update-cli.ts`) does it in the foreground with progress; `--rollback` swaps `current` and `previous` and keeps the rolled-back version out of automatic updates (`skip`) until `kiko update` is run by hand.
- **The invite** (`invite.ts`, 2026-11-05; the beta is invite-only, the site's RELEASES.md "Invites and the gate"): each invite is one person's install link, `https://kiko.sh/i/<token>`, which answers `install.sh` with the token written in. The script keeps it in `~/.local/share/kiko/install-token` and a random id for this Mac in `install-id` (both mode 600), and sends both (`Kiko-Install-Token`, `Kiko-Install-Id`, from a header file, never on a command line) with `Kiko-Client: install`. The updater and `kiko update` read the same files and send them with `Kiko-Client: app/<version>` on every request for the feed and the download, only to the feed's own origin (`headersFor`). kiko.sh can refuse (once its gate is on): a refusal carries `Kiko-Install-Error` (`missing`, `unknown`, `revoked`, `limit`), which becomes an `InviteError` in our words ("This install link was turned off. Ask for a new invite: support@kiko.sh"); the updater keeps it in `update.json` (`link`), the header says "Updates stopped: this install link was turned off", Settings → Updates has the full line, it looks again a day later, and the first look that gets through clears it. `kiko update --token <token>` (the link or the whole command work too) keeps a token for an install that has none (one from before the invites) or a new one after a re-send. Nothing in them says who you are or which Mac: the id is random, and kiko.sh logs only the invite, the id, the time, what was fetched and Cloudflare's country and city, never an IP address.
- **Switches:** KIKO_UPDATES=off (no look at all), =force (a checkout looks too, and only says); KIKO_RELEASES_URL (another feed, file:// for tests; plain http:// only on this Mac, for the site run locally).

## The two names

`src/core/name.ts` has two:
- **The product's**, `NAME`: titles, docs, the CLI's words, the OpenRouter title, the session on the app's own code ("tell the Kiko session…").
- **The one you call it**, `spokenName()`: the `spokenName` setting (Settings → Listening → Its name, first-run setup's "Its name" screen, `KIKO_SPOKEN_NAME`), `SPOKEN_DEFAULT` ("Kiko") until set. One name for both what you say ("Jarvis, ask Lacy…", the wake word hands-free) and what it calls itself out loud ("Hi, I'm Jarvis", the status line's `handsFree.name`, a session named like it shown as "Jarvis project": `sessions.ts` `appNamed`).
- **Read at each use, never at import:** `config.ts` sets it once `.env` is read and the settings screen when you change it (`setSpokenName`); `wake.ts` rebuilds its pattern when the name changes (`leadPattern`), as do `routing-rules.ts`'s `asksTrustScratch`/`aboutVoiceApp`, the dictionary's built-in entries (`dictionary.ts` `builtInEntries`: the names', the domain's and a few common coding tools', in the code, never in your file, which starts empty; your entries win) and the speech hints (`stt-hints.ts`: the name first). So a new name works on your next words, no restart.
- **Misheard spellings** are per built-in name (`name.ts` `misheard`): Kiko's (the spellings speech-to-text gives it, written "Kiko" wherever they are in what you said: built into the dictionary). A name of your own has none. `wake.ts` `nameWarning` warns (never refuses) about one syllable or an everyday word.
- **Replies without the name, hands-free** (the `replyWithoutName` setting; `wake.ts` `Listener`, `hands-free.ts`): in a session's reply window, words without the name are its reply only when they're a real one (`realReply`): two words or more once fillers ("uh", "hmm", "huh") and openers up front ("hey", "okay", "to") are left out, or one that answers what it asked (`asks`: "yes", "no", "allow", "stop"… for a permission prompt or a turn that needs you, any word for Claude's multiple-choice question; nothing for one that was only reporting, the short window). A phrase whose words turn out to open with the name ("Hey Kiko, just testing", the name written late) is said to the app by name, even after it was taken as a reply. And only in a quiet room: talk without the name heard up to `BACKGROUND_BEFORE_MS` (5 s) before the app started speaking, still going as it started, or a voice there within `ONSET_MS` (250 ms) of the mic coming back (`DEAF_TAIL_MS` after the app stops), and that one window needs the name: logged `reply_window_needs_name` (`why`: before, during, onset), shown as "say “Kiko” first" beside "waiting for reply" and in the status line. Phrases it didn't take in a window are logged `reply_window_ignored` (`why`), never their words. A reply's final words get the dictionary's fixes, as its live words do.
- **The routing eval** keeps its recorded words; `tests/eval/recorded-name.ts` maps the name they were recorded with, Kiko, to the name in use as `run.ts` loads them (`KIKO_SPOKEN_NAME=Jarvis pnpm eval:routing`).

## Two flavours: the release and Kiko Dev (2026-11-01)

A checkout and an installed release can be on one Mac side by side, sharing nothing. `src/core/name.ts` `FLAVOUR`: `release` for an installed copy (Bun's `/$bunfs/`), `dev` for a checkout; `KIKO_FLAVOUR=release|dev` forces one (the environment's, read before `.env`). Everything two copies mustn't share comes from `flavourNames(flavour)` (`OWN` this one's, `OTHER` the other's), the release keeping every real name:

| | release | Kiko Dev |
|---|---|---|
| on screen (`TITLE`: the window, its ☰ button and list label, terminal titles, the phone's hub name) | Kiko | Kiko Dev |
| command (`COMMAND`; usage, errors) | `kiko` | `kiko-dev` (`bin/kiko-dev`; `bin/kiko` in a checkout is the same) |
| settings folder (`CONFIG_FOLDER`: settings, dictionary, pronunciation, renames, session-numbers, quick-always, phones, missed, codex-sessions, `run/` with the lock) | `~/.config/kiko` | `~/.config/kiko-dev` |
| hooks socket, state file | `/tmp/kiko.sock`, `/tmp/kiko.state.json` | `/tmp/kiko-dev.sock`, `/tmp/kiko-dev.state.json` |
| tmux server (`tmux.ts` `SERVER`), launch files (`kiko-env-…`) | `-L kiko` | `-L kiko-dev`, `kiko-dev-env-…` |
| Keychain service (`keychain.swift --service`) | `sh.kiko.keys` | `sh.kiko.dev.keys` |
| phone port (Bonjour `_kiko._tcp` for both; the hub name tells them apart) | 7333 | 7334 |

- **What stays the product's** (`NAME`, `SLUG`): what it says ("Kiko's hooks", the router's "this voice app is called Kiko"), the spoken name (a setting, default Kiko, either flavour), the executable and the release's install (`~/.local/share/kiko`, `~/.local/bin/kiko`, `~/.local/state/kiko`).
- **Shared on purpose:** the scratch folder (`~/Kiko/scratch`: your sessions' work, not the app's; Claude Code's trust of it carries over), Pocket's port 8737 (one runs at a time and each stops its own server; a server left by either speaks the same API), the quick check's MCP name (`mcp__kiko__`, in a per-run config file), Codex's and T3's client names, the OpenRouter title, the keychain helper's signing identifier (`sh.kiko.keychain`: the program, not the keys), `$TMPDIR/kiko-quick-*` (random names), tmux option and buffer names (per server). Each flavour's statusline cache is its own (`kiko-statusline`, `kiko-dev-statusline`).
- **Logs:** a checkout's stay in the repo's `logs/`, a release's in `~/.local/state/kiko/logs/` (`app-root.ts`), as before. Settings → Logs → Keep logs prunes them (`core/log-retention.ts`): at start and once a day, only run logs (`<start time>.jsonl`, plain files, never links) last written longer ago than the window, never this run's or `latest.jsonl`'s; 30 days for a release, forever in a checkout unless set. `logs_pruned` has the count and bytes.
- **A release never reads a .env file** (the user's rule, 2026-11-01): not its own, not one in the folder it's started in. Only a checkout reads one, its own repo's, explicitly (`app-root.ts` `ENV_FILE` is null when `INSTALLED`; `config.ts` and `keys-cli.ts` read nothing else). Bun's executables otherwise read a `.env` and a `bunfig.toml` from the working folder before any of our code runs, so an installed `kiko` started in someone's project took that project's API keys and any `KIKO_*`/`TTS=…` in it (seen: `env -i kiko keys` there named keys from "environment"). So `scripts/release.sh` builds with `--no-compile-autoload-dotenv --no-compile-autoload-bunfig` (Bun 1.3.3 or newer, which it checks), then starts the executable in a folder with a `bunfig.toml` and fails the build if it read it; `install-live.test.ts` checks the same. The hooks and the status line run through the executable (`kiko hook …`), so they're covered too; Pocket's Python, uv, tmux and the Swift helpers read no `.env`. Never add a `.env` read for the release; its settings are `~/.config/kiko/settings.json`, the Keychain and the real environment.
- **Hooks** (`hooks/lib.mjs`, `statusline/kiko-status.mjs`): the scripts tell their flavour by where they are (a checkout's have `src/` beside them), never by `KIKO_FLAVOUR`: both installs' entries run on every event, and each must reach only its own app (its socket; one not running: nothing there, gone at once). In an agent's file, an entry is this app's by its script's root (`app-root.ts` `hookFlavour`, used by `hooks-file.ts` `isOurs`): its own root (the folder, or `current`) this flavour; `~/.local/share/kiko/…` or `<root>/kiko hook …` the release; any other `node <a checkout>/hooks/…` Kiko Dev. So status, connect, repair, `upgradeHooks` and disconnect each touch only their own entries, and both sets sit in `~/.claude/settings.json` and `~/.codex/hooks.json` together.
- **One at a time** (`running.ts` `otherRunning`): `kiko`/`kiko-dev` (the window, `claude`, `codex`, `opencode`) and the app refuse while the other's lock is held by a live app or its socket answers: "Kiko (the release) is running; quit it first…" (both want the talk key and the mic). Not asked with `KIKO_SOCKET` set (tests, harness).
- **Kiko Dev's first start** (`src/core/first-start.ts`, from `cli.ts` and `start.ts`): until a mark (`first-start.json`) or its own `settings.json` is in `~/.config/kiko-dev`, it copies `~/.config/kiko` there (not `run/`; what's there already kept, folders merged), and the keychain helper copies the keys from `sh.kiko.keys` to `sh.kiko.dev.keys` itself (`keychain copy`: never through Node, names only on stdout). The originals stay. Never with `KIKO_SETTINGS`/`KIKO_SOCKET` set or under the test runner.
- **Tests** run as the checkout, Kiko Dev (`tests/setup.ts` drops `KIKO_FLAVOUR`); the end-to-end test installs a real release, which is the release. `tests/unit/core/flavour.test.ts` has the derivation, the scripts, hook ownership side by side, the first start and the refusal.

## Environment variables

- **One prefix:** every variable Kiko reads is `KIKO_<NAME>` (`src/core/env.ts`), but the API keys and other programs' own (`CODEX_HOME`, `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, `TMUX`). A setting's variable wins over settings.json; `SETTING_ENV` is the one table of them, which `config.ts` reads and the settings screen names ("KIKO_TTS in .env wins").
- **No names from before:** until 2026-11-01 most were bare (`TTS`, `HOTKEY`, `SOUNDS`…), and the classifier's were `JEV_*`; none of those are read now (another program's `TTS=…` changes nothing). The harness's timings are `KIKO_TEST_*`, and Pocket's voice is `KIKO_POCKET_VOICE`.

## Developer switches

Env vars for working on Kiko itself. They aren't settings: no settings row, setup question or `kiko` flag shows or changes them, and settings.json can't turn them on.
- **`KIKO_DEV_SHADOW=1`**: shadow decisions (`src/core/shadow.ts`). Another classifier model (`src/core/classifier.ts`) gets the same request as the deciding one on every utterance and finished turn; its answers are only logged (`shadow` lines, paired with the `classify` line by id, `jev` in logs before 2026-11-01), never acted on, and `pnpm shadow:report` (`scripts/shadow-report.ts`) compares the two. Off without the switch, since it pays for a second decision call each time and sends your words to one more model. With it on, `KIKO_SHADOW_MODEL` picks the shadow on finished turns (d1-ultra by default) and `KIKO_UTTERANCE_SHADOW_MODEL` the one on your words (by default whichever of Jev and d1-ultra isn't deciding them); `off` turns either off. A `shadowModel` or `utteranceShadowModel` left in an older settings.json is ignored.

## tmux, always

Kiko always runs as its window, in its own tmux. There's no switch to run it without: `--no-tmux` / `--tmux`, the "Use tmux" setting and the plain full-screen app they gave are gone.
- **The CLI** (`src/cli.ts`) reads its command line with `src/cli-args.ts` (`util.parseArgs`, strict, options only before the command; an unknown option is a usage error, exit 2, with the closest known one suggested). With no tmux (a checkout without one; an installed copy brings its own), every command that needs it (the window, `claude`, `codex`, `opencode`, `attach`, `ls`, `trust-scratch`) stops and says `brew install tmux`; an agent's run that prints and quits (`kiko claude -p`) needs no tmux and runs plain. `pnpm start` is `kiko`. Its plain lines (and `keys`', `login`'s, `logout`'s, `account`'s, `update`'s, `uninstall`'s) are marked and styled by `src/ui/cli-style.ts`, as GitHub's CLI does its own (cli/cli `pkg/iostreams/color.go`): `✓` green for done, `!` yellow for look at this, `X` red for an error (the command exits 1); bold for values (an email, a code, a name, a version), dim for hints. The markers stay on a pipe; the colour only on a terminal, never with `NO_COLOR`, `CLICOLOR=0` or `TERM=dumb` (`CLICOLOR_FORCE` forces it on a pipe), as go-gh decides it. A table is padded before it's styled, so its columns hold. Setup's screens (@clack/prompts) style themselves.
- **Which tmux** (`src/terminals/tmux/tmux.ts`): `tmuxPath()` is the one for Kiko's own server (`-L kiko`) and every client of it, the only way anything of Kiko's finds tmux (the CLI, the app's runner, and the window's panes, which take it as an argument: the placeholder script, the frame, the viewer, the menu popup, `afterProgram`). An installed copy runs the tmux its release ships (`app-root.ts` `BUNDLED_TMUX`: `current/bin/tmux`, through the link so a long-lived pane survives an update; built by `scripts/build-tmux.sh`, RELEASING.md), never the PATH's; `tmuxEnv` adds its `terminfo/` after macOS's own on `TERMINFO_DIRS` for every run (the server passes it to its panes: macOS 13 has no `tmux-256color`). A checkout uses yours on PATH, else Homebrew's or MacPorts' folders (`userTmux`); `KIKO_TMUX_BIN` names another. Your own tmux server (the clash check, when `kiko` runs inside it) is always asked with yours (`userTmux`).
- **The app** (`app.ts`) runs in the window's list pane only (`start.ts --window`, then `app.ts --window`): started anywhere else, it and `start.ts` say to run `kiko` and exit. The one exception is `--plain` (scrolling text, `src/ui/plain.ts`), which only the faked-app harness uses.
- **`KIKO_KEPT_TMUX`**: LEAVE_TMUX keeps a session's TMUX there; `inKiko` reads it, and `withoutTerminal` doesn't pass it on.

## The Kiko window

`kiko` (`src/cli.ts`) opens one terminal with Kiko's list on the left, a Claude Code session on the right and Kiko's state in the bottom line. tmux does all the drawing; Kiko never draws a session's screen (it isn't a multiplexer).

- **Layout** (`src/terminals/tmux/window.ts`, which imports nothing that loads `.env`, so the CLI uses it too): the window is a tmux session of its own (`_kiko`; a name `freeName` never gives a folder) with one tmux window, the view, of two panes: the **list** (`@kiko-role list`) and the **stage**. Every Claude Code session stays a tmux session of its own, as before. Showing one `swap-pane`s its pane into the stage: the same pane, process and tty, so the adapter's pid match and safe-typing checks don't change. The one shown before goes back to its home window (`@kiko-home`, set when it came in; made again if that tmux session was killed), and a placeholder (`placeholder.sh`, asking tmux where it is) fills whichever spot is empty: the stage, or the home of the session shown. A `window-resized` hook puts the list (the leftmost pane) back to its width (as you last dragged it, `@kiko-list-width`, which a `MouseDragEnd1Border` binding notes; else a quarter), unless zoomed. The view keeps exited panes (`remain-on-exit`), and a `pane-died` hook on the window's session puts a session that quit back home with its placeholder and closes it, all in tmux, so it works with the app stopped; `tidy` does the same for what the hook couldn't. **A session Kiko opened that quits by itself** (`/exit` or Ctrl+D typed in it, a crash; any agent: log 2026-10-30 01-57-49, where `/exit` typed in a window-opened session left its bare shell in the stage after it had left the list) is tidied as Close tidies it: its launch line (`index.ts` `open`) ends `afterProgram`, which asks tmux, the moment the program returns and before the shell is anyone's, whether the pane has been in the window (`@kiko-home`, on its own server by the socket in `TMUX`, back by `BACK_TMUX`, and its own `TMUX_PANE`); if so the shell ends too, so the pane dies: in the stage `pane-died` puts the placeholder back and closes its tmux session, at home its tmux session ends quietly (no `remain-on-exit` there) and the stage stays as it was. Never in the window (shown in a terminal of its own, the window not open), or tmux not answering, it's left in the login shell as before, as a terminal is; so is a program only stopped (Ctrl+Z: its status 128 + TSTP, STOP, TTIN or TTOU by `kill -l`, kept as `kiko_status` before `BACK_TMUX`), its process not gone (then, as always, bash's `exec` ends a stopped job itself, zsh's leaves it stopped). Nothing typed or started in a shell is ever ended: the decision is made before there's a prompt, and only on a pane Kiko's `open` started (its launch line is the mark); a shell of your own in Kiko's tmux running `claude` keeps its shell and whatever you run there, and `kiko claude` needs nothing (its pane's process is the agent itself, `exec`: the pane ends with it, as before). The app's side (`src/ui/window-glue.ts` `exitedByThemselves`, at each look): a pane of a listed session gone since the last look, not being closed by Close, is logged `session_closed` with `how: "exited"`, `pane` and `shown`, and Kiko's page goes into the empty stage at once (not `PAGE_AGAIN_MS` after the last time), as after Close; one that left the list with its pane still there (Claude Code's SessionEnd comes as it quits, before its process ends) is looked at again every 250ms, up to 12 times. A stage found dead is tidied and the page put in the same look. Panes opened before this change keep their shell (their launch line is the old one). A home is only ever a placeholder's: the hook checks the pane there is one before respawning it, and `homeOf` takes nothing else, so a live session that ended up in another's home (a race) is never killed. Every layout change (`show`, `view`, `tidy`, `closeSession`, `openWindow`, `closeWindow`) reads the layout and acts on it, so they take turns (`inOrder`, one queue per process: the app's clicks, keys and voice-opened sessions); Kiko's page put in an empty stage (`view(…, onlyIfEmpty)`) never pushes out a session shown meanwhile. The window is made in one tmux run (`new-session … ; split-window … ; set …`), so two `kiko`s at once can't both make it. Several terminals attached are several views of one window (`window-size latest`). Every tmux run has `-u` (`tmuxFlags`): without a UTF-8 locale (launchd, no `LANG`) tmux writes tabs and non-ASCII as `_`, and `list-panes` read back as no panes.
- **The classic look** (tmux 3.8 and later; branch `feature/classic-look`, 2026-10-30: the user much preferred the old full-screen UI's wrapper (`--no-tmux`'s, since removed), and picked it from a preview, branch `look-preview`): the window looks like the full-screen UI, the stage's content (the real Claude Code or Codex) untouched. `src/terminals/tmux/classic.ts` decides once per runner (`classicLook`: `tmux -V`, 3.8 or later; `KIKO_LOOK=tiled` asks for the old look for windows made from then on) and has the layout and colours; the window keeps what it is in `@kiko-look` (`classic`), read with the layout (`View.look`), so the app follows the window, not its own guess. **Panes:** one ordinary pane, the **frame** (`@kiko-role frame`, `frame.ts`), is the window's only tiled one, under everything; the **list** and the **stage** are tmux 3.8 floating panes over it (`new-pane`, `-B single`), so each is a box of its own: four sides, its title on the top border (`pane-border-status top-floating`, `CLASSIC_TITLE`), square corners (tmux has no rounded pane borders), no padding inside the stage. The boxes as the full screen sizes its panels (`boxes`: the list 30% of the width, 26 to 40 columns, or as dragged, `@kiko-list-width` holding the list's box width; both from the window's first line to the one above its last: no header, branch `feature/one-line`). `swap-pane` works between a floating spot and a tiled home (checked live): a session swapped in takes the stage's box, its placeholder goes home tiled, so `show`, `view`, `closeSession`, DIED_HOOK and `tidy` are unchanged; `newStage` makes a floating placeholder in the stage's box; `tidy` respawns a frame that ended. The window is made in one run as before (`classicCommands`: frame, stage, list, each made active as it comes so its role is set on it, the list last); a tiled window opened by a `kiko` whose tmux floats (the user's, made before) is made over (`toClassic`: the frame split in across the window and given its role in the same run, the list and stage lifted out with `break-pane -W`, the keyboard back where it was; same panes, same processes). **Focus:** the two border colours are window options, so tmux picks them by the active pane (`pane-active-border-style fg=#4d5577`, `pane-border-style fg=#44475a`); titles bold, `#f8f8f2` focused, `#8d91a8` not; the list's box is "Sessions", the stage's the shown session's harness mark (`@kiko-mark`, a tmux format the app sets with the names: `markFormat`, the glyph as text, the colour only as `#rrggbb`) and name, an outside session's viewer with where it runs after it, dim ("notes · in Ghostty": `@kiko-place`; a session of Kiko's tmux, the name alone), or "Kiko" for its page. Tab (the menus' `focus`) is `focusRun`: by role, not tmux's next pane (there are four now; zoomed, tmux reports no pane floating, so not by that either). **No status line:** the frame paints one line, the window's last, which the app renders (`src/ui/classic.ts` `frameLines`, Ink's `renderToString`; the frame protocol is `{bottom, menuAt}`, no `top`) and sends over a socket of its own (`src/ui/window-frame.ts`: `~/.config/kiko/run/frame.sock`, 0600 in the 0700 run folder; its path kept on the server as `@kiko-frame-socket`, which the frame reads every second until it connects; a test with `KIKO_SOCKET` gets one next to it, or in a private `/tmp/cbf-*` folder when that's too long for a socket's 103 bytes, and never the app's own under the test runner). Up to 20 frames a second while you talk (the waveform), never through tmux's options; each one dedupes what it sends. The frame says "Kiko is starting…" before an app ever spoke, "Kiko isn't running · run kiko to start it here" after; it resizes with the window (it's the tiled pane) and puts the boxes back then (`place`), and the end of a drag of a box's border signals it (`kill -WINCH` its pane's pid); the app places them too once settings unzoom the list. **Dragging the split** (branch `fix/classic-drag`; before, only the box dragged changed while you dragged, a gap or an overlap until you let go): both boxes move together on every step, like a split. tmux 3.8 resizes a floating pane by itself as its border is dragged (`resize-pane -M`, the default `MouseDrag1Border`): no binding runs for a drag's steps (with no drag command, a step off the box is no key at all) and `mouse_x` is empty on a border (it's a pane's), so no binding can follow the mouse. But each step resizes the box, which fires tmux 3.8's `pane-resized` hook, and hooks run before tmux redraws: the window's hook (`splitHook`) puts the other box against the one dragged, in tmux (`run-shell -C` of formats, no shell): the split where the dragged box's edge is (the list's right one, the stage's left one), kept as `boxes` keeps a dragged width (`draggedFormat`: 18 columns to half the window), both boxes as the frame places them (`placeFormat`: the list from the left edge, the stage to the right one, the window's first line to the one above its last), so a drag along a top-and-bottom border changes nothing. Only while a drag is on (`@kiko-dragging`, set by the classic `MouseDrag1Border` before tmux's resize, holding the list's width as it began), so a resize of any other kind (the window, zoom, a session swapped in) is the frame's and the app's as before. Its end, over a border or over a pane (the mouse is often over the stage once the split has stopped at a limit), even one in copy mode, scrolled back, whose own table would otherwise take the release (copying, and leaving copy mode): the drag's start puts the dragging client in a key table of ours (`kiko-drag`, `switch-client -T`; tmux uses a mode's table only while a client is in its root one) with `MouseDragEnd1Border` and `MouseDragEnd1Pane`, and tmux puts the client back in its root table as one runs (or as a key not in it comes). The end: the width kept in `@kiko-list-width` when it changed (so the window keeps it as it resizes, to half a narrower one), the drag over, the frame told, which places the boxes exactly where the drag left them. A drag of another kind over a pane is tmux's as before (nothing of ours in the root table for it). Accepted limits: a box's top border still moves it while you drag (a move resizes nothing, so nothing fires) and it goes back when you let go, as before; at a limit the split stops (tmux's own drag only follows a border the mouse is on), so coming back it picks up again once the mouse passes over the border, as at a tiled pane's smallest size. **The line** (branch `feature/one-line`, 2026-10-31, the user's design: before, a header on the first line and a footer on the last) has three zones, two spaces apart at least. **Left, the state:** Kiko's state as the tiled look's bottom line words it (`bar.ts` `doing`, `waitingOn`, `noticeSpans`: spans, one piece of logic drawn two ways, the tmux format byte for byte as before), drawn the full screen's way: the red badge (`● LISTENING`, `● REPLY`, `● KIKO`) and the mic's waveform (`meter.ts`, a level a frame, newest on the right) while listening and never the words, a spinner for deciding (after 500ms) and checking, names in their harness colour, the key clash's notice after it, never blank (what showed before is kept for a moment with nothing new); the logo and "hold right ⌥ to talk" are gone (the user's choice). **Middle, the info:** what the full screen's header has on its right (`aboutText`: muted, silent on this Mac, voice, model, classifier, cost; then the mic), dim, centred in the space between the other two (rather than two spaces after the state: the two differ only once it fits whole, and centred balances the line, most of all with the stage's one key). **Right, the keys,** dim: the list's (`windowKeys`: `. repeat · m mute · 1-9 pick session · s settings · q quit · ⌃Space menu`) while the list has the keyboard, only the menu's while the stage has it (the one key that works there); which, tmux's `pane_active` for the list, asked as the list's focus reports come (not the reports themselves: your terminal losing focus sends one too). Narrow, the middle gives way first (cut from its start with "…", then gone, under 12 cells), then the keys from the left (the menu's last), then the state (cut at its end only when it alone is wider than the line). No timings (the log's `press_done` has them; the settings screen's `Footer` still shows them). ## The microphone

One helper, `native/macos/mic.swift`, stays up for the whole run (through the helper app, so the permission is Kiko's); `platform/macos/mic.ts` talks to it in lines: `start` and `stop` on its stdin, `device <uid>\t<name>` for the mic picked in Settings → Listening → Microphone, 16 kHz mono PCM back on stdout while a press is on, and status lines on stderr (`started <ms>`, `using`, `devices`, `hold`, `hold_lost`). It makes its own Core Audio input unit on the device it uses (never the system default first, 2026-11-13), keeps it set up between presses, and sets it up anew only when that device or its format changes.

- **Which device** (`mic.swift` `choose`, mirrored by `core/mic-device.ts` `resolveMic` for the settings and the log): the one you chose if it's there, else the system default, which is whatever macOS has as its default input, a Bluetooth headset's mic included, followed at once as macOS moves it (since 2026-11-14: macOS's default, nothing custom; from 2026-11-12 until then the built-in mic stood in for a Bluetooth default). Opening a headset's mic switches it to its call profile: seconds before any sound, and what it plays drops to call quality; the hold is what makes that a one-time cost rather than one at every press. For full-quality sound on AirPods, pick the Mac's own mic in the list: that needs no logic of its own.
- **The hold** (2026-11-13; `mic.swift` `held`, mirrored by `heldMic`): a Bluetooth mic *in use*, one you chose or the system default (2026-11-14), is held open for as long as it's the mic in use. Its unit runs all along, so the headset stays in its call profile, nothing switches at a press, and `start`/`stop` only gate what's sent on (measured on AirPods Pro 2: a cold open 1.6–3 s to the first sound; held, 11–19 ms, with no switch in between). The helper decides it, from the choice it's told, macOS's default input and the device's transport, since it sees the device and the default come and go first; nothing in the app turns it on. The app tells it the choice as it starts, the system default too (`device` alone), and nothing is held before that. It begins when the mic becomes the one in use (chosen, connected, made the default, or as the app starts); it ends when it no longer is (another chosen, the default moved elsewhere, the mic you chose back, disconnected), or the helper ends (its stdin closing is enough: a hold never outlives the app). `mic_hold` says each with its `why`, and `default: true` when it's held as the system default. The price is said in the setting ("System default (AirPods, mic stays on)", "AirPods (mic stays on: instant, call-quality sound)"): macOS's mic indicator stays on, and what the headset plays is call-quality. A mic that isn't Bluetooth is never held.
- **Privacy rule of the hold, not to be loosened:** what the mic hears outside a press never leaves the I/O callback. It's rendered into one scratch buffer (the input has to be pulled to know whether the link is up), looked at only for "all exact zeros or not", and zeroed before the callback returns: never handed to the helper's queue, never converted, never written to stdout, never kept for the next press (no pre-roll), never logged. A press gets what's heard between its `start` and its `stop`.
- **"started"** counts from the `start` line to the press's first audio; for a Bluetooth mic, to its first buffer that isn't all exact zeros (`mic.swift` `Press`): its unit gives zeros until the call link is up, and those aren't sent on. So a press that lands before the link is up (right after the AirPods connect) still shows "connecting to AirPods…" and, if the key goes up first, says "The AirPods mic didn't start in time." Both name the mic that press is really opening (`mic.using`, kept current as it moves): once another mic stands in for the AirPods, never them.
- **Under the hold, watched and logged** (`mic_hold`, `mic_hold_lost`): its link coming up (`linkMs`, with the headset's output rate then), its own format changing (set up again, held on), its output going back to the music rate, half a second of exact zeros after sound ("silent"), no I/O cycle for 4 s ("stalled"), and a sleep (the wall clock running ahead of the uptime clock). A press on a held mic logs `mic_started` with `held: true`, `linkUp` and the real ms to the first sound.
- **Listed, but no link** (`mic.swift` `linkStep`, `pressWaits`, `tryAgain`): AirPods out of your ears, or taken by the iPhone, can stay listed on the Mac and give no sound (2026-11-13, on the real ones, most likely out of the ears then: held, not one sample that wasn't zero in 5.5 s, and macOS moved its default devices to the built-in ones 1.7 s in). A link that isn't up 5 s after it was asked for is asked for once more, and 5 s later the hold is given up on (`mic_hold` off, "no link"): the mic counts as away, chosen or the default, and a press takes a mic that works: the system default if that's another device, else the built-in mic (`mic_started` with `noLink`, and `fallback` when a mic was chosen). The app keeps which mic that is from the hold's lines (`noLinkAfter`, `state.micNoLink`), so the header and the setting name the mic really in use. A press never waits on a link that was up and went, or that's only being tried again: only on one coming up for the first time since it became the mic in use ("connecting to AirPods…"). It's tried again when the devices or the default input or output change (AirPods back in your ears become the default again), after a sleep, or after a press, never sooner than 30 s after it was given up on (twice that each time in a row it gives none, to 8 min), and never by a timer of its own: a headset that's elsewhere isn't asked for its mic in a loop. Not while the mic is open either: hands-free, which keeps it open, stays on the stand-in until the mic is chosen again.
- **Nothing shown or said about a Bluetooth mic coming or going** (since 2026-11-14: earphones back in their case were shown as missing; a wireless mic is used while it's connected and dropped quietly when it isn't): disconnected, not there at the start, giving no link, or back, as the system default or as the mic you chose, it's only logged (`mic_fallback`, `mic_back`, `mic_hold` on and off with why, `mic_hold_lost`, `mic_switched`). The header names the mic in use, never in yellow; the setting reads "AirPods when connected (MacBook Pro Microphone now)" while a chosen one is away (its option "used when connected"), and "System default (MacBook Pro Microphone)" when macOS's default has moved. Whether a chosen mic that isn't listed is a Bluetooth one is told by its UID (a Bluetooth address, `bluetoothChoice`) or by its having been listed as one in this run: only its UID and name are saved. A mic you chose that *isn't* Bluetooth (USB, the iPhone's) keeps its notice when it goes ("USB Mic isn't connected: using …, the system default, until it's back"), the header's "(… missing)" and "… is back: using it again.": unplugging a wired mic is worth saying.
- **A device that comes and goes mid-press** (2026-11-14: AirPods put away were listed again for a quarter of a second, held, their link "live" in 30 ms, and gone 236 ms later): the hold ends with its device (`settle`, "disconnected"), so none is left running; a press that's open follows the device there and back (`follow`, `mic_switched`), losing at most the moment it was on a link that wasn't up; and a press whose mic couldn't be opened at all (it went as it was asked for) is opened where it should be at the next change (`pressed`), never left deaf until key up. Not done: a press under way still moves to AirPods the moment they connect, and their link takes a second or two to come up (silence in the middle of what you're saying); keeping it on the mic it began on needs a second input unit.
- **The volume across profiles** (`platform/macos/duck.ts`): a headset keeps one volume for music and one for calls, and the reading jumps between them as the hold begins and ends. The player says which output it's on and, for a Bluetooth one, its rate (`speaker.ts` `output`: "AirPods@24000"); a duck's note carries the output it was lowered on and is only ever compared with a volume read on that same one, so a profile change is never taken for Kiko's lowering or for yours, and nothing is raised or lowered for it. A note left on the other profile is kept aside and looked at again when that profile is back.

## Speech out

Everything the app says opens through `openSpeech()` in `app/speech.ts`: one voice at a time (`core/speech-lock.ts`), in line with the beeps (`core/audio-queue.ts`), and respelled for the voice in use on the way in (`core/pronunciation.ts`: tmux said as "tee mux"; domains, emails, URLs and file names said as they're read out, "kiko.sh" as "kiko dot S H", "localhost:3000" as "localhost port 3000", a path to a file as its name). The respelling is only for the ear: what's shown, logged and kept in the timeline is the text as written. Announcements, answers, confirmations, quick-task answers, voice samples and the macOS voice's text for a phone all go through it.

An output that takes no sound (AirPods the Mac has as its output while they're with the iPhone) doesn't hold everything up: the player gives up on it after a few seconds (`native/macos/player.swift` "stalled", with a backstop in `platform/macos/speaker.ts`), the app goes on, and what was cut (`core/audio-queue.ts` stalled()) is named on screen, stays what "repeat that" says, and is said again once sound comes out again (`core/unplayed.ts`).

An output whose format changes under a reply (AirPods going between their music and call profiles, 48 ↔ 24 kHz, as a mic on them is opened or closed: when a hold begins or ends, or another app does it) stops the player's engine by itself, and a stopped engine ends every buffer still scheduled, played or not. Until 2026-11-13 those all counted as heard: a 19 s reply said "idle" 2.4 s in and stopped mid-sentence (`speaker_rerouted` with `unheardMs` 0). Now a slice counts as heard only if its completion comes while the engine runs and no sooner than it could have played (`player.swift` `heardBy`); the rest stay queued and play on once the engine is back, from the slice that was playing (what was still on its way out of the device is said again: about a quarter of a second on AirPods). `speaker_rerouted` then says what was really left, and `output_format` the output's rate each time it changes.

Speech is mono and has to be the same on both of an output's channels. On 2026-11-14, with the AirPods' mic held open (their call profile: 24 kHz, two channels), replies were heard in the right ear only while every other app played in both, after the player's engine had been stopped by the change from 48 kHz and started again. It hasn't been made to happen without the AirPods: with the speakers taken from 48 to 44.1 kHz and back under an inaudible signal the mixer gave both channels the same and the output unit's channel map stayed `0,1`, and offline every two-channel format came out the same on both. So the player leaves nothing from before a change, and says what it plays into (`player.swift`, "Both channels"): an engine that stopped is replaced by a new one made for the output as it is then, never started again; a sound that starts on an output whose rate or channels aren't what the engine was started on gets a new engine first (that day the engine came back 0.14 s into the change of profile, 1.4 s before the link was up, and nothing looked again); on a two-channel output the mixer is connected in plain stereo at the output's rate rather than in whatever layout the device gives its channels (a mixer following a two-channel layout that isn't left and right puts mono on the first channel alone); and `player_output` logs, at start, each time the engine comes back, as a sound starts and after it ends, the output's rate and channels as the device has them and as the engine plays into it, the output unit's channel map, the device's channels for stereo and their labels, and the level of each channel the mixer gave the output over that sound (`left` and `right`, dBFS: levels only, nothing of the sound is kept). A reply that's one-sided again is read from those lines: the two levels apart is the player's mixer; the same, with a map other than `0,1` or an engine format unlike the device's, is the output unit; all of it right is below the player.

A Bluetooth output (AirPods; the device's transport type) sleeps on digital silence, though the player's engine runs all along, and loses the first few hundred ms of what comes after: a whole ~130 ms cue. On Bluetooth only, the player keeps the link awake with a signal far below hearing (30 Hz, -54 dBFS, at the player's gain: muted, silent) from a sound or cue until 30 s after the last, then stops (battery, and Apple's moving the AirPods between your devices); and a cue (`"B"`, as `Speaker.write(pcm, true)`) that comes with the link not kept awake waits 500 ms for it to wake. Speech never waits. Logged: `output_route` (device, `bluetooth`), `output_keepalive` (`on`), `cue_preroll` (`ms`, `asleep`).

## Spoken summaries, and "more"

A finished turn's summary (`core/summarizer.ts` `summarize`) is one streamed Haiku call (or the OpenRouter model picked), started at once beside the classifier's `decideStop` (worth saying? needs you?), and held until the classifier answers so a quiet turn never makes a sound. How much it says is the **Summary length** setting (`LENGTHS`): brief (the outcome in a few words, also said again at the end of the message, where Haiku heeds it), short and normal (the two there were, word for word), detailed (two to four sentences, the main points). At brief and detailed one more line keeps what the reply says still fails, wasn't done, or wasn't checked or tested, in a few words; normal and short go without it (on the eval it kept a little more, within the noise, for longer summaries). Its question (`claudeAsked`) is asked at every length, as before. A reply over 6,000 characters is cut by `core/reply-shape.ts` `clipReply`: its start and end, and every must-keep sentence of the middle (`mustKeepOf`: needs you, failure, risk, skipped, caveat, by word lists) that fits, never a "2 tests still fail" in the middle.

What decides the length is the model reading the reply, not a read made before it. Measured on the user's replies (`tests/eval/summary.ts`): the code's read of the kind of reply (`shapeOf`) and Jev's (`decisions.ts` `replyKindQuestions`: Jev gets the kind right more often, 71 of 84 against 55) given to the summary as hints raise how much it keeps, but Jev's has to be waited for (about 0.35 s more to the first word) and both make summaries longer; the hints stay an option of `Turn.shape` for the eval only.

**"More"** (`decide.ts` `moreOf`, `routing-rules.ts` `moreRequest`): "more", "tell me more", "details", "what else?", "go on", "Lacy, more", "more from Lacy", "more about the failing tests", "what did it say about the migration?" — the whole of your words, read in code like "skip that", before anything that would send them to the session waiting or the one whose screen they're said in on the phone. Not "what about Germany?" or "what else is left to test?", which go to the session as they always did. It's about the session your words name, else the one whose screen you're in, else the one you heard summed up last (`heardFrom`: the newest timeline entry with `spoken`); with none heard yet, it's words like any others. `app/announce.ts` `tellMore` says `Summarizer.more`: a longer summary of that same reply (what can't be left out, then what the short one left out; four to six sentences, three or four at brief and short), or, with a topic, close to all the reply says about that part, or "It didn't say anything about the migration." when it doesn't (`MORE_NOTHING`). Streamed like an announcement; the turn waiting keeps waiting, and your next words still answer it. Logged as `more`.

**What you say right after a summary** that says it fell short is logged as `summary_feedback` (`core/summary-feedback.ts`: `more`, `more_about`, `missed` for "you missed…", `what_about`, `follow_up` for a question back), with the reply's id (`replyId`, also on `announce`; the summary eval's case ids are the same), the summary heard, your words, and what the app did with them (`went`). Only logged: nothing about where the words go changes. They're for the eval's next cases.

## Quick tasks and their permissions

A quick task (`core/quick-task.ts`, started by the router's `quick_task`) is one `claude -p` run, look-only, whose answer is spoken. What it may use without asking: Read, Grep, Glob, and Bash for `READ_ONLY_COMMANDS` (`--allowedTools`, each bare and with arguments; the name lookups `whois`, `dig`, `host`, `nslookup` among them). What it never gets: `DENIED_TOOLS` (`--disallowedTools`: Edit, Write, NotebookEdit, git's writing commands), whatever any settings say. `--restricted --strict-mcp-config` keep the user's settings, hooks and MCP servers out; `--tools` names the built-in tools it has (WebFetch and WebSearch included, so they can be asked for).

**Everything else is asked of you** (since 2026-10-29; before, `dontAsk` and `--permission-prompts none` denied it silently):
- **Claude Code's side:** `--permission-mode default --mcp-config <file> --permission-prompt-tool mcp__kiko__permission`. In print mode Claude Code sends any call that no rule decides to that MCP tool, `{ tool_name, input, tool_use_id }`, and takes back one text block holding `{"behavior":"allow","updatedInput":…}` or `{"behavior":"deny","message":…}` (checked in Claude Code 2.1.312's docs and code). The config file (`mcpConfigFor`) is written for each run in a private temp folder (`kiko-quick-*`, mode 0600) and removed when the run ends; it runs `hooks/quick-permission.mjs` with this Node, and gives it the socket and the longest wait in its environment, never on a command line.
- **The MCP server** (`hooks/quick-permission.mjs`, stdio JSON-RPC, no dependencies, in the spirit of `claude-code-hook.mjs`): each call goes to the app's hooks socket as a `PermissionRequest` for session `kiko` (`QUICK_CHECK_ID`), the input's long fields cut. Allowed: the input back whole. Anything else is a deny, at once when there's no socket or nobody listening, else on a deny, a let-go (`decision: null`), a closed connection, or no answer within `KIKO_QUICK_ASK_MS` (the app sets 3 minutes: a backstop, the permission window comes first). A call about its own tool (the model calling it) is denied without asking.
- **The app's side** (`core/quick-ask.ts`, wired in `app/announce.ts` `onRequest` → `quickAsked`): it reaches the app through the agent's hooks socket like any session's prompt. With no quick task running it's denied. `quickVerdict`: denied outright when it would change things (`deniedOutright`: the editing tools, any MCP tool, a git command that isn't one of its look-only forms, even inside `cd x && …`); allowed without asking when that exact command is in `quick-always.json` (`loadQuickAlways`, next to the settings file); else held as a `QuickAsk`, which wraps the hook's `Held` for the floor: named "Kiko's quick check" (`nameOf`), asked in fixed words (`quickAskLine`, no model) with the classifier's risk rating as for any prompt, never made the `target` your next words go to; `allow(always)` keeps the command (Bash only: `alwaysRuleOf`; the web is asked every time) and tells Claude Code a plain allow; `release()` (the window ran out, `whyClosed` says so) is a deny, since there's no terminal to fall back to. The quick task's time limit stops while it asks (`Running.pause()`, resumed when it's answered or goes away).
- **After it:** a run that ends with a denial (`permission_denials`) or needing changes is offered to a session, once (`offersHandOff`): its answer, then `HAND_OFF_LINE` ("I couldn't check that quickly. Want me to open a session for it?"), or for needs-changes `needsChanges()` as before. A yes to that offer, as the app's last words (`handOff` in `decide()`'s state), starts a session in the task's folder with the task as its first message (`start`, the same path as the router's `start_session`), with no router; from ~ in a scratch folder instead, unless your yes says "home" (above, "Sessions for no project"); the same for a change asked for outright, offered without a run; no is "Okay, I'll leave it."; anything else goes to the router as for any question the app asked. "Send it to <session>?" (a session already in that folder) is still left to the router.
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/core/quick-permission.test.ts` (the MCP server against a stand-in socket; a whole run with `tests/support/fake-claude.mjs`, a fake `claude` that asks through it), `tests/unit/core/quick-ask.test.ts`, and the harness case "phone: a quick task asks to run whois" (`T.realQuick`, `T.hooks.listen()`): the real run, the fake `claude`, the real server and socket, allowed and denied from the phone. Never the real Claude.

## The phone

**Hidden in the release until the iPhone app reaches testers** (2026-11-16): behind `PHONE_LIVE` in `src/core/name.ts` (false; `core/env.ts` `phoneLive`, read at each use: the release only once it's true, Kiko Dev always; `KIKO_PHONE_LIVE=0` turns it off, `1` on). Off, Settings has no Phone section (`app/phone.ts` `phoneRows` none, and `settings-screen.ts` `applySetting` refuses the Phone keys with "The iPhone app isn't out yet."), `setPhone` never starts the server, so no relay and no Bonjour, whatever a saved `phone: true` says (`config.phone` false; the saved value is kept and takes effect once it's on), Play on this Mac counts as on, and nothing mentions the phone: `kiko --help`'s `logout` line, setup's microphone step, the trust card's "or on your phone", signing out's warning about a paired phone (`account-cli.ts` `pairedPhones` none). Asking the app to pair the phone ("pair my phone", `core/routing-rules.ts` `asksPhone`) is answered with that one line in `app/deliver.ts`, before anything is decided. On, everything is as below.

The phone is one more mic, speaker and screen. `src/remote/server.ts` (off unless Settings → Phone is on) only translates between `protocol/` and the app. It is the connections and their messages; beside it, `tell.ts` is what the phones are told (the sessions, the state, the prompt, each line), `missed.ts` (`PhonesMissed`) what they missed and its settling, and `phone-hands-free.ts` hands-free from the phone:
- **What the phone sees** is what the TUI draws, from the same UI store (`core/ui-store.ts`, told by `src/remote/tell.ts`), plus a session's conversation from its agent (`agent.watchHistory`: `core/history.ts` on Claude Code's transcript), which a TUI session view can use too.
- **A session's screen (the chat)** gets nothing an agent has to say specially: `core/history.ts` turns any agent's record (Claude Code's transcript, Codex's and OpenCode's mirrored into one) into the same steps, with each edit's counts and diff and the step still running, worded as what the session is doing ("Running pnpm build": `doingOf`), so the live line comes from the transcript as it's written, every half second, not from a hook; earlier messages are the same fold read further back (`historyBefore`). Who answers and in what colour is the agent's own `speaker` and `mark` (as `ui/marks.ts` speakerOf), and what its menu can do is `core/can.ts` canDo, the window menu's list; Interrupt, Rename and Close run the window's own paths (`stopSession`, `renameSession`, the window's `close`), told back to that phone.
- **What the phone does** goes through `PhoneHub`, implemented in `app/phone.ts`, its presses in `app/talk.ts` as one with the key's and hands-free's (`begin`, `listen`, `wordsOf`, `end`): the talk button holds the floor like the push-to-talk key and its audio goes through the same speech-to-text; spoken or typed, its words go through `act()` and `decide()`; a permission tap is `settlePermission()`, like the Mac's allow and deny keys (a quick check's prompt too: its session ID is `kiko`, which the phone knows has no session to open), and the answers to Claude's questions are `settleQuestions()`, like the answers by voice. A session's screen adds `to` to `decide()`, as if you'd named the session.
- **What the Mac says** is teed: the PCM its player gets (Cartesia, Pocket) goes to phones that asked for it, as it's made; the macOS voice can't be streamed, so its text goes for the phone to say. **Play on this Mac** (switched only from the phone app; the Mac remembers it but has no setting for it) turns the Mac's own speaker off: `core/mac-output.ts` then stands in for the player and `say` with a clock, so phones get the same audio and the floor, reply windows and "speaking" keep the timing of the speech. **A press's cues** (the "listening" blip, and "sent" when your words reach a session or answer a prompt) are feedback for whoever pressed, so they play only where the press was: a press on the Mac (key, hands-free, the TUI's keys) on its speaker whatever Play on this Mac says, never on a phone; a press on a phone on that phone only (no "listening" blip: its haptic says so).
- **Mute** (`m`) is a TV's (`core/mute.ts`): nothing stops, it goes silent. The Mac's player's gain goes to 0 (`player.swift` "V", the player node's volume, at once mid-line) and the floor still hears "playing" and "idle" when it always would; the phones' playback is cut and nothing more is sent, the chunks kept with when they play, and unmuting sends them what's left from that moment (the chunk it's in trimmed), which their playback buffer takes as a new stretch: no phone change. The macOS voice can't be turned down mid-line, so with it muting still cuts the line and nothing is said until unmuted (and only then is a line's text on screen taken as heard: `floor.forget`). Cues stay silent while muted; a voice sample asked for in Settings is heard anyway.

- **What the phone missed**: each line Kiko says is kept for every paired phone that isn't there to hear it (not connected, or away in the background), in `src/remote/missed.ts` (the record, `MissedLog`, and the server's side of it, `PhonesMissed`), and sent as `missed` when it's back. Only a record: Catch me up reads the lines through `openSpeech()` like anything said, behind what's being said, and nothing about the Mac's own floor changes. It says one short summary of what still needs you (`src/remote/catch-up.ts`, one Haiku call); a line that no longer does (a question answered on the Mac, an update replied to or superseded) is settled and left out, and the phone's Stop cuts a catch-up like a press. A trust card still up, or a prompt standing after its window (`hub.promptUp`), keeps its question unsettled even while another prompt is shown over it, or long after it was asked; it's settled when the card is. A standing prompt let go unanswered (its hold limit, or its questions left to the terminal) never just disappears from a phone that wasn't there (`promptLetGo`): its question is `superseded` by a note, "Lacy asked to run: npm test — waiting in its terminal" (the Claude app, or its agent's own app), kept as a `permission` line with no prompt of its own (nothing to press) for each paired phone not in front, and told at once to an away one (`missed_line`, quiet while you're at the Mac). It settles as any question let go of: the session no longer listed blocked (`watchSettled`: `answered_on_mac`), a newer update from it (`closed`), or gone (`checkSettled`). A phone in front sees the session's own "needs you" card instead.
- **A new session from the phone**: its New session sheet is the window's picker (`c`), asked over the socket (`src/remote/new-session.ts`): `get_new_session_options` is answered from the same code as the window's picker and its agent question (`pickerChoices`, `offeredStarters`, `preselect`, and `launchersIn`'s apps with their folders), and `new_session` goes through `placeOf` and `startSession` / `startInApp` with `by: "phone"`, which return what became of it (the starting-… id, or why not in the words the window would show) for `new_session_status`. A first message said there is a press with `dictate`: it holds the floor and goes through the same speech-to-text, but `phoneTalkEnd` only writes its words down (`Dictations`) for the `new_session` that names it, never through `act()`. A session the phone opened while it was starting is listed with `startedAs` once it has started, so the phone's chat follows it. A name given there (`new_session` `name`) goes where a name said goes (`startSession`'s `name`): on the agent's command line, else kept on the starting entry (`by: "phone"`) and given once its agent lists it (`started` → `pendingRenames` → `renameWhenIdle`: `renameByAgent` where its `can` allows, else its rename command typed once it's idle), so only a voice-asked rename is dropped when Voice can doesn't allow managing; `core/can.ts` `namedAtStart` says which agents can be named (`canName` in the options), and one that can't is refused in words. **The chat of a session still starting** (`PhoneServer.watch`): a starting-… id belongs to no agent yet (`together` would hand it to the main agent, whose record of it doesn't exist), so the screen gets an empty history; each flush, `followStarted` watches it again once its agent lists it (found by that id as an alias), through that agent now, still under the id the phone asked for. The phone also watches again when its screen moves to the new id (`SessionView` `onChange(of: sessionId)`).
- **Where the Mac is**: `paired` and `welcome` list every address the phone can use (`src/remote/addresses.ts`, from the platform's `network`: home, Bonjour, Tailscale's address and MagicDNS name, and the relay for a phone set up for it). The phone keeps them and tries them itself (the one that worked last, then home, then Tailscale, then the relay), so pairing once at home is enough.
- **Encrypted at home** (`src/remote/local.ts`, `cpace.ts`): every direct connection (home network, Bonjour, Tailscale) runs the relay's tunnel too, so nothing crosses the network in the clear. The phone opens with Noise IK to the Mac's static key (`relay-identity.ts`, now loaded whenever Phone is on, from the Keychain or, with it off, `device-key.json`), with its own static key and the prologue `kiko local v1`; to pair, it runs CPace (CFRG's PAKE) with the 6-digit code, so the code never crosses and a LAN eavesdropper can't test codes offline, and swaps the static keys inside (`pair` `key`, `welcome` `macKey`); a phone paired before this opens once with CPace on its token's hash and moves to keys, so nobody pairs again. `LocalLink` hands the server a `TunnelSocket` (`tunnel-socket.ts`, shared with the relay) and how it was opened, which decides who may sign in on it. An app from before is told in the clear to update; the phone acts on no refusal that comes in the clear. Spec and threat model: `protocol/README.md`, "Encrypted at home".
- **Pairing by QR code** (`src/remote/pair-link.ts`, `src/core/qr.ts`, `src/ui/pair-phone.ts`, `app/phone.ts` `pairPhone`): Settings → Phone → Pair a phone draws a QR code in the terminal (two modules a cell, black on white whatever the theme) holding one URL in the iPhone app's scheme (its bundle identifier, from `IDENTIFIER`): where the Mac is, a one-time 128-bit secret, its static key, the relay. The phone pairs with CPace on the secret (the home connection's `qr` opening), so nothing is typed, and checks `welcome`'s key is the one the code showed. One phone, 5 minutes, dead once its screen closes; the 6-digit code shows beneath it and works as before. Spec and threat model: `protocol/README.md`, "Pairing with a QR code".
- **The relay** (on only with `KIKO_RELAY_URL`): the Mac connects out to its room on the relay (`relay/`, a Cloudflare Worker) and signs in with its own Ed25519 key, kept in the Keychain (`src/remote/relay-identity.ts`); phones join the room with a credential from `relay_pair` (sent inside the encrypted connection at home). Each phone runs an end-to-end encrypted tunnel to the Mac through it (Noise IK, `src/remote/tunnel.ts`), and the relay client (`src/remote/relay-client.ts`) hands each tunnel to the server as a socket like any other (`PhoneServer.adopt`), so nothing else in the server knows. The spec, byte for byte, is `protocol/README.md`, Relay. **The Mac never sits "connected" on a dead socket** (2026-11-13; a VPN turned on left it unreachable for 75 s on a socket dialled while the VPN was coming up, log 2026-11-13T21-35-13-752Z): the relay client pings every 10 s and a pong must come within 5 (it was every 25 s, looked for only at the next ping), so a dead socket is noticed within 15 s (while frames are still waiting to go, the ping queued behind a reply's audio on a slow uplink, the pong has 25 s in all); and the Mac's network is watched (`src/remote/network-watch.ts`: `os.networkInterfaces()` read every 2 s, loopback and Apple's peer-to-peer `awdl`/`llw` left out; a poll 10 s late is the Mac waking), so once a change has settled for 2.5 s, and once more 5 s later, the socket is pinged at once and its pong given 3 s (`RelayClient.check`). No pong: the socket is ended and dialled again at once; a dial that then fails waits as before (1 s doubling to a minute), and a dial not signed in within 15 s is given up. A change while it waits to dial dials now; one while it's dialling is left to that dial unless it's been 3 s. The pings are answered by the relay's runtime without waking the room and count against no limit, so the relay didn't change. Logged: `relay_checked` (`why`: "network changed" or "wake"; `result`: "pong" with `pongMs`, "no pong", "dialled", "dialling", "dial stuck") and `relay_disconnected`'s `why` ("no pong", "network changed", "wake", "dial timed out", or the close's code). The home path needs none of it: there the phone dials the Mac, so there's nothing for the Mac to dial again; a connection left half-open is taken over by the phone's next one (`supersede`), or dropped by the server's own 15 s ping.

The protocol, its examples and both sides' tests are in `protocol/README.md`; the server's pieces in `src/remote/README.md`; the app in the iPhone app's repo's `README.md`.
