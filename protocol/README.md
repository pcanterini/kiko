# Protocol

The messages between Kiko on the Mac (the hub) and the apps that talk to it: the iOS app first, others later. This folder is the only thing the two sides share. The Mac side (`src/remote/`) and each app (the iPhone app, in its own private repo; later Android) never import each other's code.

**This folder is the source of truth.** The iPhone app keeps a copy of it (`protocol/` in its repo), brought over by that repo's `scripts/sync-protocol.sh` (from this repo beside it by default), which lists what changed; its tests then decode every example. So: change the protocol here, with the hub's side and its tests, then sync it into the iPhone app's repo and run the app's tests there. Never edit the copy.

## Rules
- **One schema**: `schema.json` describes every message, both ways. A message that isn't in it isn't sent. `$defs` has one entry per message type, named after it, and `x-from` says who sends it (`phone` or `hub`).
- **A version number**: `version` in the schema, and `v` on every message. Bump it on any change a client must know about; the hub takes only its own version and answers anything else with `error` `version`.
- **Example messages**: `examples/` holds one JSON file per message, named `<type>.json`, with variants as `<type>.<variant>.json` (`announcement.end.json`). The hub's unit test (`tests/unit/remote/protocol.test.ts`) checks each against the schema and that every type has one; the iOS tests (`KikoTests/ProtocolExamplesTests.swift` in the iPhone app's repo, against its synced copy) decode each, so the sides can't drift apart.
- **The hub checks what it gets**: every message from the phone goes through the same validator (`src/remote/schema.ts`, no dependencies: only the keywords the schema uses). A new keyword in the schema needs a line there.

## Version 1

JSON text frames over one WebSocket (`ws://<address>:7333`; the port is `KIKO_PHONE_PORT`), each sealed in an end-to-end encrypted tunnel opened at the start of every connection (below, Encrypted at home): what this section calls a text or binary frame is one record of it. The Mac advertises `_kiko._tcp` with Bonjour, with TXT entries `v`, `host` (its `.local` name) and `port`, so the phone finds it to pair.

**Every way to reach the Mac.** `paired` and `welcome` carry `addresses` (optional; a hub that doesn't send it leaves the phone with the address it paired on): `{ kind, host, port }` for each of the Mac's home-network addresses (`home`), its Bonjour name (`bonjour`, the `.local` name) and, with Tailscale running on the Mac, its Tailscale address and MagicDNS name (`tailscale`), in that order, then, for a phone set up for it, the relay (`relay`: its `url` and the Mac's `room`; below, Relay). The hub looks them up when it starts and again as each phone connects. The phone keeps the list, refreshed by every `welcome` (keeping the Tailscale entries it knew when one lists none: Tailscale off on the Mac), and tries the address that worked last first, then home and Bonjour, then Tailscale, then the relay, each starting when the one before fails or hasn't answered within a second; the first `welcome` wins and the others close. The token is the Mac's, so it works on every address: pair once at home, and the phone finds the Mac anywhere Tailscale reaches.

**Signing in.** The first message (the first record, once the tunnel is open) must be `hello` (a token) or `pair` (the 6-digit code the Mac's settings show, or the secret of the QR code it shows: Pairing with a QR code, below), within 10 seconds. `pair` is answered with `paired` (the phone's token, which it keeps; the Mac keeps only its hash), then `welcome`; `hello` with `welcome`. Then the hub sends `missed` (what was said while the phone was gone), then `sessions`, `state` and `permission_prompt` at once, and again whenever they change. Refusals (`unauthorized`, `bad_code`, `pairing_closed`, `too_many_attempts`, `qr_used`, `qr_expired`, `auth_timeout`, `version`) close the connection; `unauthorized` means pair again. While Kiko Pro is on sale, a phone whose Mac's plan doesn't include it gets `plan_required` instead of `welcome` (or `paired`), and the connection closes with 4014: not an error, and never a reason to pair again (below, The iPhone app and the plan).

**Where the app is, on every connection.** Right after `welcome` the phone sends `presence`: `back` when its app is in front, `away` when it's in the background or the phone is locked (an app still running there reconnects: the Mac restarted, the socket dropped as the phone locked, the network came back). Until it does, the hub counts the connection as one that doesn't play what's said (the Mac plays it, with Play on this Mac off too) but doesn't keep what's said for it as missed. An app from before this never says on connect: a connection that hasn't said within 2 seconds is taken to be in front, as before (unless the same phone has said on another connection: that one is the phone's, and this is a dial it dropped). The first `presence` on a connection also says the app took it: the hub lets go of that phone's older connections then (closed with 4000 `replaced`; often one already gone at the phone's end, still open at the Mac's, as the phone moves between the home network and the relay), as a replacement, not the phone leaving. Nothing changes in the messages themselves (an older hub takes `presence` right after `welcome` as it always did), so this stays version 1.

**What the phone missed.** Each line Kiko says (a session's turn read out: `announcement`; a session asking to use a tool: `permission`; Kiko's own line, such as a quick question's answer: `answer`) is kept for every paired phone that isn't there to hear it: not connected (locked, the app closed, out of reach), or connected but away (`presence` `away`, sent best effort as the app goes to the background; a phone that goes without saying is away once its connection drops). `missed` carries the unread ones, oldest first, the newest 50 at most: `{ id, at, sessionId, name, kind, text }`, with `text` as shown on screen (not respelled for the voice) and `id` going up with time. The phone marks them read with `seen` (`upTo` an id) and asks for them aloud with `catch_up`: the hub reads them in order through its usual speech (on phones with `play_on_phone`, on the Mac with Play on this Mac), behind anything being said and never cutting it, with the session's name first where the line doesn't say it; a press stops them like any speech, and they count as read. Only a record: nothing about what the Mac says, or when, changes. All four are additions to version 1: an app that doesn't know `missed` ignores it.

**One summary, and Stop.** `catch_up` with `summary: true` (the phone's Catch me up) says one short combined summary of the named lines that aren't settled, grouped by session ("While you were away: Lacy finished the migration and is waiting for you; Kiko merged two fixes and asked you to restart."): one quick model call with those lines as its input, two to four sentences, streamed through the usual speech. One line is just read; if the call fails, the lines are read one by one. Without `summary`, the named lines are read one by one, as before (a line's play button, Read all). While a catch-up is being said or waits its turn, `state` has `catchingUp: true`, and the phone shows Stop: `catch_up_stop` drops what's still to come and cuts what's being said of it, as a press would (a press still stops it too).

**Settled.** A line that no longer needs you carries `settled: { how, at }`, kept in `missed.json`:
- a permission question: `answered_on_mac` (the Mac's keys, by voice there, or in its terminal once the hub let it go: the session stops being listed as blocked), `answered_on_phone` (the phone's Allow or Deny, or by voice from the phone), or `closed` (it went away without an answer here: the session's turn finished, the session was interrupted or ended, or the hub let go of it a while ago and the session isn't blocked on it);
- a session's update: `replied` (words went to that session since, by any path: a reply, a send, typed into it by hand), or `superseded` (a newer update from the same session);
- any line: `seen_on_mac` (you took it in at the Mac, so it isn't news on the phone). Said out loud by the Mac (Play on this Mac, or the Mac standing in, and not muted) while you were at it: input in the last 90 s, the screen unlocked and awake, read as it's said. Or only shown there (the screen unlocked and awake as it was said, but not said out loud, or you'd been idle longer) and Kiko used on the Mac within 2 minutes after: its talk key, its window, words typed into a session it holds, or (with input at the Mac just then) a prompt typed into a session's terminal or a question answered there. Said while the Mac was locked or asleep, or when it can't tell: never. Added 2026-11-02; a phone that doesn't know a `how` shows it as handled.

Connected phones hear of it at once (`missed_settled`), and `missed` carries it. Catch-ups (the summary, and `catch_up` without ids) leave settled lines out; a line named on its own is still read. All of these (`settled`, `missed_settled`, `summary`, `catch_up_stop`, `catchingUp`) are optional additions to version 1.

**Told at once while away.** A phone whose connection is still open but away (`presence` `away`: its app went to the background or the phone was locked, and iOS hasn't suspended it yet) gets each line kept for it as it's kept: `missed_line`, `{ item }`, the item exactly as `missed` would carry it. Only to that phone's away connections, and only a line it kept (a repeat it already has isn't told again); never to a phone in front (it heard it), one that hasn't said where it is yet, or one not connected. The app posts it as a local notification; the line still comes in `missed` as the phone comes back, and settles as usual (`missed_settled`, which also takes the notification down). An optional addition to version 1: an app that doesn't know it ignores it. Permission prompts need nothing new: `permission_prompt` already goes to every connection, away or not.

**Quiet while you're at the Mac.** When a line or a permission question goes to an away connection while someone is at the Mac (its keyboard or mouse used in the last 2 minutes, its screen neither locked nor asleep, read as it's sent), `missed_line` and `permission_prompt` carry `quiet: true`: the app keeps the line (its list, its badge) and shows the card as usual, but posts no notification. Without it (away from the Mac, or the Mac can't tell) it notifies as before. Optional additions to version 1: an app that doesn't know `quiet` notifies as before.

**Kiko's quick checks.** A question that needs the Mac ("how much disk space do I have?") is answered by Kiko's quick check: a one-off, read-only look, never a session (it's never in `sessions`). While one runs, `state` carries `checking: { what, startedAt }` (what it was asked to find out, and since when, ms since 1970): the talk bar says "Kiko is checking: <what>". When it asks to use a tool, its `permission_prompt` has `sessionId` `"kiko"`, `name` "Kiko's quick check" and `checkFor` (the same words as `checking.what`): the card says "Kiko's quick check, for: <checkFor>" over what it wants to do. The line Kiko says as it ends (its answer, or why it couldn't) carries `checked` (what it checked): on its `announcement` (`kind` `app`), and on the `missed` / `missed_line` item kept for a phone that wasn't there (`kind` `answer`). The phone keeps it in Updates as "Kiko checked: <checked>" with the text as the answer, read like any other line; a catch-up reads it as "Kiko checked <checked>: <text>". All optional additions to version 1 (2026-11-05): an older app ignores them, and shows the answer as Kiko's own line as before.

**Every prompt held.** Kiko asks one prompt at a time, out loud; others wait their turn behind it, or behind a reply being read out (2026-11-04: kiko-dev's prompt waited two minutes behind a reply whose speech kept stalling, never asked: the phone listed kiko-dev as needing you, with nothing to answer). So `permission_prompt` also carries `queued`: each other prompt Kiko holds open, in the order they'd be asked, shaped as `prompt` with `remainingMs` null (no window until it's asked), and `risky` only as far as is known before it's asked (the safety gate's; the classifier reads it as it's asked). The phone shows each as a card with Allow and Deny (Answer for questions), answered with `permission` (or `answers`) by its `promptId`, exactly as the one asked: settled at once, never asked after, and what's being said isn't cut for it. The message goes again whenever the list changes; left out when it's empty. A prompt asked moves from `queued` to `prompt`. An optional addition to version 1: an app that doesn't know it shows only `prompt`.

**Prompts that stand after their window.** A prompt nobody answered in its voice window stands, whether or not anyone is at the Mac: Kiko keeps its hook held for up to an hour, and it stays answerable from every phone (and the Mac's card, and the session's own terminal, where its agent shows it too). It comes as `prompt` when nothing else is being asked (with `remainingMs` null: no countdown), else in `queued`, oldest first ahead of the ones not asked yet; answered with `permission` (or `answers`) by its `promptId` exactly as any other, through the held prompt itself. It goes by itself only before its hook's limit (the hour); then, or once it was answered anywhere (a phone, the Mac's card, its terminal), or its session moved on or ended, every phone gets `permission_prompt` without it, and an answer after that gets `prompt_gone`. A phone that missed its question gets, when it's let go unanswered, the question settled `superseded` and a new `permission` line in its stead, `"<session> asked to <what> — waiting in its terminal"`, with nothing to press: a note that settles once the session moves on (no longer blocked, a newer update, or gone). Nothing new in the schema: an app that knows `queued` and `missed` shows it as it is.

**A prompt in full.** `what` is short, for a card's line ("run: " and a command cut to 300 characters on one line). A prompt's `detail` is its tool call in full where there's more: a command as written (its lines kept, up to 8000 characters), a file's or a page's whole path. The app shows `what` on the card and `detail` on the request's sheet. Optional: left out where `what` says it all, and by older hubs.

**Claude's questions.** A session can ask you questions instead of asking to use a tool (Claude's multiple choice: 1 to 4 questions, each with a header, the question, 2 to 4 options and whether it takes several; any can also be answered in your own words). They come as a `permission_prompt` whose `prompt` has `questions` (`{ question, header, options: [{ label, description }], multiSelect }`), and `what` says it ("ask 2 questions: Install tmux, Checks"). The phone answers with `answers`: one `{ labels, text? }` per question, in order, `labels` being the options' own (one at most unless it takes several), with something picked or written for each; the hub checks them (`bad_answers` when they don't fit) and settles them like an answer by voice on the Mac, and `prompt_gone` when they aren't waiting any more. The Mac asks the same questions out loud meanwhile, one at a time; whichever answers first settles them, and the other side's card goes (`permission_prompt` null). `permission` `deny` declines them; an app that only knows `permission` and sends `allow` leaves them in the session's terminal. Optional additions to version 1: a hub without them never sends `questions`.

**An agent's trust question.** A session Kiko opened in a folder its agent (Claude Code, Codex) doesn't trust yet (with Claude Code, every new scratch folder) asks, before it starts, whether to trust it. Kiko shows it as a `permission_prompt` whose `prompt` has `folder` (as shown, `~` for the home folder), with `what` saying it ("trust its folder ~/Kiko/scratch/2026-10-29-dozer") and the session still starting (its `starting-…` id, listed idle with "Starting…"). It's not a tool permission: `allow` trusts the folder and the session starts (what was said for it meanwhile goes in then); `deny` ends that session. Answered with `permission`, like any other; there's no "always". It has no window (`remainingMs` null): it stays up until it's settled, answered on the Mac, by voice, on this or another phone, in the session's own window, or the session ended; then every phone gets `permission_prompt` null (or the next prompt) and what was missed of it is settled (`missed_settled`). A `permission` for it is taken while it's up even if another prompt is the one shown meanwhile. An optional addition to version 1: an app that doesn't know `folder` shows it as any permission ("Wants to trust its folder …").

**What became of your words.** A phone that sends a `talkId` with `talk_start` or `type` (any string up to 100 characters; the app uses a UUID per press or message) gets `talk_status` back as its words move on, to that phone only: `{ talkId, stage, sessionId?, name?, text?, detail?, queued? }`.
- `heard`: the transcript is ready (`text`: your words as heard, for a press; typed words come without, the phone has them).
- `routed`: where they're going: a session (`sessionId`, `name`), or Kiko itself (both null). Again for another session when several are asked.
- `held`: they wait before going in: a send held a moment because the hub had to guess where it goes ("To lacy in a moment"), or words for a session whose finished turn is still to be read out ("Waiting for lacy's answer to be read out"); `detail` says which, in words to show.
- `delivered`: in the session. `text`: what went in (it may have been cleaned up), how the phone finds it in the conversation; `queued: true` when it was typed while the session was busy: Claude Code takes it once it finishes (or as it goes).
- `started`: Claude began the turn with them: at once for a reply through the session's held turn; for typed words, when the session takes that prompt in (Claude Code's optional `UserPromptSubmit` hook), or when the turn ends with them among its words (taken in mid-turn).
- `replied`: the turn they started is over: the session's Stop hook came, so its reply is ready (to be read out, or not). Until then the phone shows that a reply is owed (the pill, the line under your message, the session's dot). A typed message taken in mid-turn starts and is replied to as that turn ends. A session that never says (no Kiko hooks, closed) never sends it: the phone stops waiting by itself after a while.
- `answered`: Kiko itself dealt with them (they went to the app, not a session: a question, a permission answer, starting or stopping a session).
- `failed`: nothing was sent; `detail` says why, short ("The lacy session has closed", "Not sent to lacy: osascript: not allowed", or the `error`'s own message when the hub turned the press or message down: someone else talking, the session gone). Every way words can go wrong between hearing and typing ends here; none is left at `heard` or `routed`.

Stages only move forward; `failed`, `answered` and `replied` end a talk (`replied` came later in version 1: a hub before it ends at `started`, and an app before it ignores it, as any stage it doesn't know). A phone that reconnects gets each recent talk's latest status again (the last 20, from the last 10 minutes). All of it is optional in version 1: an older app sends no `talkId` and gets nothing new, and since an older hub refuses fields it doesn't know, the app sends `talkId` only once `welcome` has said `talkStatus: true`.

**A new session from the phone.** The phone's New session sheet is the Mac window's picker (`c`, "+ new session") over the socket. A hub that does it says so in `welcome` (`newSession: true`); the phone sends none of what follows to one that doesn't (an older hub refuses types and fields it doesn't know), and an older phone never sends them, so nothing changes for either.
- `get_new_session_options` is answered with `new_session_options`, what the window's picker and its agent question offer now: `projects` (where sessions have run, the folders the coding agent knows and the projects root's, newest first: `{ folder, name, detail }`, `detail` the folder it's in, `~` for home), `root` (where a new project is made, `~` for home; null when there's nowhere set), `harnesses` (the agents a session can start in, connected, the main one first: `{ id, name }`; an app that starts sessions in itself, while it's open, with `folders`: only its own projects, T3 Code; `canName`: whether one started in it can be named as it starts, missing from an older hub, which takes no name), and `selected` (the one preselected: the run's, the last used, or the setting) with `why` ("last used", "this run", "your setting"; missing for none).
- `new_session` asks for one: `requestId` (the phone's own id), exactly one of `folder` (one of the projects), `newProject` (a name: its folder is made in the root, or opened if it's there) or `scratch: true`, `harness` (an id from the list; missing: the preselected one), and the first message: `text`, or `talkId` for one said, or neither (it just opens), and optionally `name`: what to call it (one line; given on the agent's command line, or as a rename once its agent lists it, the same rename as `session_action` `rename`; for a harness whose `canName` is false, `failed` with why). It starts as the window's picker starts one: shown on the Mac, not said, and "Voice can" doesn't apply.
- **A first message said** is a press like any: `talk_start` with `dictate: true` and a `talkId`, `talk_audio`, `talk_end` (or `talk_cancel`). It holds the floor (`busy` as ever when someone else is talking: `error`, and `talk_status` `failed` for its `talkId`) and goes through the Mac's speech-to-text (`heard` while you talk, as any press), but its words are only written down, for the `new_session` that names its `talkId`: they never go to Kiko or a session by themselves, whatever they say. The phone sends `new_session` as the button goes up, before `talk_end`; the hub waits for the words. It has no other `talk_status`.
- `new_session_status` answers, for its `requestId`, to every connection of that phone: `heard` first for a said message (`text`: the words as they'll go in, the Mac's dictionary applied), then `started` (`sessionId`: the session as listed, a `starting-…` id while it starts, the T3 thread's own for T3 Code; `name`; `text`, the first message; `message`, what the Mac says of it: "Starting a Codex session in clipkit.") or `failed` (`message`, why, in the words the window would show: "That folder isn't one of your projects any more.", "OpenCode isn't connected. Connect it in Kiko's settings, under Agents.", "I can't open a new terminal on this computer.", "I didn't hear anything. Hold the button while you say it, or type it.").
- A session the hub opened is listed under its `starting-…` id until its agent lists it; from then on, under its own id with `startedAs` (the `starting-…` one), so a phone that opened its screen meanwhile follows it. A `watch` of a `starting-…` id gets an empty `history` at once (nothing has been said in it yet), then its agent's history once it's listed, still under the `starting-…` id it watched, live from there; a `watch` of its own id from then on gets the same under that id.

All of it is an optional addition to version 1.

**Background work, apart from working.** A session is `working` only while it's in a turn (Claude Code: from your prompt to its Stop, as its hooks say), never just because something it started still runs: a background shell, a subagent. That comes as `background` on the session: `count` (how many run), `agents` (how many of them are subagents, the rest shells and monitors; missing from an older hub), and when some have shown no sign of life for 10 minutes or more (no output, no transcript written: one left over and stuck), `quiet` (how many) and `quietSince` (when the longest quiet one last did, ms since 1970). The phone shows the ones still running beside an idle session's status ("idle · 3 agents running", "idle · 2 tasks running"), and ones gone quiet quietly in its small print ("3 in background (quiet 8h)"), as the Mac's list does. Missing when nothing runs or the agent can't tell. Claude Code's background jobs say in their own file; a terminal (or desktop) session's transcript says when each task started (its tool's result) and ended (Claude Code's notice that it's done), and a session whose process `claude agents` lists as idle runs nothing, so an end never written can't keep a count up. An optional addition to version 1: an older app ignores it, and an older hub never sends it.

**The home screen's cards.** `sessions` also carries, per session, what the phone's home screen draws a session's card with (`protocol/examples/sessions.home.json`):
- `mark`: its harness's mark, one character, as the Mac's lists draw it before its name (`✻` Claude Code, `⌬` Codex, `⊤` T3 Code, `▣` OpenCode), in its agent's colour; `colorLight`: that colour for a light background (`color` is the dark one). Missing for an agent without them.
- `inApp`: whether it runs in Kiko's own window (its tmux: the Mac's list's INTERNAL) or outside it (EXTERNAL: a terminal, the desktop app, T3 Code). The phone groups its cards so, "In Kiko" and "External".
- `doing`: while it's `working`, what it's doing now ("Running pnpm test", "Editing app.ts"), worded as its history's `doing` (the newest step still running). The hub follows each working session's history for it (eight at most) only while some phone is in front (`presence` not `away`), and lets go once it stops working; a change goes out with the list. Missing between steps, when nothing is known (a Codex command is written down only once it's done: the phone says "Working…"), and when it isn't working.

All optional additions to version 1: an older app ignores them; with an older hub the phone draws a dot for the mark, lists the sessions together, and says "Working…".

**A session's screen (the chat).** All optional additions to version 1, so an older app and an older hub work with either side as before:
- **What each session is, for its screen:** `sessions` carries, per session, `speaker` (who answers in it, as the Mac's viewer heads its replies: "Claude", "Codex", "OpenCode", else its agent's name, "T3 Code"), `color` (its agent's, `#rrggbb` for a dark background, as the Mac's lists draw it; none for an agent without one) and `can` (`{ reply, interrupt, rename, close }`: what can be done to it from there, as the Mac's window menu offers it, `core/can.ts`).
- **Live:** `history` carries `doing` while the newest item's last step runs ("Running pnpm build", "Editing app.ts": what the session is doing now, worded on the Mac), and each step still running has `running: true` (only in the newest item; one interrupted before its result just loses it as the turn moves on). The history is still sent whole each time the transcript changes, so this comes within half a second of the step starting, not only when the turn ends. Only what an agent's own record says: a Codex command is mirrored once it's done, so its sessions show "Working…" with no step.
- **Edits:** a file edit's step carries `added`, `removed` and `diff` (a unified diff's lines, `-old`, `+new`, ` context`, `@@ -3,2 +3,3 @@` before each hunk, cut to 80 lines and 4000 characters with "… N more lines"): from Claude Code's structured patch once the edit is done, from the edit's own input until then. None where the record has no lines (a Codex change: only its counts), and only the newest edits' within 60,000 characters a history (it goes out whole as it changes, up to twice a second while a session works; the others keep their counts).
- **Paging (a window, deltas, pages by cursor, Show full):** a phone sends `watch { sessionId, paged: true, turns?, bytes? }` to a hub whose `welcome` says `paging` (an older hub refuses fields it doesn't know). Its `history` is then a window: the newest turns that fit `turns` and `bytes` (the hub's 30 turns and 64 KB when left out; at most 100 and 256 KB), starting at your words where it can (a turn whole: your words and what came of them; the newest turn alone too big starts part way), at least the newest item. Each item has an `id`: where it starts in its agent's record (the file's byte offset, `.1` for an entry's second item), so the same item has the same id in a window, a delta, a page and `full_text` (an agent with no file of its own, T3 Code, gets the hub's own, `t:…`). Long things are cut to a preview, with `cut: true` on the item (a message, past 2,000 characters) or the step (an output past 8 lines or 600 characters, a diff past 30 lines or 2,000 characters, a diff left out for the snapshot's diff budget): `show_full { sessionId, id, step? }` brings the rest as `full_text { sessionId, id, step?, text | detail, diff?, cut?, missing? }`, the item read again from the record with far more kept (60,000 characters of text, 400 lines or 24,000 characters of output, 800 lines or 48,000 characters of diff; `cut` past that, `missing` when it can't be found any more). `truncated` with `cursor` (the window's first item's id) says there's more before it: `history_before { sessionId, before, cursor, turns?, bytes? }` gets `history_page { …, cursor, items, more, next? }`, the newest turns before that item within the same budget, read back from where it starts in the file (not the whole file), previewed the same way; `next` (its first item's id) is the cursor for the page before it. A cursor the file doesn't have as a line's start any more (its record written again) is taken by time (`before`), as an older phone's. After the window, each change is a `history_delta { sessionId, after?, items, doing? }`: the phone keeps its items up to and including `after`'s, drops the rest and puts `items` after it; none, when only `doing` changed (nothing at all is sent when nothing it shows did). Typically the open fold's item again as its steps run, or a new message: a few hundred bytes to a few KB, where the whole history went each time before. An `after` the phone doesn't have: it sends `watch` again for a whole window. A whole `history` can come again (the window moved past everything the phone has); the phone keeps its earlier items when the new window's first item is among them. Examples: `watch.paged.json`, `history.paged.json`, `history_delta.json` (and `.doing`), `history_before.cursor.json`, `history_page.cursor.json`, `show_full.json`, `full_text.json` (and `.message`, `.missing`).
- **An older phone** (no `paged`) gets the whole history each time as before, without ids or `cut`, but never more than 128 KB a message (the oldest items left out, `truncated`; the newest always goes), and its pages by time likewise: before, a session with long outputs could send megabytes each time it changed.
- **What the phone loads, not the agent's context:** all of this is only what the phone is sent to show. The agent's own context window (what Claude Code or Codex keeps of the conversation, when it compacts) is the agent's, not Kiko's, and nothing here changes it.
- **Earlier messages:** `history_before { sessionId, before }`, `before` the `at` of the oldest item the phone has, is answered with `history_page { sessionId, before, items, more }`: the last 60 items that started before it, folded as the live history is, oldest first; `more` when there are earlier ones still (ask again before the first of these). The hub reads the end of the transcript, more of it each time, until it has the page (64 MB at most). An agent with no transcript of its own (T3 Code's threads are on its server) gives an empty page, `more: false`. `no_session` for one not running any more.
- **The menu and Interrupt:** `session_action { sessionId, action: interrupt | rename | close, name? }`, for what the session's `can` says, is done as the Mac's window menu does it (rename waits for a busy session to finish, as there; close asks Claude Code to exit, or ends it where it runs; Close needs the Mac's window open). `session_note { sessionId, text }` says what came of it, to that phone only ("Renamed pixie to docs.", "pixie is busy, so I'll rename it to docs when it finishes.", "I can't rename pixie from here."). `no_session` for one not running any more. The permission mode is only shown (the Mac has no way to change a running session's).
- **Always:** a `permission_prompt` that can be allowed for good (its agent offers "don't ask again", as the Mac's card does with `a`) carries `canAlways: true`; the phone may then answer `permission` with `decision: "always"`. Never for a trust question, questions, or a block allowed once.
- **Hear it again:** `hear { text, sessionId? }` has the hub read the words aloud through its usual speech (as a missed line's play button: on phones with `play_on_phone`, on the Mac with Play on this Mac), Markdown's marks out, a code block or table said to be there rather than read, a long one cut where a sentence ends.
- **Who sends what to whom:** `welcome` says `chat: true` when the hub takes `history_before`, `session_action` and `hear`; the phone sends them (and `always`, only for a prompt with `canAlways`) to no other: an older hub refuses a message it doesn't know. `welcome` says `paging: true` when it takes `watch`'s `paged`, `history_before`'s `cursor` and `show_full`; the phone sends them to no other. An older app ignores the new fields and messages.

**Audio is base64 inside JSON** unless both sides agree on Opus (below), so every message carries `v` and goes through the same schema. It's about 46 KB/s up while talking and 65 KB/s down while the Mac speaks: fine on Wi-Fi, a lot on cellular.

**Opus.** Both ways in binary WebSocket frames, ~18–21× less (about 2.5–3.3 KB/s at 24 kbps). How the two sides agree, so that any app works with any hub:
- `welcome` lists `codecs`, what the hub takes besides PCM (`["opus"]`). An older hub lists none; an older app ignores the field.
- An app that takes one of them, with its Compressed audio on (the default), says what it takes right after `welcome`: `audio_codecs` `{ codecs: ["opus"] }`. With Compressed audio off it says `{ codecs: [] }`. It never sends `audio_codecs` to a hub whose `welcome` lists no `codecs`: an older hub refuses a type it doesn't know. (Not in `hello`, for the same reason: an older hub refuses a `hello` with a field it doesn't know, as a phone to pair again.)
- The hub picks the first of the app's it has (Opus only when its own helper works), else PCM, and says it: `audio_codec` `{ codec }`, logged on the Mac (`phone_audio_codec`; `phone_connected` and `phone_disconnected` carry `codec` too). That codec holds both ways for the connection until the app says again (Compressed audio switched). The app sends a press in Opus from the first press that starts after `audio_codec` `opus`; the hub sends its voice in Opus from its next frame.
- Nothing said, or PCM picked: everything is exactly as before (`talk_audio` and `audio` in JSON). The hub takes `talk_audio` in JSON from an Opus connection too (a press that began before the pick, or one queued while connecting).

A binary frame is: byte 0 the protocol version (`1`), byte 1 its kind (`1`: the phone's talk audio, 16 kHz; `2`: the hub's voice, 24 kHz; `3`: the phone's hands-free audio, 16 kHz, above), then one or more Opus packets of 20 ms, each behind its length as 2 bytes, big-endian. Mono, 24 kbps both ways (VBR: speech at 16 kHz comes out nearer 19). The codec is Apple's (AudioToolbox, `kAudioFormatOpus`) on both sides; the packets are plain Opus, with no container.
- **Up:** the first frame of a press as soon as there's 20 ms (one packet), then one every ~100 ms (five packets): the phone's cadence, as in JSON. `talk_start`, `talk_end` and `talk_cancel` stay JSON. The hub decodes each press's frames in order (a decoder per press) and hands all of the PCM on before it ends the press, so nothing said is lost to the end overtaking it; what the phone sends meanwhile waits its turn. A binary frame from a connection that didn't agree on Opus, or one of the hub's kind, is answered with `error` `invalid`; one outside a press with `not_talking`, as `talk_audio` is.
- **Down:** one encoder for every phone on Opus. The first packet of each stretch of speech goes at once; then five to a frame, or what's ready once nothing more has come for 40 ms; once nothing has come for 250 ms the line is over, and what's short of a frame goes, padded with silence. `audio_stop` drops whatever wasn't sent of what it cut (a new encoder for the next line). Cues stay `audio` with `cue: true` in JSON.

Binary frames aren't in the schema (it describes the JSON messages), but they carry `v` in their first byte, and the hub checks their header and packet lengths. All of it is an addition to version 1: neither side sends anything new to a side that hasn't said it takes it.

**The phone's playback buffer.** The app doesn't play each chunk of the hub's voice the moment it comes (a late one was a gap mid-word): it waits for ~150 ms of a stretch (or that long since its first chunk, for a short line), grows towards 250 ms after a late chunk, and shrinks back after stretches that played clean (the iPhone app's `README.md`). `audio_stop` still cuts at once and empties it; the end of a line plays out whatever is buffered. Nothing in the protocol changes for it.

**The read-along (`voice_marks`).** While Kiko's voice plays on the phone, its Talking to Kiko screen shows the line in grey, each word turning white as it's said. The phone follows the voice by where its own player really is (the buffers it has played back), never a clock, so the network and the playback buffer don't matter; it needs to know which sample of which line it's playing, and when each word is said. Optional and backward compatible: an older app ignores `voice_marks` and `speak`'s `line`, and an app that gets none (an older hub, or Play on phone off) shows the line in one colour, as before. Added 2026-11-04.
- **Lines.** Each line said (a spoken reply, a line of the app's) has a number, `line`, counting up from a random start on each run of the Mac. Its words are its text as the phone shows it (the `announcement`'s), split on white space, by their place `i`; each word sent carries its `text` too, so the phone checks it against the line on screen and shows the line in one colour if they don't match.
- **Where the voice is (`sync`).** `{ line, sync: { at, offset } }`: the next voice audio this connection gets, `offset` samples (24 kHz) into it, is sample `at` of the line. Sent just before that audio, in order with it, so it's exact whatever the codec: at each line's start, and again whenever what comes doesn't carry on from what came before (a gap in what was sent, an Opus packet padded with silence at a pause, a phone that came mid-line, a mute's end). On PCM `offset` is 0; on Opus it's where in the next frame the line starts (a frame can end one line and start the next). The phone counts what it's played since its last cut (`audio_stop`, a press): sample `at` + what it has played since the mark is where it is.
  ```json
  { "v": 1, "type": "voice_marks", "line": 4127, "sync": { "at": 0, "offset": 0 } }
  { "v": 1, "type": "voice_marks", "line": 4128, "sync": { "at": 0, "offset": 312 } }
  ```
- **When each word is said (`words`).** `{ line, words: [{ i, text, start, end }], exact }`, `start` and `end` in samples from the line's start, sent as they're known (a few messages a line; before or after the audio they're about). The phone turns a word white once the voice reaches its `start`; a word with no time (an emoji, a word the voice said differently) turns white with the next one that has one. How each voice gives them:
  - **Cartesia** says when each word is said (`add_timestamps`): `exact: true`. The Mac matches the words it says (respelled: "engine-x") back to the words on screen ("nginx") letter by letter, so a word respelled into several ("kiko dot S H") is still the one word.
    ```json
    { "v": 1, "type": "voice_marks", "line": 4127, "exact": true,
      "words": [ { "i": 0, "text": "Restarted", "start": 1440, "end": 13680 }, { "i": 1, "text": "nginx", "start": 14160, "end": 27840 },
                 { "i": 2, "text": "on", "start": 28320, "end": 33600 }, { "i": 3, "text": "kiko.sh.", "start": 34080, "end": 69600 } ] }
    ```
  - **Pocket** says nothing of its words, but is asked for one sentence at a time, so each sentence's audio is known exactly, start to end; within it the Mac estimates each word from the audio (the speech found by its loudness, its pauses tied to the commas and full stops, the time between shared by the words' lengths) and gives each a little late (~100 ms) rather than early: `exact: false`. Sent as each sentence's audio is all there (Pocket makes it ~10× faster than it's said, so well before it's heard). Every sentence starts from its own audio, so an error never carries on to the next. How close it gets is measured by the Mac's scripts/perf/readalong-pocket.ts.
  - **The macOS voice** is said by the phone itself (`speak`), whose synthesizer says which part of the text it's saying: the words carry `from` and `to` instead, UTF-16 offsets into the `speak` text that follows (respelled, so "nginx" is `engine-x` there), and `speak` carries the `line`. Exact.
    ```json
    { "v": 1, "type": "voice_marks", "line": 4130, "exact": true,
      "words": [ { "i": 0, "text": "Restarted", "from": 0, "to": 9 }, { "i": 1, "text": "nginx", "from": 10, "to": 18 },
                 { "i": 2, "text": "on", "from": 19, "to": 21 }, { "i": 3, "text": "kiko.sh.", "from": 22, "to": 35 } ] }
    { "v": 1, "type": "speak", "text": "Restarted engine-x on kiko dot S H.", "line": 4130 }
    ```
- **When the Mac plays the voice** (Play on phone off, or the app away), the phone gets no audio and no `voice_marks`: the line in one colour.
- **When anything doesn't fit** (no `sync` for a line, words that don't match what's on screen, a position that's gone back), the phone shows the whole line in one colour rather than a guess.

**Hands-free from the phone.** An optional addition to version 1: the phone's Hands-free setting (off by default; push-to-talk stays the default) works as the Mac's hands-free does, with the Mac doing all of the listening, so the two behave the same and can't drift apart.
- **Who sends what:** `welcome` says `handsFree: true` when the hub listens for phones; the phone sends `hands_free` and its audio to no other hub (an older one refuses a message it doesn't know), and an older app never sends them.
- **On and off:** `hands_free { on: true }` once the setting is on and the app is in front: right after `welcome` (after `presence`), and when it's switched on or the app comes back to the front; `{ on: false }` when it's switched off or the app goes to the background or the phone is locked (foreground only). The hub also stops it when the connection says `presence` `away` or closes, and doesn't start it for a connection that's away (it answers `on: false`). One listener per phone: a newer connection of the same phone takes it over.
- **The Mac listens:** a listener of its own for each phone, exactly the Mac's hands-free (`src/core/hands-free.ts`, `wake.ts`): Apple's on-device speech-to-text on what the phone sends, the spoken name at the start of a phrase wakes it, the request ends after ~1.5 s of quiet, the name alone waits 4 s, a session's reply window takes a real reply without the name in a quiet room, and nothing is heard while the app speaks or plays a cue (plus 400 ms, or until the phone says its mic is back: below). Phrases without the name are dropped and only counted. What it takes is dealt with as that phone's press would be (routing, cues and replies on the phone with Play on phone), and the Mac's own hands-free goes on beside it, each holding the floor in turn.
- **The phone's audio:** the app keeps its mic open while it listens and sends only while someone is talking: a level gate on the phone (above the room's own noise), with ~400 ms before the voice sent first so the name isn't clipped, and ~700 ms after it. Nothing while the phone plays Kiko's voice or a cue (and ~300 ms after), unless the hub says it's woken. `listen_audio` in JSON, or on Opus binary frames of kind 3 (as a press's: five 20 ms packets each, the moment before the voice in one). The hub drops it while the phone's listener isn't on. A press (the talk button) takes over at once, as the Mac's key does: its audio goes as the press's, and listening goes on after it.
- **The phone's playback:** to a hub whose `welcome` says `handsFreePlaying`, the phone says when it plays Kiko's voice or a cue (`hands_free_playing { playing: true }`) and when its mic is back (`playing: false`: the playback over, and its own ~300 ms after, during which it sends nothing), on each change only, while its Hands-free is on. The phone hears the hub's voice later than the hub sends it (the network, its playback buffer, which can grow), so the hub doesn't guess: once the phone said it played in a stretch of the app speaking, that phone's listener is deaf until the phone says its mic is back, rather than until 400 ms after the hub finished sending, and the reply window's quiet room (a voice already there as the mic came back) is judged from that moment. A phone whose `playing: false` never comes is heard again 5 s after the app stopped. Without it (an older phone or hub) the hub's own timing stands. Optional and backward compatible: an older hub refuses the message, so the phone sends it to no other hub.
- **What the phone shows:** `hands_free_state { on, by, name?, talkId?, error? }`, to that phone only: `on` while it listens ("Listening for Kiko"), `by` while it's woken (`name`, or `reply` in a window): the phone then sends everything its mic hears, through the cue, until `by` is null again. `talkId` comes as the words are taken: the phone adds a talk for it, and `talk_status` follows as for a press. `error` when the Mac can't listen (Apple's speech-to-text isn't there).
- **Data:** nothing at all while the room is quiet; ~3 KB/s (Opus) or ~32 KB/s (PCM) while someone talks near the phone. Well within the relay's limits (below): ~10 messages a second at most.

**Signing the Mac in from the phone.** When the Mac is simply signed out, the phone that holds the problem can fix it: its approval page opens in the phone's browser, and approving there signs **the Mac** in (the device code flow, RFC 8628, that `kiko login` runs; the phone itself never signs in to anything and keeps no account).

2. A phone that can show it answers `sign_in` within 3 seconds; the connection stays open (no 4014), and the authentication timer is off. One that doesn't is let go after 3 seconds with 4014, as before.
3. The button opens `url` in the browser, outside the app, and the phone sends `sign_in_opened`; the Mac shows "Signing in from your iPhone… Check the code there matches: WDJB-MJHT". 5. The phone connects again at once (no backoff), and is let in, or refused with the plan's own reason (signed in, but no plan).

The phone going (the app to the background, the network) ends nothing on the Mac: the sign-in runs on until its code runs out, and the phone's next `hello` gets the same code back. ## Encrypted at home

Every direct connection (the home network's address, Bonjour's name, Tailscale's) is encrypted end to end like the relay's, with the same tunnel: one handshake when the socket opens, then every frame either way one sealed record (Relay, below: records, their types, pings and close, are exactly as there). Nothing goes in the clear after the handshake but a refusal (below). The Mac's side is `src/remote/local.ts` (with `cpace.ts` and `tunnel.ts`), the phone's `Kiko/Relay/` in the iPhone app; the bytes are in `relay-vectors.json`, `local`.

**The keys.** The Mac's static X25519 key (the relay's: Relay, The keys), made the first time Phone is on, kept in the Keychain (with the Keychain off, `KIKO_KEYCHAIN=off`, in `device-key.json` next to `phones.json`, readable only by you). Each phone's own static X25519 key, kept in its Keychain. Each side learns the other's at pairing, inside the encrypted channel: the phone's in `pair` (`key`), the Mac's in `welcome` (`macKey`, sent on every welcome). The Mac keeps the phone's with it in `phones.json` (`relayKey`, the name it had when only the relay used it).

**The first frame** is binary, and its first byte says how the phone opens the tunnel:

| byte | name | then | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0x01` | keys | Noise IK message 1 (96 bytes), exactly as Relay's handshake but with the prologue `"kiko local v1"` (13 bytes ASCII), so a handshake made for one way in never passes on the other | a paired phone with its keys: every connection after the first |
| `0x02` | code | the phone's CPace share `Ya` (32 bytes), with the pairing code | pairing |
| `0x03` | token | the phone's id (16 bytes), then `Ya` (32), with its token | a phone paired before this, which has a token but no keys: once |
| `0x04` | qr | the QR code's id (16 bytes), then `Ya` (32), with its secret | pairing from the QR code (below) |

The Mac answers a keys handshake with message 2 (48 bytes) as through the relay, and a code or token one with its share `Yb` (32 bytes, binary). Then records, the phone's first being `hello` or `pair` as ever.

**CPace** (CFRG's balanced PAKE, `draft-irtf-cfrg-cpace` revision 21, suite CPACE-X25519-SHA512, initiator-responder: the phone is A, the Mac B; `cpaceDraft` in the vectors is the draft's own B.1, to check an implementation against first): `sid` empty, `ADb` empty.
- **code:** PRS = the code's 6 ASCII digits, CI = `"kiko pair v1"`, ADa empty.
- **token:** PRS = SHA-256 of the token's UTF-8 (32 bytes, the hash the Mac keeps), CI = `"kiko token v1"`, ADa = the phone's id = the first 16 bytes of SHA-256(`"kiko phone id v1"` || 0x00 || PRS), sent in the clear in the first frame so the Mac knows whose hash to use (it gives nothing of the hash away).
- `g` = Elligator 2 (RFC 9380's, Z = 2) of the first 32 bytes of SHA-512(generator string), as the draft has it; `Ya` = X25519(`ya`, `g`) for a random 32-byte `ya`, `Yb` likewise; K = X25519(own scalar, other share), all-zero refused; ISK = SHA-512(lv_cat(`"CPace255_ISK"`, sid, K) || lv_cat(Ya, ADa) || lv_cat(Yb, ADb)).
- **The records' keys:** (`k1`, `k2`) = HKDF(salt `"kiko local v1"`, ISK), Noise's HKDF as in Relay (RFC 5869 HKDF-SHA256, no info, 64 bytes split): `k1` seals what the phone sends, `k2` what the Mac sends, counters from 0.
- **Confirmed by the first record each way:** the Mac's doesn't go until the phone's first record has opened under `k1`. One that doesn't open is a wrong code (counted as `pair` with a wrong code always was: five close pairing, `too_many_attempts`) or a token that isn't the one the Mac has; refused in the clear, and the socket closes.

**Who may sign in on it** (the hub decides, as through the relay):
- **code:** `pair` only, with `key`: the Mac keeps it. `pair` on any other handshake is refused (`pairing_closed`), so the code never crosses the network, even sealed.
- **qr:** `pair` only, with the QR code's secret as `code` and with `key`, for that code (below).
- **keys:** the phone whose key it is: `hello` with another phone's token is `unauthorized`. A key no phone has: only a phone the Mac has no key for yet may take it (its token says which); one with another key is `unauthorized` (pair again).
- **token:** that phone's `hello` only, with `key`, which the Mac keeps; `welcome`'s `macKey` the phone keeps. From then on it opens with keys. Once the Mac has a phone's key, a token handshake for it is refused (`unknown_phone`): a token seen on the network before this (when it went in the clear) no longer lets anyone in. A phone that lost its keys, or whose Mac made its keys again, pairs again.

**Refusals before the tunnel** are the only text frames: an `error` as above, in the clear, then the socket closes (4001). `version` (an app from before this, whose `hello` or `pair` came in the clear and wasn't read: "Update Kiko on this iPhone…"; or a first byte the Mac doesn't know), `pairing_closed`, `too_many_attempts`, `bad_code`, `qr_used`, `qr_expired`, `stale_key` (a keys handshake to another key than this Mac's: its keys were made again, or another Mac answers at that address; the phone drops the Mac's key and opens with its token next) and `unknown_phone`. None says anything secret, and since anyone on the network could send one, the phone shows it and changes nothing for it but `stale_key`'s Mac key; `unauthorized`, which makes it forget its pairing, counts only inside the tunnel. A Mac from before this answers the handshake with `unauthorized` in the clear (it reads a binary first frame as a bad first message): the phone says to update Kiko on the Mac. A text frame once the tunnel is open, or a record that doesn't open, ends it (the phone dials again).

**Pings:** the hub pings through the tunnel as through the relay (the phone answers), and the phone pings the WebSocket itself as it always did at home.

**What it protects, and from whom.** Someone on the network (the Wi-Fi, a café's, the path to a Tailscale address) sees only that a phone connects, when frames go and how big they are; never a word, a sound, a conversation, the token, the code or a key. Watching a pairing gives them nothing: CPace leaves nothing to test codes against offline, and the session keys need a scalar only the two ends had. Someone who can also change traffic while you pair gets one guess at the code per attempt (a wrong one fails and counts; five close pairing), and none afterwards: every later connection is Noise IK between the two static keys, so a stranger can't pass for the Mac or the phone. What it doesn't hide: that it's Kiko (port, Bonjour, the frames' sizes), and it can't stop someone from cutting the connection. Someone who can read the Mac's `phones.json` has each phone's token hash, which a token handshake uses: they could pass for the Mac to a phone that hasn't opened with keys yet (once that phone has, the hash opens nothing); they still can't sign in as one.

**The QR code, from whom.** On the network it's as the code is, and stronger: the secret has 128 bits, so someone in the middle of a QR pairing has no guess worth making, and a phone scanning it can't be paired with a Mac that only pretends to be this one (an impostor on the Wi-Fi answering the code's addresses doesn't have the secret, so its first record never opens; and `welcome` must carry the key the code showed). What the code is: a bearer pass, for 5 minutes, for one phone. Whoever sees it on the screen (over a shoulder, a photo, a screen share or recording, someone at the Mac) can pair a phone of theirs with it before yours does; the Mac then says that phone's name as paired ("✓ Alex's iPhone paired") and lists it under Paired phones, where Forget lets it go, and your phone hears `qr_used`. So the code is shown only while you ask for it, closes as soon as a phone pairs, after 5 minutes or with Esc, and showing it again makes a new one. It can't be used later: a photo of a code that paired, expired or was closed opens nothing. With the relay on it also works from away, through the relay (Relay, Pairing through the relay), for the same 5 minutes and the same one phone: the relay sees only a credential drawn from the secret, which pairs nothing by itself. Nothing in it is a key to anything once it's spent: the Mac's public key, addresses and room aren't secrets. What it doesn't change: anyone who can use the Mac itself can open Settings and pair a phone.

**Pairing with a QR code.** An optional addition to version 1, beside the typed code (which goes on as above). In Kiko on the Mac, Settings → Phone → Pair a phone draws a QR code in the terminal, with the 6-digit code beneath it for a phone without a camera. The phone's app scans it (or the iPhone's Camera does and offers to open the app) and pairs at once, with nothing typed.
- **The code says** one URL, in the app's own URL scheme (its bundle identifier, `sh.kiko.ios`: the app registers it from its identifier, so renaming the app moves both), every value in its query, percent-encoded: `sh.kiko.ios://pair?v=1&n=<name>&h=<host>,<host>…&p=<port>&s=<secret>&k=<mac key>[&r=<relay url>&m=<room>]`. `v` the link's own version (1; an app that doesn't know it says to update the app); `n` the hub's name; `h` where to dial: up to three of the home network's addresses, then Tailscale's address and MagicDNS name when the Mac has them; `p` the port; `s` the one-time secret, 16 random bytes (128 bits), base64url without padding (22 characters); `k` the Mac's static X25519 public key, base64url (43); with the relay on, `r` its url and `m` the Mac's room there. About 180 bytes: a version 8 symbol (49 modules a side) at level L, version 10 with the relay; `relay-vectors.json`, `local.qr`, has a URL and its symbol, module for module.
- **Pairing** is CPace as for the code, with the secret: first byte `0x04`, then the code's **id**, the first 16 bytes of SHA-256(`"kiko qr id v1"` || 0x00 || secret), then `Ya`. PRS = the secret's 16 bytes, CI = `"kiko qr v1"`, ADa = the id (in the clear, so the Mac knows which code; it gives nothing of the secret away). The keys and the first record each way as for the code. The phone's first record is `pair` with `code` = the secret as the URL has it, and `key`; the Mac answers `paired` and `welcome` as ever. The phone dials every host in `h`, staggered as for a paired Mac, then, with `r` and `m`, the relay (Pairing through the relay), and the first to answer wins.
- **The phone checks it reached this Mac:** `welcome`'s `macKey` must be `k` (compared in constant time); any other and the phone drops the pairing it just made and says so. The phone keeps `k` as the Mac's key, and every later connection is Noise IK to it, which only the Mac that holds its private key can answer. With `r` and `m`, `relay_paired` (sent at once, as after any pairing at home, to a hub whose welcome says `relay`) must name the same room, or the phone keeps nothing of it.
- **One use, five minutes:** a code works for one phone, for 5 minutes from when it's shown, and stops when its screen closes on the Mac (Esc, or a phone paired, either way). Showing a new one stops the one before. A code that paired a phone already is refused with `qr_used`, one that has expired or was closed (or that this Mac doesn't know: shown before it restarted) with `qr_expired`: in the clear on the handshake (its id says which code), or as the answer to `pair`; through the relay, by the relay or with the channel's close (below). A first record that doesn't open (a secret that isn't the code's) is `bad_code`; it isn't counted against the typed code. Pairing either way stops the other: one phone per pairing screen.
- **Never written down:** the secret (and the URL, and the symbol) are only in the Mac's memory and on its screen; the Mac logs that a phone paired and how (`phone_paired`, `how: "qr"`), never the secret or the id.

**Phones paired before this** keep working without pairing again: their first connection opens with their token (or, set up for the relay, with their keys and `relay_paired`'s `macKey`) and they move to keys. Tokens that went in the clear before this could have been seen on the network; pairing again makes a new one that never did.

## Relay

How a phone reaches its Mac from anywhere with nothing to set up (Kiko Pro). The Mac and the phone each connect out to the relay over a WebSocket (`wss://`), and it passes what one sends to the other. Inside, the phone and the Mac run an end-to-end encrypted tunnel keyed at pairing, so the relay never sees a token, a word, a sound or a key: only that a phone joined a room, when frames go, and how big they are. Through the tunnel it's the same protocol as above, unchanged (hello and its token first, then everything else). All of it is an optional addition to version 1: a hub says `relay: true` in `welcome` only with the relay on, an app sends `relay_pair` only to such a hub, and the relay's entry in `addresses` goes only to a phone set up for it. The relay itself is `relay/` in this repo (a Cloudflare Worker, one Durable Object per Mac); the Mac's side is `src/remote/relay-client.ts` and `tunnel.ts`. Every byte below is in `relay-vectors.json`, made from fixed inputs (each private key is SHA-256 of `kiko vector <label>`): build the phone's side from this section and check it against those.

Notation: `||` joins bytes; base64 is standard base64 with padding; hex is lowercase; a room is 32 hex characters.

**The keys.** The Mac has two, made the first time the relay is on and kept in its Keychain: an Ed25519 signing key (the Mac's identity on the relay: no account) and a static X25519 key (the tunnel's). Its **room** is the first 16 bytes of SHA-256 of its Ed25519 public key, in hex. The phone has one, a static X25519 key (CryptoKit's `Curve25519.KeyAgreement.PrivateKey`), made when it sets up the relay and kept in its Keychain.

**Setting up, on the home network.** Pairing stays as it is (the code, at home). Then, to a hub whose `welcome` says `relay`, the phone sends `relay_pair` `{ key }`, its static public key (32 bytes, base64; `rawRepresentation`). The hub keeps it with the phone (`phones.json`) and answers `relay_paired` `{ url, room, macKey, signingKey, credential, addresses }`: the relay, the Mac's room, its static X25519 public key and its Ed25519 public key (the phone may check that the room is the one named after it), and a **relay credential**, 32 random bytes sent only this once. The phone keeps them all (the credential in its Keychain). The hub keeps only the credential's hash, SHA-256 of its 32 raw bytes in hex, and registers it with its room. A later `relay_pair` replaces the key and the credential; forgetting the phone on the Mac drops both, and the relay stops letting it in. From then on `welcome`'s `addresses` ends with `{ kind: "relay", host, port, url, room }` (`host` and `port` from the url, for an app that reads only those), tried last. `relay_pair` and `relay_paired` go inside the tunnel at home (Encrypted at home), so nobody on the network sees or swaps them; the phone uses the same static key at home and through the relay, and the Mac's static key is the same as welcome's `macKey`.

**The phone and the relay.** A WebSocket to `<url>/v1/phone/<room>`. Its first message, a text frame, within 10 seconds: `{"type":"join","credential":"<base64>"}`. The relay answers `{"type":"joined"}`, or `{"type":"error","code":"<why>"}` and closes with that why's code:

| code | close | why |
|---|---|---|
| `replaced` | 4000 | (the Mac's socket) the same Mac signed in again elsewhere |
| `unauthorized` | 4001 | a credential the Mac didn't register (forgotten: pair again at home), or (the Mac) a bad signature |
| `mac_gone` | 4002 | the Mac's socket went (it reconnects by itself: try again in a moment) |
| `mac_offline` | 4003 | the Mac isn't connected to the relay (asleep, off, no network, Phone off) |
| `too_many` | 4004 | ten phones are in the room already |
| `timeout` | 4005 | no `join` (or the Mac's `auth`) within 10 seconds |
| `bad_message` | 4006 | something other than what's expected |
| (`ticket_required`) | 4007 | retired, never sent (the account check's placeholder before the pass) |
| `too_big` | 4008 | a message bigger than the relay takes (limits, below) |
| `rate_limited` | 4009 | too many messages too fast from this socket |
| `over_quota` | 4010 | the room passed what it may pass in a while or in a day; the error has `retryIn`, the seconds until it may again |
| `idle` | 4011 | (a signed-in or joined socket) nothing from it for 90 seconds: taken to be gone half-open |
| `qr_used` | 4012 | (pairing by a QR code) the code let a phone in already, or paired one (below, Pairing through the relay) |
| `qr_expired` | 4013 | (pairing by a QR code) the code has run out, was closed on the Mac, or lets no more phones in |
| `plan_required` | 4014 | the relay requires a pass and the Mac hasn't shown a good one: Kiko Pro or the free trial is needed (below, The pass). To the Mac's own sign-in too, with `pass`: its status |
| `too_many_rooms` | 4016 | (the Mac's sign-in only) the pass's account has opened as many rooms as it may in a day (below, Limits) |

After `joined`, every binary message either way is one tunnel frame (below), and the relay passes it as it is. Either side may send the text `ping` at any time; the relay answers `pong` itself (keepalive; it costs the room nothing). Other text from a joined phone is ignored.

**The Mac and the relay.** A WebSocket to `<url>/v1/mac/<room>`. The relay sends `{"type":"challenge","nonce":"<base64 of 32 random bytes>"}`; the Mac answers `{"type":"auth","key":"<its Ed25519 public key, base64>","sig":"<base64>","pass":"<its relay pass>"}` (`pass`: below, The pass; required with `requirePass` on), its Ed25519 signature over `"kiko relay mac v1" || 0x00 || room || 0x00 || nonce` (the strings ASCII, the nonce raw). The Mac also shows its pass in the upgrade, as the header `Kiko-Relay-Pass: <pass>` (since 2026-11-19): a Mac with a good pass for the room there waits outside the room's budgets for waiting sockets (below, Limits), one such socket per account (a newer one replaces it, `timeout`). The relay checks that the room is the one named after the key and the signature, and with `requirePass` on that the pass (the one in `auth`, else the upgrade's) is good for this room and not revoked, else it says `{"type":"error","code":"plan_required","pass":"<status>"}` and closes (4014), and the Mac signed in before stays; then `{"type":"ready","pairing":true,"pass":"<status>","passExpiresAt":<ms>,"passRequired":<bool>}` (`pairing`: it takes a pairing QR code's way in, below; a relay from before that says `{"type":"ready"}`, and the Mac sends it no `pairing`; `pass`, `passExpiresAt` and `passRequired`: below, The pass, absent from a relay from before it). A Mac that signs in replaces one signed in before (4000), and the phones in the room go (4002). Then the Mac sends `{"type":"phones","credentials":["<hash>", …]}` (32 at most), again whenever one is set up or forgotten; a phone whose credential leaves the list is closed (4001). The relay tells the Mac `{"type":"open","channel":<n>}` as a phone joins and `{"type":"closed","channel":<n>}` as it goes; between them, binary messages carry a phone's frames behind its channel, 4 bytes big-endian, both ways. `{"type":"close","channel":<n>}` from the Mac closes that phone's socket; with `"code":"qr_used"` or `"qr_expired"` (a pairing channel's, below) the relay says that to the phone first, and closes with its code.

**Limits.** Anyone can make a key, so anyone can open a room: the relay keeps each to what Kiko really sends, well above it, so it can't be used as a free tunnel for anything else. Each is a close code above, with its error just before it (or an HTTP status on the upgrade, before any socket). Two kinds: the protocol's, which the Mac and the phone build on, are fixed and given here; the abuse limits (rate, a room's bytes, sockets waiting, new rooms) are the relay's own, set on the deployed relay and not published (`relay/README.md`, "Limits"), always far above what Kiko sends, so an app never aims at a number: it handles the close. What the app should show for each:

| limit | what passes | over it | the app |
|---|---|---|---|
| message size | text up to 16 KiB; binary up to 1 MiB + 1 KiB (a tunnel record holds 1 MiB of plaintext, +17 bytes sealed, +4 of channel) | `too_big` 4008 | a bug, not the user's doing: connect again; say nothing unless it repeats |
| rate, per socket | messages a second, with a burst; the Mac's is bigger (it speaks for up to ten phones). `ping` isn't counted | `rate_limited` 4009 | connect again after a few seconds (back off) |
| a room's bytes | a budget per window (minutes) and per day, binary frames both ways together, every phone in the room counted (each window starts with its first byte) | `over_quota` 4010 `{ retryIn }`: every phone goes, joins are refused until then, the Mac stays signed in (told once, the same error) and its frames are dropped | "The relay has passed its limit for now: try again in N minutes" (or at home); don't retry before `retryIn` |
| signing in | `join` (or `auth`) within 10 seconds, and nothing else first | `timeout` 4005, `bad_message` 4006 | connect again |
| sockets waiting | each role its own budget of sockets waiting in a room (phones, and Macs without a good pass in the upgrade), so strangers waiting as phones never keep the Mac out; a role's budget full, its oldest waiting socket goes for the new one; one address (IPv6: its /64) holds only so many waiting sockets in a room and opens new ones only so fast; a Mac with a good pass for the room in the upgrade waits outside all of this | `timeout` 4005 to the oldest; HTTP 429 at the upgrade for an address over its share | connect again (back off) |
| half-open | anything (a frame, or a `ping` answered by the relay) at least every 90 seconds: the Mac pings every 10 (and lets go of a relay whose `pong` hasn't come within 5), and a phone answers the hub's pings every 15 | `idle` 4011 | connect again |
| new rooms | counted in shared state, before any room is made: a Mac's upgrade with a good pass, against the pass's account (so many distinct rooms a day); without, against its address (so many new rooms an hour). A room already counted always passes | HTTP 429 at the Mac's upgrade (503: try again soon), or `too_many_rooms` 4016 at sign-in for a pass shown only in `auth` (the phone never sees either) | the Mac backs off |
| pairing by a QR code | one phone per registration of the code's credential, for the time the Mac gives and never more than 5 minutes from its first registration, 5 phones over its life at most (below) | `qr_used` 4012, `qr_expired` 4013 | what the Mac's own refusal says (show a new code) |

Real traffic, for scale: Opus is about 3 KB/s each way while someone talks or the Mac speaks; with Compressed audio off, PCM in JSON is about 46 KB/s up and 65 KB/s down; text and cards are small, and the largest messages (a `history_page`, the list of projects) are tens to a few hundred KB. A session's screen: opening one is a window of about 35 KB (64 KB at most; it was the last 60 items, 40 to 95 KB, a few MB at worst), and while it works each change is a delta of about 1.5 KB where the whole history (about 50 KB) went each time, up to twice a second. A phone listening to PCM for ten minutes straight is ~40 MB, well inside a room's budget.

**The tunnel's handshake** is Noise IK, exactly as the Noise Protocol Framework (revision 34) defines `Noise_IK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256`, with the phone as initiator and the Mac as responder, so any Noise implementation that passes its test vectors works (ours passes cacophony's). The prologue is `"kiko relay v1" || 0x00 || room` (46 bytes, ASCII), and the Mac's static key is the pre-message. Both handshake payloads are empty in this version (a side ignores one it gets). Spelled out, with CryptoKit's names:
- `HKDF(ck, ikm)`: RFC 5869 HKDF-SHA256 with `ck` as the salt, no info, 64 bytes (`HKDF<SHA256>.deriveKey(inputKeyMaterial: SymmetricKey(data: ikm), salt: ck, outputByteCount: 64)`): the first 32 are the new `ck`, the last 32 the key `k`, whose nonce counter starts at 0.
- `ENCRYPT(k, n, ad, p)`: ChaCha20-Poly1305 (`ChaChaPoly.seal(p, using: k, nonce: N(n), authenticating: ad)`), the ciphertext then its 16-byte tag; `N(n)` is 4 zero bytes then `n` as 8 bytes little-endian. `DH` is X25519 (`sharedSecretFromKeyAgreement`, raw); an all-zero result fails (CryptoKit's does).
- Start: `h` = the 32 ASCII bytes `Noise_IK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256`; `ck` = `h`; `h` = SHA-256(`h` || prologue); `h` = SHA-256(`h` || the Mac's static public key).
- **Message 1, phone → Mac,** one binary message, 97 bytes: `0x01` (the tunnel's version, not part of Noise) || `e` || `s` || `p`. The phone makes an ephemeral key pair; `e` is its public key (32); `h` = SHA-256(`h` || `e`); (`ck`, `k`) = HKDF(`ck`, DH(ephemeral, Mac static)); `s` = ENCRYPT(`k`, 0, `h`, the phone's static public key) (48); `h` = SHA-256(`h` || `s`); (`ck`, `k`) = HKDF(`ck`, DH(phone static, Mac static)); `p` = ENCRYPT(`k`, 0, `h`, empty) (16); `h` = SHA-256(`h` || `p`).
- The Mac reads it the same way, decrypting `s` to learn which phone it is: a key it doesn't hold for a paired phone (or a message that doesn't open, or a version it doesn't speak) and it lets the channel go, with nothing said.
- **Message 2, Mac → phone,** 48 bytes: `e` || `p`. The Mac makes an ephemeral pair; `h` = SHA-256(`h` || `e`); (`ck`, `k`) = HKDF(`ck`, DH(Mac ephemeral, phone ephemeral)); (`ck`, `k`) = HKDF(`ck`, DH(Mac ephemeral, phone static)); `p` = ENCRYPT(`k`, 0, `h`, empty); `h` = SHA-256(`h` || `p`). The phone does the same with DH(phone ephemeral, Mac ephemeral) and DH(phone static, Mac ephemeral); a `p` that doesn't open means it isn't the Mac it paired with: close.
- **Keys:** (`k1`, `k2`) = HKDF(`ck`, empty). `k1` seals what the phone sends, `k2` what the Mac sends, each with its own counter from 0. The final `h` is the same on both sides (`handshakeHash` in the vectors).

**Records.** Every binary message after message 2 is one record: ENCRYPT(that direction's key, its next counter, empty, `type || body`), the counter going up by one per record and never sent (the relay's WebSocket keeps them in order). A record that doesn't open under the counter expected (altered, replayed, reordered, dropped, or the wrong key) ends the tunnel: close the socket and connect again, with a new handshake. Records hold up to 1 MiB (more than Noise's own 65,535-byte messages, since each goes in a WebSocket message of its own); counters never wrap (the last value is never used), and nothing is rekeyed: a tunnel is one connection.

| type | body |
|---|---|
| 1 text | one JSON message of this protocol, UTF-8: what a text frame carries on a direct connection |
| 2 binary | one binary frame exactly as Opus (above) has it |
| 3 ping | anything up to 125 bytes; answer at once with a pong carrying the same body |
| 4 pong | the ping's body |
| 5 close | the hub closed this connection, as it would close a WebSocket: the code (2 bytes, big-endian), then the reason (UTF-8, 123 bytes at most); e.g. 4001 `unauthorized`. The relay then closes the phone's socket |

The hub pings each connection every 15 seconds, through the tunnel as on a direct socket, and lets go of one that didn't answer the last: the phone must answer pings. The phone may ping too (the hub answers). Through the relay, `pair` is refused (`pairing_closed`: pairing is at home, or by a QR code's own way in, below), so is `relay_pair` (`invalid`: a new credential would end the connection it came on; but not on a pairing channel), and `hello` must carry the token of the phone whose key did the handshake (another phone's: `unauthorized`).

**Pairing through the relay.** A phone that scans the Mac's pairing QR code (Encrypted at home, Pairing with a QR code) away from the Mac's network, on cellular or another Wi-Fi, pairs through the relay instead, with nothing more to set up and the same code: one phone, 5 minutes, the secret never anywhere but the screen and the two ends. An optional addition to version 1. The relay is tried after every address in `h`, as for a paired Mac (each starting when the one before fails or hasn't answered within a second): at home a direct one answers first.
- **The credential:** `C` = SHA-256(`"kiko qr relay v1"` || 0x00 || the secret), 32 bytes. While the code works, the Mac (with a relay that says `pairing` in `ready`) registers its hash, SHA-256(`C`) in hex like any credential's, with its room: `{"type":"pairing","credential":"<hash>","ttl":<seconds the code has left>}`. As the code stops working it says `{"type":"pairing","credential":null}`, with `"used":true` when it paired a phone (either way: at home or through the relay). A new code replaces the old one; the Mac registers its code again when it signs in again (a relay restart, a new connection).
- **The relay** keeps one such credential per room, for the `ttl` given and never more than 5 minutes from when that hash was first registered, and lets **one phone** in with it per registration: a `join` with `C` (exactly as with a phone's own credential) is answered `joined`, and the Mac is told `{"type":"open","channel":<n>,"pairing":true}`. The credential is then spent: the next phone with it hears `qr_used` (4012); once the Mac said it's over, `qr_used` again when it paired a phone, else `qr_expired` (4013), as also past its time or after 5 phones over its life; a credential no code of the Mac's has is `unauthorized`. The Mac offline is `mac_offline`, and spends nothing. A phone in on it isn't one of the Mac's phones: the Mac's list (`phones`) changing doesn't touch it.
- **The channel** carries, first, exactly what the phone sends at home: `0x04`, the code's id, `Ya` (49 bytes, as one binary message), and the Mac answers `Yb`; then records under the CPace keys, the phone's first being `pair` with the secret, as at home. The Mac takes nothing else first on a pairing channel. A code this Mac knows as used or expired (one shown before it restarted, or a phone that came as the code was used another way) is refused with the channel's close: `{"type":"close","channel":<n>,"code":"qr_used"}` (or `qr_expired`), which the relay says to the phone. A first record that doesn't open (not the code's secret) closes the channel with nothing said. A channel that ends without the code being used, while it still works, has the Mac register the code again (the relay let one phone in for it), so a dropped connection or a stranger doesn't spend it.
- **Then, on that same connection:** `paired` and `welcome` as at home (the phone checks `macKey` is `k`), and the phone's `relay_pair`, which the Mac takes there (unlike any other connection through the relay): the phone's credential is registered as any, and this connection, in on the code's way, stays. From then on the phone comes through the relay as any phone set up for it, with its own credential and Noise IK.
- **Old and new together:** a Mac from before this registers nothing, and a relay from before this says no `pairing` in `ready` and is sent nothing: either way the phone's `join` is `unauthorized`, and it says it couldn't pair through the relay (join the Mac's network and scan again). An app from before this never tries it. Nothing changes for anything else on the relay.
- **What the relay learns:** that a phone joined to pair, when, and how big the frames are, as for any phone; `C` and its hash, which give nothing of the secret (128 bits, through SHA-256): it can't pair with them, since pairing is CPace with the secret, nor answer for the Mac, since the phone checks `welcome`'s `macKey` against the code's `k`. A relay that misbehaves can let a phone in more than once or not at all, or keep it out: the Mac still pairs one phone per code (`qr_used` for the rest), and the pairing still needs the secret. Someone who saw the code on the screen can pair through the relay just as on the Wi-Fi, as before: the Mac says which phone paired and lists it.

**The pass (Kiko Pro or the free trial).** Decided 2026-11-04: Kiko's iPhone app, at home and away, comes with Kiko Pro or the free trial; pairing and using the phone need one of them. Through the relay, the relay checks it, with the **relay pass**: a compact JWS our account server signs for the Mac's account (`POST https://api.kiko.sh/v1/relay/pass`, with the Mac's account session and its room, `{"room":"<room id>"}`; the account API's "The relay pass"), EdDSA (Ed25519), its claims `iss` `"https://account.kiko.sh"`, `aud` `"relay.kiko.sh"`, `sub` (the account id), `plan` (`"trial"` or `"pro"`), `room` (the Mac's room id, as in its routes), `iat` and `exp` (**24 hours on** since 2026-11-04, 7 days before; never past the plan's own end, such as the trial's). The phone never holds one: the Mac shows it to its room.
- **The Mac shows it** in `auth` (`"pass"`), and again on the open socket whenever it renews one, `{"type":"pass","pass":"<pass>"}` (with under 12 hours left, or as soon as the plan changes), which the relay answers `{"type":"pass","pass":"<status>","passExpiresAt":<ms>,"passRequired":<bool>}`. A good renewal replaces the pass the room holds; a bad one leaves a good one held in place. A Mac from before this shows none.
- **The relay checks it** with no call to the account server per pass: the signature against our JWKS (`https://account.kiko.sh/v1/jwks`, else `https://api.kiko.sh/v1/jwks`; cached an hour, fetched again for a `kid` it hasn't seen, at most every 10 seconds; **the last good one kept in the relay's own storage, with no expiry**), then `iss`, `aud`, `plan`, `room`, `iat` and `exp` (a pass living longer than 26 hours from its `iat`, such as a 7-day one from before 2026-11-04, is `invalid`: the Mac asks for a new one). The status in `ready` and `pass`: `valid`; `missing` (none shown); `expired`; `invalid` (a bad signature, a `kid` our JWKS doesn't have, another issuer or audience, **another room**, an access token, anything malformed); or `unverified`, a pass whose claims are good that can't be checked: no JWKS has ever been fetched (nothing kept), or the account server is away and its `kid` isn't among the keys kept. `unverified` is **refused** like `invalid` (never taken on trust, so nobody can forge one in an outage). An outage still never locks a phone out: the relay checks passes against the keys it kept. `passExpiresAt` (ms) comes with `valid` only. A pass is held per Mac socket: a Mac that signs in again shows it again.
- **The room:** a pass for another room is `invalid`, so a copied pass opens no other room, and a pass without `room` is `invalid` too, with `requirePass` on or off (since 2026-11-19). The Mac asks for a new pass when its room changes (a new relay identity).
- **The revocation list.** Access can end before a pass runs out: a ban, the account deleted, a Mac signed out (by itself or from the account page), signing out everywhere, or the plan ending (the trial over by time or its cap, Pro ended after a failed payment's grace, cancelled Pro at its end, the plan taken away). Each writes a short entry to a list the relay reads: `sub:<account id>` for the whole account, `room:<account id>:<room id>` for one Mac signed out, the value `{"at":<Unix s>,"until":<Unix s>}`. **A pass whose `iat` is before an entry's `at` is `revoked`**; one issued after (signed in again, subscribed again) works at once. With `requirePass` on, the relay reads both keys as the Mac signs in, when a phone joins a room whose Mac's pass is otherwise good, and about once a minute while phones are in the room (one bulk read; kept in the room's memory about a minute), so a revocation reaches the relay within about two minutes (that, plus the list's own spread across Cloudflare's locations), and **the phones already in go** (`plan_required`, the Mac told `phone_refused` with `revoked`). An entry expires on its own at `until`, 26 hours on, when every pass it could refuse has run out. **The list not answering fails closed:** a read of it from the last 10 minutes still stands, else the pass is `unverified` and refused (a sign-in or a join; phones already in are never cut for it). It's a Cloudflare KV namespace the account server writes and the relay only reads (`relay/README.md`, "The pass").
- **At sign-in and join.** With `requirePass` on, a Mac signs in only with a good pass (above). A phone (a paired one, or one in on the pairing QR code's way) joins a room whose Mac's pass is good now; otherwise it hears `plan_required` and the socket closes with 4014, and the Mac is told `{"type":"phone_refused","code":"plan_required","pass":"<missing|expired|invalid|unverified|revoked>"}` (with `"pairing":true` for the QR code's way in, which a refusal doesn't spend). A phone already in a room stays when the pass runs out (the Mac renews it; an outage shouldn't end a session), but goes when it's revoked (above). `join` is checked after the credential (a stranger hears `unauthorized`, as ever) and after the Mac is found; no Mac in, but one refused for its plan in the last day: `plan_required`, else `mac_offline`.
- **The switch:** `requirePass`, in the relay's own settings (`relay/README.md`, "The pass"), **on by default** since 2026-11-19 (settings missing or malformed: on), off only when set so (development, tests). Off, every room works without a pass: a Mac signs in and phones join as ever, and one let in without a good pass is only logged; `ready` still says what came of a pass shown, and `passRequired: false`.
- **The phone,** on `plan_required`: don't retry on a loop (it won't change until the Mac's plan does); show the same notice as the hub's `plan_required` (The iPhone app and the plan, above: "The iPhone app comes with Kiko Pro. On your Mac, run kiko upgrade (or start the free trial with kiko setup)."), and try again when the app comes to the front, at home, or after a few minutes. **The Mac,** on `phone_refused`: say once a day that the phone tried and the plan is needed (`kiko upgrade`, or the trial if it's available), and ask for a pass (`POST /v1/relay/pass`) when the plan changes. It holds the pass in memory (never the Keychain), for its room, asks for a new one once under 12 hours are left (looked at hourly), as soon as its room changes, and as soon as it hears the plan changed (a check, a trial started, Pro bought or ended); on `phone_refused` with `revoked` it asks for a new one too (once a minute at most: one issued now works if the account has the plan again); on the account server's `403 plan_required` it shows none and logs it once. Its own sign-in refused (`plan_required` before `ready`): it says nothing (no phone tried), asks for a new pass if it was `revoked`, and dials again only when a new pass comes (or after 10 minutes; an hour with none at all; `unverified`, the relay's side: as any failure). Its first dial waits up to 5 seconds for the pass, so the upgrade carries it. It acts on a `phone_refused` only on a signed-in socket, once a minute per status, and takes at most 11 channels at once (10 phones and a pairing one), opened no faster than phones come: the relay can't make it do more.
- **The account server away:** the Mac can't renew, so a phone away from home keeps working until the pass the relay holds runs out, **up to a day**. At home the Mac's own rule holds, unchanged: the plan as last checked, for 7 days after that check (The iPhone app and the plan, above).
- **What the relay learns:** the account id behind a room (`sub`) and its plan, as it checks the pass; it keeps only the status, `exp`, `sub` and `iat`, in the Mac socket's own state, never in storage (only the JWKS's public keys are kept), and reads the revocation list's entries for that account (an account id, a room id, two times), never writing them. The account server learns the Mac's room id. A pass is tied to its room: someone who copied one can't open another room with it (room-less passes are refused); a session token's holder could still ask for a pass for any room (the account server doesn't yet ask for proof of the room's key), so the relay caps the rooms one account opens in a day. The hub's own `checkTicket` (`src/remote/server.ts`, `relay_pair`'s `ticket`) is the placeholder from before the pass, unused: no phone sends a ticket.

**The vectors** (`relay-vectors.json`, made by `scripts/relay-vectors.ts`, which also writes their room, keys and credential into `examples/relay_paired.json` and `welcome.relay.json`, checked by `tests/unit/remote/tunnel.test.ts`; every private key is SHA-256 of `kiko vector <label>`): `keys` (every private and public key, and the room), `local.qr` (with `relayCredential`, its hash, the phone's `relayJoin` and the Mac's `relayPairing`, for Pairing through the relay), `macSignIn` (the challenge, the bytes signed, the signature, the `auth` message), `phoneJoin` (the credential, its hash, `join` and the Mac's `phones`), `handshake` (the prologue, message 1 with its version byte, message 2, the handshake hash and both keys), `records` (each side's first records, in order: the text of a `hello` and a `welcome`, a binary Opus frame, a ping, its pong, a close; each with its counter, nonce, plaintext and frame), and `macFraming` (a frame behind its channel). Ed25519 signatures are deterministic, so `signature` is exact.
