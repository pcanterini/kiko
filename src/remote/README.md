# Remote

The Mac-side server for the phone. The iOS app connects to it (the local address at home, found with Bonjour or typed in, to pair; after that, whichever of the addresses `welcome` lists answers first: home, Bonjour, Tailscale's, or, with the relay on, the relay), streams your voice in, and gets back the session list, what Kiko is doing, a session's conversation, permission prompts and, if you ask, the spoken output. Every connection is encrypted end to end, at home as through the relay (`local.ts`, below; protocol/README.md, "Encrypted at home").

It speaks only the messages in `protocol/`, and it calls into `src/core/` like the Mac's own push-to-talk does: the phone is one more mic, speaker and screen, and the rules stay on the hub. Nothing here imports the iPhone app's code (it lives in its own repo).

- `server.ts`: the WebSocket server (the `ws` package), off unless Settings → Phone is on (`KIKO_PHONE=true`). Port 7333 on every interface (`KIKO_PHONE_PORT`, `KIKO_PHONE_HOST`). It only translates. `PhoneServer` itself is the connections (sign-in, presence, the floor, audio) and their messages; what the phones are told, what they missed and its settling, and hands-free from the phone are pieces of their own beside it (`tell.ts`, `missed.ts` `PhonesMissed`, `phone-hands-free.ts`, below), each handed what it uses of the server (its connections, `send`, the hub):
  - out: what the TUI draws, from the UI store (`core/ui-store.ts`), told by `tell.ts` (`Teller`): sessions and their status, the state line, the permission prompt (with its id), each line spoken; a watched session's history from its agent (`agent.watchHistory`: core's watcher, `core/history.ts`, on Claude Code's transcript), pushed entry by entry as it's written.
  - the home screen's cards: each listed session carries its agent's `mark` and light-background colour (`colorLight`, from `Agent.mark`), `inApp` (in Kiko's tmux: the window list's INTERNAL, `ui/nav.ts` listed) and, while it works, `doing`: `tell.ts` follows each working session's history through its agent (`followActivity`, eight at most) only while a phone is in front, and lets go once it stops working or no phone looks.
  - a session's screen (the chat): each listed session says who answers in it and its agent's colour (`speaker`, `color`, as `ui/marks.ts` speakerOf has them) and what can be done to it (`can`: `core/can.ts` canDo); its history carries what it's doing now (`doing`, from `core/history.ts`: the newest item's step still running, worded there, `doingOf`) and each edit's counts and diff; `history_before` pages back through its agent's own file (`agent.historyFile`, `historyBefore`: an empty page for one with none, T3 Code); `session_action` (Interrupt, Rename, Close) goes to `PhoneHub.sessionAction` only for what canDo allows, else a `session_note` says why, and the app does it as the window's menus do (`stopSession` / `renameSession` with `by` "phone", the window's `close`), telling that phone what came of it (`sessionNote`); `hear` goes to `PhoneHub.hear`, which reads it through Catch me up's lines (`chat.ts` spokenMessage: Markdown's marks out, code and tables said to be there); a prompt that can be allowed for good says `canAlways`, and `permission` `always` settles it as the Mac's `a`. `welcome` says `chat`, so a newer phone sends none of it to an older hub.
  - paging the session's screen (`history-window.ts`, `welcome` `paging`): a phone that watches with `paged` gets a window of the newest turns within its budget (`windowOf`: 30 turns or 64 KB, whole turns where it can), each item with its id (`core/history.ts`: where its entry starts in the file) and long ones cut to a preview (`preview`, `cut`), then only what changed (`deltaOf` → `history_delta`); `history_before` with a `cursor` reads back from that item (`itemsBefore`, else the agent's last history for one with no file, else by time) and says `next`; `show_full` reads the item again with more kept (`itemAt`, `FULL`) and answers `full_text`. An older phone gets the whole history as before, without ids, capped (`legacy`, LEGACY_BYTES).
  - in: `PhoneHub`, which `app.ts` implements. The talk button holds the floor like the push-to-talk key (`floor.keyDown` / `keyUp`), its audio goes to the same speech-to-text, and its words, spoken or typed, go through `act()` and `decide()` like any others (a session screen's words carry `to`, as if you'd named it). A permission tap is `settlePermission`, like the Mac's own allow and deny keys (for a prompt still in line too: `queued`, answered without cutting what's being said); the answers to Claude's questions (`permission_prompt` `questions`, the phone's `answers`) are `PhoneHub.answers`, checked against the questions (`core/ask.ts` checkAnswers) and settled like the answers by voice.
  - audio: `audio()` gets the speech the Mac's player gets (Cartesia, Pocket: 24 kHz PCM) as it's made, `cue()` a press's cue for the phone it was pressed on only (`cue: true`: it plays there whatever Play on phone says; the Mac's own presses' cues never come here), `speak()` the macOS voice's text (it plays by itself and can't be streamed), respelled for that voice like everything said (`core/pronunciation.ts`), `stopAudio()` every barge-in. The read-along (protocol/README.md, "The read-along"): each chunk comes with its place in its line (`core/voice-marks.ts`), and a phone is told where the voice is (`voice_marks` `sync`, just before the audio, in order with it: at a line's start and whenever what follows doesn't carry on, the Opus encoder saying where in a frame a line starts and when a padded packet moved it); `voiceMarks()` sends when each word is said, and `speak()` the macOS voice's line with each word's place in its text. Only to phones with Play on phone on. Whether the Mac plays it too is its own setting, Play on this Mac, switched only from the phone (`core/mac-output.ts`): off, a clock stands in for the Mac's player and `say`, so the phones get the same audio at the same pace and the floor's timing is unchanged; a phone switches it with `play_on_mac` (`PhoneHub.playOnMac`). `audible()` says whether any phone plays it (one in the background or locked doesn't, nor a connection that hasn't said where its app is yet: the app says right after `welcome`, and one that doesn't within 2 s is an older app, taken to be in front): when none does, the Mac plays it even with Play on this Mac off (`mac_stands_in`, `macPlays` in `app.ts`), so what's said is never played to no one. `nothing_plays` is no longer sent.
  - missed: each line Kiko says (`spoken` about a session: an announcement, or a permission question while that session's prompt is being asked; `voice`: its own answer) is kept (`missed.ts`: `PhonesMissed` keeps it in its `MissedLog`) for every paired phone that isn't there, not connected or away (`presence`). `missed` goes right after `welcome` and on `presence` `back`; `seen` marks them read; `catch_up` hands the lines (the session's name first where they don't say it) to `PhoneHub.catchUp`, which `app.ts` reads through `openSpeech()`, each waiting its turn behind what's being said, so a press stops them like any speech; with `summary` (Catch me up), the ones not settled go to `PhoneHub.catchUpSummary` for one short summary; `catch_up_stop` is `PhoneHub.catchUpStop`. A line kept for a phone that's still connected but away also goes to that connection at once (`missed_line`, the item as `missed` sends it; not for a repeat it already has), so the app, while iOS lets it run, posts a notification (the iPhone app's `README.md`, Notifications).
  - settled: what no longer needs the phone is marked on the item (`settled`) and sent to connected phones (`missed_settled`). The app tells the server as it happens, which hands it to `PhonesMissed`: `promptSettled` from `settlePermission` (the Mac's keys or voice: `answered_on_mac`; a press or tap on a phone: `answered_on_phone`), `sessionReplied` from `landed()` and from words typed into a held session. `PhonesMissed` sees the rest itself: a newer update from the same session (the older one `superseded`, its question `closed`: missed.ts), a session that stops being listed as blocked after the hub let its question go (answered in its terminal: `answered_on_mac`), and, as a phone comes back or catches up, a session asked something since its update (`agent.lastAsked` changed: typed in by hand, `replied`) and a question let go of over 15 s ago whose session isn't blocked (`closed`). And what you took in at the Mac (`seen-on-mac.ts`, `seen_on_mac`): a line the Mac said out loud (`macHears`: `macPlays` and not muted) while the presence reading (the one `missed_line`'s `quiet` uses) had input in the last 90 s and the screen unlocked and awake; or a line shown with the screen unlocked and awake, once Kiko is used on the Mac within 2 minutes (`macUsed`: the talk key from `talk.ts` `begin`, any window action from `onAction`, `typed_in` from `watchTyping`; and, only with input in the last 15 s, a prompt a session took in, from `onPromptSubmitted`, or a session unblocked in its terminal). Logged as `phone_seen_on_mac` (`ids`, `why`: `heard` or the use).
  - Opus (`opus.ts`): the phone's audio is PCM in JSON as always, unless the app takes Opus: `welcome` lists `codecs` (`["opus"]`, when the server has Opus: `PhoneServerOptions.opus`, the platform's unless a test swaps it), the app answers `audio_codecs` (empty with its Compressed audio off), and the server picks (`pickCodec`: Opus only when the app lists it and the helper builds) and says `audio_codec`. On Opus a press comes in binary frames: a `TalkDecoder` per press decodes them to 16 kHz PCM in order for `talkAudio`, and `talk_end` waits until all of it has been handed on (what the connection sends meanwhile waits, in order, behind it). The voice goes out through one `VoiceEncoder` for every phone on Opus: the first packet of a stretch at once, then five 20 ms packets to a frame, the tail padded once the line is over; `stopAudio()` cuts it, so nothing of a cut line follows `audio_stop`. Cues stay PCM in JSON. A phone on PCM gets exactly what it got before. The codec is the platform's `opus` piece: `src/platform/macos/opus.ts`, one Swift helper (`native/macos/opus.swift`, Apple's Opus in AudioToolbox, built like the other helpers) for every stream. The protocol side, and why `hello` doesn't carry it: `protocol/README.md`, Opus.
  - new session: a phone whose `welcome` says `newSession` (the app implements `PhoneHub.newSessionOptions` and `newSession`) can start one from its New session sheet, as the Mac window's picker does (`new-session.ts`, below). `get_new_session_options` → `new_session_options`; `new_session` → `new_session_status` (`heard`, `started`, `failed`), to each of that phone's connections; a `talk_start` with `dictate` (it needs a `talkId`, and is refused as `invalid` without one or by a hub that doesn't start sessions) goes to `PhoneHub.talkStart` with `dictate` and no session; and a session listed with a `starting-…` alias carries it as `startedAs`; `new_session` `name` goes to the app with the rest (`canName` per harness in the options: `core/can.ts` namedAtStart); a `watch` of a session still starting gets an empty history, then its agent's once it's listed (`followStarted`, under the id watched).
  - hands-free from the phone (`phone-hands-free.ts`): a phone whose `welcome` says `handsFree` (the app implements `PhoneHub.listen` and `listenAudio`) sends `hands_free { on }` while its Hands-free setting is on and its app is in front; `PhoneHub.listen` starts its listener (`core/phone-listen.ts` PhoneListeners, wired in `app/talk.ts` phoneListeners: the Mac's own HandsFree on that phone's audio, with `sparse` set so the deaf window and the reply window's quiet room are watched between its chunks), which tells the phone how it is through `handsFreeState` (`hands_free_state`). Its audio (`listen_audio`, or binary frames of kind 3 decoded by a `TalkDecoder` of the connection's own) goes to `listenAudio` only while it's on; `presence` `away` and the connection closing stop it. What it hears holds the floor as hands-free does (`begin` with the listener; every other listener drops what it heard, and while the floor is someone else's each is deaf), with the phone's press's `pressAt` (cues and replies there), and its words go through `act()` inside a talk of their own (`hands_free_state` `talkId`). `voiceFrom` says which phone's listener holds the floor, so its live words go to the phones as a press's do. A phone whose `welcome` says `handsFreePlaying` (the app implements `PhoneHub.listenPlaying`) also sends `hands_free_playing { playing }` as it starts playing Kiko's voice and once its mic is back after it; `listenPlaying` hands it to that phone's listener (`PhoneListeners.playing`, `HandsFree.phonePlaying`), whose deaf window and reply-window onset follow the phone's own playback instead of the Mac's send. The protocol side: `protocol/README.md`, Hands-free from the phone.
  - the relay: with `KIKO_RELAY_URL` set (no default yet: `wss://relay.kiko.sh` is deployed, and becomes the default with phase 2; no setting of its own: it's on with Phone), `app/phone.ts` loads the Mac's relay keys (`relay-identity.ts`) and starts the relay client (`relay-client.ts`) beside the server, and hands the server what phones are told of it (`RelaySide`: the url, the room, the Mac's two public keys). `welcome` says `relay`; `relay_pair` (on a direct connection, normally at home right after pairing; refused through the relay) keeps the phone's static key and a new credential's hash with it in `phones.json` (`Pairing.setRelay`) and answers `relay_paired`; from then on that phone's `addresses` end with the relay's entry. Each tunnel the client opens is handed in as a socket of its own (`adopt`, a `PhoneSocket` like the `ws` package's) and is then a connection like any other, logged with `via: "relay"`; `pair` and `relay_pair` are refused on it (but on a QR code's pairing channel: Pairing through the relay), and `hello` must carry the token of the phone whose key did the handshake. `checkTicket` is the account check's hook at `relay_pair` (phase 2; unset).
- `relay-client.ts`: the Mac's end of the relay (`relay/`). Connects out to its room (`<url>/v1/mac/<room>`), signs the room's challenge with the Mac's Ed25519 key, registers the phones' credential hashes (`Pairing.relayCredentials`; again on every `relay_pair` or forget, through `Pairing.onRelayChange`), and runs a `Channel` per phone that joins: its first frame is the tunnel's handshake (`tunnel.ts` respond), whose static key must be a paired phone's (`Pairing.byRelayKey`), else the channel is let go; then a `TunnelSocket` (records in and out: text, binary, ping, pong, close) goes to the server. Pings the relay every 10 s and drops a socket whose pong hasn't come within 5 s; as the Mac's network changes or the Mac wakes (`network-watch.ts`: the interfaces' addresses read every 2 s, a change said once it has settled for 2.5 s and once more 5 s later) it pings at once and gives the pong 3 s (`check`). A socket dropped for no pong is dialled again at once; any other end, or a dial that fails (one not signed in within 15 s included), with a growing wait (1 s doubling to a minute, give or take a fifth), which a network change cuts short. Its phones are let go meanwhile. Pairing by the QR code from away: with a relay whose `ready` says `pairing`, it registers the shown code's way in (`Pairing.qrRelay`, the hash of `cpace.ts` `qrRelayCredentialOf`; again on every change, through `Pairing.onQrChange`, and when a pairing channel ends without the code used) and drops it as the code stops (`used` when it paired); a channel the relay marks `pairing` takes only the qr opening (CPace with the secret, as `local.ts`), refuses a used or expired code with the close's `code`, and hands the server a socket with the qr way (`adopt(socket, null, way)`), where `pair`, then `relay_pair`, are taken.
- `tunnel.ts`: the tunnel's crypto, node:crypto only: Noise IK (`Noise_IK_25519_ChaChaPoly_SHA256`; `initiate` for the phone's side, which only tests use here, `respond` for the Mac's), the records (a type byte, then the body, sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 under a counter nonce: a record that doesn't open under the next counter ends the tunnel), X25519 and Ed25519 from raw keys, the room id, what the Mac signs, a credential's hash. The spec, byte for byte: `protocol/README.md`, Relay; the vectors: `protocol/relay-vectors.json` (`scripts/relay-vectors.ts` makes them).
- `local.ts`: every direct connection's tunnel (protocol/README.md, "Encrypted at home"). `LocalLink` sits between a `ws` socket and the server: it reads the phone's first frame (keys: Noise IK with the prologue `kiko local v1`; code: CPace with the pairing code; token: CPace with a paired phone's token hash, once, for a phone paired before this; qr: CPace with the QR code's secret, named by its id, `qr_used` or `qr_expired` before any CPace when it doesn't work any more), answers, waits for the phone's first record to open (CPace's confirmation: a wrong code is counted by `Pairing.wrongCode`), then hands the server a `TunnelSocket` with how it was opened (`LocalWay`), which `server.ts` uses to decide who may sign in (`pair` only on a code or QR handshake, with that code or that QR code's secret; on keys only that key's phone, or a keyless phone whose key it becomes; on a token handshake only that phone, a keyless one, whose hello brings its key). Refusals before the tunnel go in the clear (`version` to an app from before, `bad_code`, `pairing_closed`, `too_many_attempts`, `qr_used`, `qr_expired`, `stale_key`, `unknown_phone`); a text frame or a record that doesn't open once it's open ends it. Logged: `phone_refused` (`reason`: `unencrypted`, `stale_key`, `unknown_token`, `token_after_key`, `token_mismatch`, `bad_code`, `qr_used`, `qr_expired`, `qr_mismatch`, `handshake` with its `step`, `handshake_timeout`…, `tunnel`), `phone_tunnel_broken` (`why`), `phone_key` (a phone's key kept, `from` keys or token); `phone_connected` has `tunnel`.
- `cpace.ts`: CPace (`draft-irtf-cfrg-cpace` revision 21, CPACE-X25519-SHA512: Elligator 2 with BigInt, X25519 and SHA-512 from node:crypto), checked against the draft's own vector; the record keys from ISK (`sessionOf`); the token's secret and id (`tokenSecret`, `tokenIdOf`); `cpaceSelfCheck`, which `app/phone.ts` runs with `tunnel.ts selfCheck` before Phone turns on.
- `tunnel-socket.ts`: `TunnelSocket`, a tunnel as the server sees a connection (as the `ws` package's socket), over a `TunnelLink`: a relay channel or a `LocalLink`.
- `relay-identity.ts`: the Mac's keys for the phone (a static X25519 key, which every phone's tunnel is to, at home and through the relay; an Ed25519 signing key, which names its relay room), made the first time Phone is on and kept in the Keychain (`KIKO_RELAY_DEVICE_KEY`, this flavour's service, through the keychain helper); with the Keychain off (`KIKO_KEYCHAIN=off`), in `device-key.json` next to `phones.json` (0600, `fileKeyStore`). Phone stays off, said on screen, when they can't be read (`phone_off` `why: keychain`) or this runtime's crypto fails its self-check (`why: crypto`). `kiko uninstall` takes the Keychain item out with the API keys.
- `new-session.ts`: a new session from the phone. `askOf` reads `new_session` (exactly one place: a project's folder, a new project's name, a scratch folder, as the window's `StartPick`; the first message typed or said, not both) and says why not in words for the phone; `Dictations` keeps a dictated press's words, by phone and `talkId`, from its `talk_start` until its `new_session` takes them (which may come before the press ends: the phone sends it as the button goes up, ahead of `talk_end`; a cancel ends it with nothing; one nobody asks for goes after 5 minutes; one asked for waits a minute at most). In `app.ts`: `phoneSessionOptions` lists what the window's picker and its agent question offer (`pickerChoices`, `offeredStarters`, `preselect`; apps open with their own projects as `folders`: `elsewhere`), and `phoneNewSession` opens it with the window's code (`placeOf`, then `startSession` or `startInApp`, `by: "phone"`: shown, not said, no "Voice can"), which return the session's starting-… id (or the T3 thread's) or why not; a dictated press's words (the dictionary applied: `fixHeard`) are written down in `phoneTalkEnd` and never go through `act()`.
- `talk-status.ts`: what became of each press or typed message from a phone that gave a `talkId` (`talk_status`): heard, routed, held, delivered (`queued` when the session was busy), started, replied, answered, failed. `app.ts` begins a `Talk` per press or message (`PhoneHub.talkStart` and `type` get the id) and runs its words' handling inside it (`Talks.run`, an `AsyncLocalStorage`), so the places that route, hold, type, refuse or fail tell the talk they belong to without being passed it: `holdIf` (routed to a session), `decideAndAct` (nothing to send: routed to Kiko), `startHold` and `replyLater` (held; the talks go along with the words and are run again where they're given), `replyThrough` (delivered and started: a reply through the hook), `typeInto` (delivered, or failed: the session closed, nothing to type into, typing refused), `notSent` and `act`'s catch (failed). When the handling is over, `finish` leaves none hanging: routed to Kiko is answered, anything else not in or held is failed. Started: Claude Code's `UserPromptSubmit` hook with the same words (`agent.onPromptSubmitted`), or a session's next finished turn listing them among what reached it (`turnEnded`, `Held.saidThisTurn`); typed words waiting for that are forgotten after 30 minutes (still delivered). Replied: the next Stop hook of the session a talk started in (`onRequest` calls `turnEnded`, which also replies to words it just started); one waiting longer than an hour is forgotten (still started), and the phone stops waiting by itself. The server sends each status to that phone's connections only, keeps the latest per talk (20, 10 minutes) for when it reconnects, and fails at once a press or typed message it turns down (`busy`, `no_session`).
- `opus.ts`: which codec (`pickCodec`), the binary frames (`frame`, `parseFrame`, packets behind 2-byte lengths), and the voice's encoder and each press's decoder (above).
- `catch-up.ts`: Catch me up, said: one summary (one Haiku call, `Summarizer.catchUp`, its own prompt: grouped by session, two to four sentences, 200 tokens at most, streamed into one speech), one line read as it is, the lines one by one if the call fails, or each line in order (Read all). On (`catchingUp` in the state) until it's been heard; Stop drops what's to come and, once it's being heard, cuts it like a press (`floor.deferUnheard`, `stopTalking`); a press ends it too.
- `missed.ts`: what each phone missed (`MissedLog`), the newest 50 per phone (read ones go first), with ids that go up with time, and how many it has read. Each phone's item is its own copy, settled (and why) on its own. Kept in `missed.json` next to `phones.json`, so it survives the phone reconnecting, Phone going off and on, and Kiko restarting; a forgotten phone's record goes. And the server's side of it (`PhonesMissed`, above: missed, settled): which spoken lines are kept, and for whom (`record`), `missed_line` to an away phone (`tellAway`), `missed` and `missed_settled` as a phone signs in or comes back (`sendMissed`), `catch_up`, and settling, from the app (`promptSettled`, `sessionReplied`, `macUsed`), from each change in the store (`watchSettled`) and before a phone hears what it missed (`checkSettled`).
- `tell.ts`: what the phones are told (`Teller`, above: out, the home screen's cards): `sessions`, `state`, `permission_prompt` (`quiet` to an away phone while you're at the Mac; `queued`, every other prompt Kiko holds, each answerable by its id, and each prompt's `detail`), `announcement` and `heard`, sent together a moment after the store changes (`schedule`, `flush`) and to each connection only what changed; each change is first handed back to the server (`before`: settling, `followStarted`).
- `phone-hands-free.ts`: hands-free from the phone (`PhoneHandsFree`, above): `hands_free`, `listen_audio` and its binary frames, `hands_free_playing`, what `welcome` says of it, `hands_free_state` (`PhoneServer.handsFreeState` hands it on), and the listener let go of as a connection goes away or closes.
- `addresses.ts`: every way the phone can reach this Mac, sent in `paired` and `welcome` (`addresses`): the home network's addresses (Wi-Fi and Ethernet first, at most six), the Bonjour name, then Tailscale's address and MagicDNS name. The server looks them up (the platform's `network.addresses()`) before it listens and again as each phone connects, one lookup at a time; a `welcome` carries the latest list.
- `pair-link.ts`: what the pairing QR code says (protocol/README.md, "Pairing with a QR code"): `pairUrl` (one URL in the iPhone app's scheme, `IDENTIFIER.ios`: the Mac's name, `hostsFor` its home and Tailscale addresses, the port, the one-time secret, its static key, the relay when it's on) and `parsePairUrl` (the phone's reading of it, for the tests and the vectors). `app/phone.ts` `pairPhone` shows it (Settings → Phone → Pair a phone), drawn by `src/core/qr.ts` and `src/ui/pair-phone.ts`; the URL lives only in memory and on screen.
- `pairing.ts`: the 6-digit code (10 minutes, five wrong ones close it), the QR code's secret (`openQr`: 16 random bytes, 5 minutes, one phone, closed with its screen, `closeQr`; `qrFor` names it by its id, `pairQr` pairs; used ones refused `qr_used`, expired or closed `qr_expired`; nothing counted, nothing written), the phone's token (32 random bytes), and `phones.json` next to the settings holding only each token's SHA-256, each phone's static X25519 public key (`relayKey`: from `pair`, a token handshake's `hello`, or `relay_pair`) and, for a phone set up for the relay, its relay credential's SHA-256. The code is never sent: the phone proves it with CPace (`codeFor`, `wrongCode`); `byTokenId` finds the phone a token handshake names. Forgetting a phone lets it go at once, on the relay too.
- `account-status.ts`: the plan on the phone (`account`, protocol/README.md "The plan on the phone"): `accountStatus` turns the account as last known (`core/account/account.ts` `Account.view()`, account.json) and whether your own OpenRouter key is in use into the message, deciding as `core/account/plan-words.ts` `planLine` does (a running trial, Pro, a trial that ended and why, none, signed out); no email, no token. `app/phone.ts` hands the server a reader for it (`PhoneServerOptions.account`, null while accounts aren't open: nothing is sent then). The server sends it right after `welcome`, then, looked at each minute (`ACCOUNT_CHECK_MS`) or when the app calls `accountChanged`, to every phone only when it changed. While Kiko Pro is on sale, `phoneApp` says whether the plan includes the iPhone app.
- The plan at connect (protocol/README.md "The iPhone app and the plan"): `server.ts` asks `phonePlan` (the app's `app/phone-plan.ts`, from `core/account/phone-plan.ts`) at each `hello` and `pair`; without the plan, `plan_required` with its `reason` (`phoneReason`: `mac_signed_out`, `no_plan`, `trial_ended`, `pro_ended`, `payment_failed_grace_over`) and a 4014 close, never mid-session, the pairing kept; `planRefused` lets the app say it once a day, by reason. Signed out, a paired phone (hello, never pair) is offered to sign the Mac in (`signIn`): it says `sign_in` within 3 s and is held (`Conn.signIn`, 30 minutes at most) while the Mac's one shared sign-in (`core/account/mac-sign-in.ts` `MacSignIn`, the app's in `app/mac-sign-in.ts`, which Enter on the Mac's popup shares) sends it `mac_sign_in` (the code, a new one when it runs out, `failed`), then `signed_in` and a 4015 close; nothing else ever reaches a refused phone, and any other message from it is let be (never `unauthorized`, which would unpair it). `accountChanged` settles a sign-in made some other way (Settings, `kiko login`). `relay-client.ts` `setPass` shows the relay pass (in `auth`, `{"type":"pass"}` on renewal) and hears the relay's `phone_refused`.
- `schema.ts`: checks messages against `protocol/schema.json`, with no dependency.
- `chat.ts`: a message's words as Hear it again says them (`spokenMessage`).

Bonjour and the addresses shown in the settings screen come from the platform's `network` piece (`src/platform/macos/network.ts`: interfaces, `tailscale status --json` for Tailscale's address and MagicDNS name (2 s at most; nothing of Tailscale's when it isn't running), `dns-sd -R`).
